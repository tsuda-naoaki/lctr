Evidence Effect

Evidence-Effect Scope

The formal evidence in this reference supports 18 results concerning the Hamiltonian form of general relativity, 3+1 decomposition, constraints, canonical reduction, asymptotic quantities, gravitational radiation, Einstein–Maxwell–particle coupling, proper time, the preferred time coordinate, and canonical choice in quantization.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

Establishment of parametrization, deparametrization, an intrinsic time coordinate, 3+1 decomposition, normal proper time, and Lorentz proper time of the total system does not imply that Hamiltonian time, boundary times, an evolution parameter, a 3+1 temporal reference, worldline time, and the preferred time coordinate have been generated from non-temporal physical inputs. Nor does posing the question of preferred time in quantization retroactively confer physical-time qualification on the 17 time contents. These transitions lie beyond the evidence-effect boundary in Definition 7.115.

Result-Preservation Domain

Result-Preservation Domain

The 18 results are preserved insofar as every time-content premise is supplied by the corresponding generated time representation in the LCTR generation sequence, while preserving the gravitational field, matter fields, electromagnetic field, particles, spatial metric, canonical variables, constraint relations, asymptotic-flatness conditions, wave-zone conditions, and the original source’s directions of dependence. The asymptotic orders of Result 11 and the rest-state and point-limit conditions of Results 13 and 16 are also retained. For Results 17–18, what is preserved is the open question concerning preferred time coordinates and quantum unitary equivalence.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The 17 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already-established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment follows by matching the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation against the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or validity condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

For each reference, the reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from that failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the objects of repair are the corresponding time contents and the generation or validity conditions required before their use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual failure is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that failure location. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual Statements

Objects of the Statements

The preferred time coordinate tt already selected in the comparison with particle mechanics, and NN and qM+1q_{M+1}, which are left arbitrary by the dynamics in the parametrized system.

Verbatim Original (English)

preferred time coordinate tt has been decided upon

Thus NN and hence qM+1q_{M+1}, are left arbitrary by the dynamics

The first quotation contains the time-coordinate symbol tt; the second contains NN and qM+1q_{M+1}.

Original-Source Locations

The first quotation is from Arnowitt, Deser, and Misner, The Dynamics of General Relativity, arXiv:gr-qc/0405109v1, p. 28, §8.1, footnote 25. The second quotation is from p. 5, §2.3, in the same reference.

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

Section 2.3 states the freedom to choose qM+1⁢(τ)q_{M+1}(\tau) and use it as the new independent variable. The change of independent variable is valid where it is differentiable and locally invertible. Choosing this function also fixes NN. Page 8, §3.3 gives a coordinate choice in which lines of constant spatial coordinates are normal to the hypersurface and x0x^{0} measures proper time along these lines. The first quotation, in footnote 25 on p. 28, §8.1, concerns the particle-mechanics example. The same footnote states that no such single preferred coordinate frame exists in the interior in gravitation, at least classically, and that it is not known whether quantum consistency requirements may force a preferred frame. The main text on that page states that classically equivalent choices of canonical variables need not remain unitarily equivalent in quantum theory.

Attribution of the Statements

Both statements are attributable to the authors’ reposted version of the 1962 chapter by Arnowitt, Deser, and Misner.

Predicates Assigned to the Objects by the Statements

The first quotation states that the time coordinate tt has already been selected as preferred in the particle-mechanics example. The second quotation assigns dynamical indeterminacy to NN and qM+1q_{M+1}.

Explicit Domains of Application of the Statements

The domain of application comprises parametrization and deparametrization in Hamiltonian form, 3+1 decomposition, canonical-variable choice, and canonical quantization.

Individual Audit

Interpretability

The first statement describes a preferred time coordinate already selected in the particle-mechanics example. The source contrasts this particle-mechanics example with the absence of a single preferred coordinate frame in the interior in classical gravitation. It states that whether consistency requirements in quantum theory force a preferred frame is unknown. The second statement describes the freedom to choose a function left undetermined by the dynamics. Using it as a new independent variable requires differentiability and local invertibility. The surrounding context also discusses why classically equivalent choices of canonical variables need not be unitarily equivalent starting points in quantum theory.

Independent Grounds

Both quotations directly state, in the original wording, the selection of a time coordinate and indeterminacy under the dynamics.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first statement supports prior selection of a preferred time coordinate in the particle-mechanics example, but does not support that the coordinate has been generated from non-temporal physical inputs or that it is qualified as physical time. The second statement supports that NN and qM+1q_{M+1} are left arbitrary by the dynamics, but does not provide grounds for identifying that arbitrary function with physical time. The quantum-theoretical non-uniqueness of canonical-variable choice belongs to the discussion in the main text of §8.1 and footnote 25.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The contrast and qualifications in footnote 25 and the main text of §8.1 correspond to Time Content 17 in Definition S.90, Claim Components 36–37 in Definition S.91, and Results 17–18 in Definition S.93.

The second statement and the action transformation in §2.3 correspond to Time Contents 3 and 5, Claim Components 11–12, and Results 2 and 3. The field extension at the end of that section and the reduction by constraint solutions and coordinate conditions in §4 correspond to Time Contents 4 and 8–9, Claim Components 22–24, and Result 8.

Within-Source Comparison

The first statement describes a time coordinate already selected in a particle-mechanics example; the second describes an arbitrary function left by parametrized dynamics. The former concerns a choice of variable in particle mechanics; the latter concerns the indeterminacy of a function in parametrized dynamics.
