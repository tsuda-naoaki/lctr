Formal Objects

The audit uses 22 time contents, 59 claims, 12 transfers and 21 results. The claim table and formal-input definition specify the cosmological term and vacuum contributions, Friedmann models, supersymmetry, constant-field adjustment mechanisms, modified gravity and quantum-cosmological constructions. Weinberg examines supersymmetry, anthropic considerations, adjustment mechanisms, modifications of gravity and quantum cosmology in response to the disparity between theoretical expectations for the cosmological constant and observational limits. Ages calculated from cosmic expansion use a formation redshift and a cosmological model; the stellar ages used for comparison come from independent observational evidence. His discussion of quantum cosmology constructs a matter-field local clock, an average over histories and measurement times, a global configuration time from spatial volume, and a conserved flux associated with that coordinate.

Assumptions

Each argument uses a chosen spacetime metric, field action, cosmological model, symmetry and symmetry-breaking regime, and observational inputs. The galaxy-formation and recollapse bounds have their respective formation and lifetime conditions. The argument about constant-field adjustment mechanisms assumes a constant metric and matter fields and local field-space coordinates. The quantum-cosmological constructions require specified boundary conditions, contours, measures, operator orderings and normalization. The corresponding tables state the conditions for each transfer and result.

Individual Audits

First Statement

Weinberg states an approximately 120-order disparity between vacuum contributions at the field-theoretic scales adopted in the source and observational limits as of 1989. The grounds are the vacuum estimates in Sections II–III and the bound obtained by comparing redshift, expansion-rate and density information with a cosmological model. The corresponding evidence is Time Contents 2–4 and 17, Claims 14–15 and Result 2.

Second Statement

Weinberg assesses all five approaches examined in Sections IV–VIII as candidates still worthy of consideration in 1989. This assessment is his summary in Section IX of their constructions, conditions and remaining problems. The corresponding evidence is Claims 21 and 58 and Results 4 and 18.

Third Statement

In his 1989 comparison of the five approaches, Weinberg assesses the fifth, based on quantum cosmology, as apparently the most promising. The constructions in Hawking’s and Coleman’s proposals in Section VIII, together with their remaining qualms, are the specific subjects of that comparison. The corresponding evidence is Claims 53–58 and Results 12,13 and 18; the ranking itself corresponds to Claim 58 and Result 18.

Common Audit with Specified Domain of Application

The first statement compares theoretical scales with observational limits; the second and third express Weinberg’s comparative assessment of five approaches. Their domains are determined by the models and observational information specified in the source and its assessment as of 1989.
