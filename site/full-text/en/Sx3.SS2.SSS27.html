Formal Objects

The audit uses 17 time contents, 40 claims, seven transfers and 14 results. The claim table and formal-input definition specify causal sets, continuum causal order, volume correspondence, faithful embedding, coarse graining and the conditional effective-action argument. Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin propose that spacetime at the smallest scales is a causal set. They identify a locally finite partial order with physical past–future relations and element counts with spacetime volume. Continuum approximation is discussed under faithful-embedding conditions; coarse graining restricts the causal order to a representative subset. The quantum-action argument uses causal-set amplitudes and a given Lorentzian geometry.

Assumptions

A mathematical causal set satisfies transitivity, noncircularity between distinct elements, the reflexive convention and local finiteness of intervals. Recovery of conformal geometry from continuum causal order uses suitable smoothness and causality conditions and dimension greater than two. Faithful embedding uses time orientation, absence of closed timelike or null curves, order agreement, uniform unit density and separation of geometric variation scales from point spacing. Result 14 specifies the quantum-action conditions.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Initial inputs for generation are Claims 1–3. Data for sets, cardinalities, binary relations, samples, density coefficients, maps and algebraic coefficients are separated from physical correspondences with past and future, events, spacetime volume and Lorentzian geometry.

Individual Statements

Object of the statements

The four authors’ proposal that smallest-scale spacetime is a causal set, and the correspondence of its partial order with the macroscopic past–future relation.

Verbatim originals

We propose that space-time at the smallest scales is in reality a causal set

a partial order corresponding to the macroscopic relation that defines past and future

Original-source location

Bombelli et al., Space-Time as a Causal Set, Physical Review Letters 59, p. 521, abstract.

Minimal context

Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin propose a locally finite partially ordered set as the structure of smallest-scale spacetime. The second quotation is the part of the same opening abstract sentence specifying the physical meaning of that order.

Attribution

Luca Bombelli, Joohan Lee, David Meyer and Rafael D. Sorkin.

Predicate

The four authors propose that smallest-scale spacetime is a causal set and identify its partial order with the macroscopic past–future relation.

Explicit domain of application

The causal-set proposal identifying smallest-scale spacetime with a locally finite causal set and exploring its continuum approximation by a Lorentzian manifold.

Individual Audit

First Statement

The four authors propose a locally finite causal set as the fundamental structure of smallest-scale spacetime. The statement concerns spacetime ontology, with its proposal status expressed by the governing verb. Its mathematical support consists of the causal-set definition and the three faithful-embedding conditions. Essential uniqueness of the continuum approximation is the authors’ conjecture; its precise formulation and proof remain open tasks. The corresponding evidence is Time Contents 4,5,10, Claims 4,10,13–20 and Results 1,2,6,8.

Second Statement

The four authors give causal-set order the meaning of macroscopic past–future relations. In a continuum approximation using a faithful embedding, this correspondence is expressed by agreement of discrete order and continuum causal order. Its formal support is the order-agreement condition and the conditional recovery of conformal geometry from an existing continuum causal order. In the LCTR audit, Claims 4 and 14 and Transfers 1–4 and 7 use temporal premises in their physical order interpretation. The corresponding evidence is Time Contents 1–4,6,7,9,10, Claims 4–6,11–14,18 and Results 2,3,6.

Common Audit with Scope

The two statements concern spacetime constitution and the physical meaning of order within the authors’ single causal-set proposal.
