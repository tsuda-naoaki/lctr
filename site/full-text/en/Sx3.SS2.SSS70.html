Audit Conclusions

Proposition S.688 (Primitive time roots and result dependence in the Feynman gravitation lectures).
𝖠𝗎𝖽𝗂𝗍𝖯𝖺𝗋𝗍𝖨𝖽𝗑R=𝖠𝗅𝗅𝖱𝗈𝗈𝗍𝖣𝖾𝗉𝖨𝖽𝗑⁢(11,7).\mathsf{AuditPartIdx}_{R}=\mathsf{AllRootDepIdx}(11\mathchar 44\relax% \allowbreak 7).
Proof.

The generation-initial set consists of Claims 1–3. All six transfers have temporal inputs; adding the source formal-evidence environment provides no transfer without temporal inputs. Complete generation and local reach under source-evidence conditions are therefore both zero, leaving eleven primitive time roots. All seven results have temporal premises; supplying the time contents and source formal-evidence environment under the respective application conditions satisfies each result edge. ∎

Individual Statements

Statement Evidence 1: Generalization to All Physical Processes

Original quotation

Our theory is then not inconsistent, and it suggests that the frequency shift required by energy conservation be considered as a general property of all physical processes, that they run more slowly in lower gravitational potentials.

Primary-source location

Lecture 5, §5.2, printed p. 69, PDF p. 110.

Minimal context

After deriving the process-rate relation from the matter-field action on pp. 67–68 and the nuclear-frequency shift from energy conservation on p. 69, the lecture states the process-rate relation common to both arguments.

Attribution

The text of Richard P. Feynman’s lecture.

Statement Evidence 2: Application to Human Living and Aging

Original quotation

The man on top of a mountain is living and aging at a faster rate than we.

Primary-source location

Lecture 5, §5.2, printed p. 69, PDF p. 110.

Minimal context

Immediately after the generalization to all physical processes, the predicate is transferred to human living and aging.

Attribution

The text of Richard P. Feynman’s lecture.

Statement Evidence 3: Attribute of Temporal Flow

Original quotation

One of the ways of describing this situation is to say that the time scale is faster at the top; time flows are different in different gravitational potentials, so that time flows are unequal in various parts of the world.

Primary-source location

Lecture 7, §7.2, printed p. 94, PDF p. 135.

Minimal context

Immediately after the comparison of frequencies between upper and lower clocks and light signals, this is presented as a way of describing the same situation.

Attribution

The text of Richard P. Feynman’s lecture.

Common Evidence with a Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Generalization to all physical processes

Interpretive possibility

This is a theoretical generalization comparing the rate of the same physical process at different locally constant potentials.

Independent grounds

The first-order derivation from the matter-field action, stress-energy coupling and transformation of the time variable on pp. 67–68. The nuclear-transition and energy-conservation frequency relation on p. 69 also supports the same rate comparison.

Boundary of evidential force

The domain uses an action depending on fields and first derivatives, a locally constant weak gravitational field and the first-order approximation.

Corresponding formal evidence

Time Contents 1–3 and 10, Transfers 1 and 2, and Results 1–3.

Application to human living and aging

Interpretive possibility

The statement applies the matter-field process-rate relation to human bodily processes.

Independent grounds

The preceding process-rate generalization and the gravitational-potential difference between the mountaintop and the lower elevation.

Boundary of evidential force

This is a theoretical application to bodily processes following the same matter-field description, with the comparison positions specified. Accumulated age differences depend on initial ages, the comparison interval and rate histories along the paths.

Corresponding formal evidence

Time Contents 3–6 and 11, Transfers 3 and 4, and Results 4 and 5.

Time scale and flow described through clock and signal comparison

Interpretive possibility

The same comparison of upper and lower clocks and signal frequencies is described as a difference in time scale and flow.

Independent grounds

The low-velocity Doppler relation based on the equivalence principle and the immediately following correspondence between d⁢sds and d⁢tdt.

Boundary of evidential force

The stationary-clock scale relation is given in Eqs. (7.2.2)–(7.2.3); elapsed proper time for a moving clock includes the velocity term in Eq. (7.3.1).

Corresponding formal evidence

Time Contents 7–9, Transfers 5 and 6, and Results 6 and 7.
