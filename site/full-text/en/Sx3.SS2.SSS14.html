Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

Repair Scope

Individual Statements

Verbatim original (English):

if we insist on viewing RR, and hence XX, as a “time” coordinate

The first quotation uses the model variables RR and XX.

The source location is DeWitt, Quantum Theory of Gravity. I. The Canonical Theory, Physical Review 160, immediately after Eq. (7.16) in §7.

Verbatim original (English):

It is for this reason that we may say that “time” is only a phenomenological concept, useful under certain circumstances.

The source location is §8, p. 1137, of the same paper.

Verbatim original (English):

For the only “time” which a covariant theory can admit is an intrinsic time defined by the contents of the universe itself.

The source location is §8, p. 1137, of the same paper, in the paragraph after the second quotation, following two sentences on general covariance and the phenomenological character of quantum mechanics in everyday use.

Verbatim original (English):

Any intrinsically defined time is necessarily non-Hermitian, which is equivalent to saying that there exists no clock, whether geometrical or material, which can yield a measure of time which is operationally valid under all circumstances, and hence there exists no operational method for determining the Schrödinger state function with arbitrarily high precision.

The source location is §8, p. 1137, of the same paper, following the immediately preceding sentence concerning intrinsic time.

Verbatim original (English):

Because every clock has a “one-sided” energy spectrum, its ultimate accuracy must necessarily be inversely proportional to its rest mass. When the whole universe is cast in the role of a clock, the concept of time can of course be made fantastically accurate (at least in principle) because of the enormity of the masses and quantum numbers involved. But as long as the universe is finite, a theoretical limit to the accuracy nevertheless remains.

The source location is §8, p. 1137, of the same paper. In the second sentence of the quotation, DeWitt discusses the accuracy obtained when the entire universe is treated as a clock.

Objects of the Statements

The treatment of the model variables RR and XX as time coordinates; the status of time as a phenomenological concept; intrinsic time defined by the contents of the universe; the non-Hermiticity of intrinsic time and the operational limits of clocks; and the dependence of clock precision on rest mass and its limit in a finite universe.

Original-Source Locations

The first quotation is immediately after Eq. (7.16) in §⁢7\S 7, and the second through fifth quotations are in §⁢8\S 8, p. 1137. The second quotation ends the opening paragraph on that page; the third and fourth quotations occur consecutively in the next paragraph. The fifth quotation also belongs to the discussion of clock precision in that paragraph.

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

The first quotation belongs to a context that conditionally describes a time-coordinate interpretation of the variables RR and XX in the closed Friedmann model. Equation (7.13) of the source defines X=R3/2X=R^{3/2}. With mass eigenvalues and the stated boundary condition, the source treats Eq. (7.14) as a Schrödinger equation and discusses normalizability when RR or XX is regarded as a time coordinate. The second quotation belongs to a context that treats time as a phenomenological concept useful under certain circumstances. The third quotation belongs to a context that restricts the time admissible to a covariant theory to intrinsic time defined by the contents of the universe. The fourth quotation belongs to a context that identifies the non-Hermiticity of that intrinsic time as equivalent to the nonexistence of a clock that yields an operationally valid measure of time under all circumstances. The fifth quotation belongs to the context of a clock’s one-sided energy spectrum, rest mass, the treatment of the whole universe as a clock, and the precision limit in a finite universe.

Attribution of the Statements

All five quotations are attributable to DeWitt.

Predicates Assigned to the Objects by the Statements

The first quotation places a condition on treating RR and XX as time coordinates. The second describes time as a phenomenological concept useful under certain circumstances. The third describes the only time admissible to a covariant theory as intrinsic time defined by the contents of the universe. The fourth describes intrinsic time as necessarily non-Hermitian and states this to be equivalent to the nonexistence of a clock providing a universally valid measure of time. The fifth makes the ultimate precision of a clock inversely proportional to its rest mass and states that a theoretical precision limit remains in a finite universe.
