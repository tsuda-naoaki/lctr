Statement evidence 1: a single mathematical description of redshift

Original text (English):

The central thrust of this paper is that it is incorrect to think that there are three distinct mechanisms for redshifting photons in relativity, and that there is truly only a single underlying mathematical description for use in all occasions.

Primary-source location

Lewis, On the Relativity of Redshifts: Does Space Really “Expand”?, Australian Physics, Vol. 53, No. 3, the Universal Redshifting section on p. 98.

Minimal context

The paragraph introduces Narlikar’s 1994 generalization of Synge’s argument. “This paper” in the quotation refers to Narlikar’s paper.

Attribution

Geraint F. Lewis’s account of the central claim of Narlikar’s 1994 paper.

The predicate is that relativistic redshift has a single underlying mathematical description applicable in all cases. The explicit domain of application is the relativistic description of Doppler, gravitational, and cosmological redshift compared in the primary source.

Individual Audit

A single mathematical description of redshift

The Narlikar account introduced in the first statement parallel transports the same photon’s four-vector along its light path and compares local measurements obtained by contraction with the observer time direction at each endpoint. Transfers 1 and 2 in Table 577, together with Results 3 and 4, express this relation. Its inputs include emission–reception order, the metric’s temporal and causal structure, and the observer time directions.

Relational attribution of wavelength

The photon–observer relation in the second statement is expressed by contraction of the photon four-vector and observer time direction at the same measurement event. The energy and corresponding wavelength measured for the same photon depend on the observer’s state of motion. Under coordinate transformation with the specified observer fixed, this local reading is preserved. Time Contents 4–8 retain the inputs and readings of this comparison and connect them to Results 1, 2, and 10.

FRW comoving observers and the expanding-space picture

The particular scenario in the third statement is photon emission and reception by comoving observers in FRW spacetime. Their proper separation changes with the scale factor, and the ratio of wavelengths measured at the endpoints corresponds to the ratio of scale factors at reception and emission. Time Contents 2, 8, 10–12, and 14 represent emission–reception order, local readings, cosmic time, scale-factor history, proper-distance history, and the wavelength-stretching picture, supporting Results 7, 8, and 11. Lewis points out that treating space as a physical medium, like a river carrying objects, leads to incorrect conclusions about their motion.

Local Doppler description and coordinate representation

The source construction preceding the fourth statement compares adjacent members of a large collection of comoving observers evenly spread between FRW comoving endpoints A and B. The source describes the endpoint redshift as a long series of Doppler shifts. Cancellation of ratios for a finite reading sequence is Result 5; local comparisons between FRW comoving observers are Result 9. A change of coordinate representation for the same physical spacetime preserves redshift at the specified endpoints and spacetime curvature, as expressed by Result 10.

Common Audit

The redshift accounts in the source depend on photon propagation, observer time directions, local readings, and the temporal histories of the models. Table 579 and Proposition S.615 give these dependencies and the generation judgment.
