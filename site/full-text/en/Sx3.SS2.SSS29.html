Formal Objects

The audit uses 45 time contents, 84 claims, 27 transfers and 43 results. The tables specify the constructions and conditions of ordinary quantum theory, relativistic spacetime, ADM constraints, internal clocks, reference fluids, Wheeler–DeWitt interpretations, WKB time, conditional probabilities, histories and evolving constants. Isham examines the problem of time in canonical quantum gravity, starting from the relation between the external time used in ordinary quantum theory and the dynamical spacetime metric of general relativity. He discusses the constructions and obstacles of internal-time reduction, material clocks, unimodular time, Wheeler–DeWitt interpretations, WKB time, conditional probabilities, histories and evolving constants.

Assumptions

Each approach uses specified metrics, canonical variables and states, together with constraints and boundary conditions. Quantum evolution with an internal clock requires a clock domain, constraint root, operator domain, inner product and integrability. WKB constructions use a selected phase and branch and an approximation order; history probabilities use state, coarse-graining and decoherence conditions. Each transfer and result states the conditions of its approach.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Claims 1–4 are the initial inputs for generation: spatial geometry and canonical data, functions and coefficients, states and operators, and boundary and coarse-graining data. The conditions under which each approach assigns temporal meaning and operations to these data are given in the corresponding claims and transfers.

The source reports zero directly measured objects and zero direct-measurement predicates from measurements of its own. Proposals belong to their respective proponents; the comparative assessments in the review are Isham’s.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count is zero; 12 time contents are locally reachable under source-evidence conditions, leaving 33 primitive-time roots. Local reach includes the internal times defined using scale factor, geometric momentum and matter field; York time; logarithmic configuration time; WKB time; and the reduced evolution or conserved flux satisfying the respective conditions. The corresponding time contents are 19–26, 33, 35, 37–38.

Result Dependence

Of the 43 results, 26 depend on primitive-time roots and 17 are reachable from the source formal-evidence environment. The latter are Results 6, 9–12, 17, 19, 22, 25–27, 29–31, 33, 37, 42. No result is generated from nontemporal physical inputs alone. There are 47 root–result dependence pairs, of which 43 are necessary.

Evidentiary Effect

Isham presents three classes of approaches to the problem of time and their specific constructions, conditions and obstacles. Internal-clock definitions, reduced evolution, positive-frequency interpretations, WKB approximations, conditional probabilities and histories have the distinct conditions stated in the result table. Identifying history families that yield quasiclassical clocks remains an unresolved construction task in the source.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

External instants, Lorentzian causal structure, worldlines and endpoints, foliations, material clocks, clock sections and history ordering provide temporal premises for the respective approaches. None of the 45 time contents belongs to the generation closure from nontemporal physical inputs alone, and 33 roots remain even after the local constructions under source formal evidence. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Isham discusses global clock obstructions, energy conditions for Gaussian reference fluids, the obstruction to the positive-frequency method and changes at the next WKB order. Actual failure of the physical-time generation conditions audited by LCTR, and consequent changes in result values, are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Result-Preservation Domain

Individual Statements

Objects of the Statements

A definition of time using only the canonical variables of the gravitational field; physical clocks made of matter; the scale that fixes the lowest order of the WKB expansion; and the first-order Schrödinger form that is lost at the next WKB order.

Verbatim Originals (English)

a satisfactory definition of time using only the canonical variables of the gravitational field

Real physical clocks are made of matter with definite properties

to lowest order in an expansion in powers of ℏ⁢κ2≃(LP)2\hbar\kappa^{2}\simeq{(L_{P})}^{2} (where LP:=(G⁢ℏ/c3)1/2L_{P}:={\left(G\hbar/c^{3}\right)}^{1/2} is the Planck length)

The elegant first-order form of the Schrödinger equation (5.4.10) is lost at the next order in the WKB approximation. This makes it difficult to assess the proper status of (5.4.10) and to understand how the physics of time changes as one gets nearer to the Planck regime.

The third quotation states that the lowest order is taken in an expansion in powers of ℏ⁢κ2≃(LP)2\hbar\kappa^{2}\simeq{(L_{P})}^{2}, where LP:=(G⁢ℏ/c3)1/2L_{P}:={\left(G\hbar/c^{3}\right)}^{1/2} is the Planck length.

Original-Source Locations

The first and second quotations are in §4.3.1, p. 59; the third is between Eqs. (5.4.3)–(5.4.5) in §5.4.2, p. 79; the fourth is the fifth item in §5.4.4, p. 82. Page numbers refer to the text of arXiv:gr-qc/9210011.

Minimal Context

In the sentence containing the first quotation, Isham states the difficulty of obtaining a satisfactory time definition from the gravitational canonical variables alone, then turns to using matter to define time. The second quotation states that real clocks are made of matter with definite properties. The third specifies the expansion scale used to obtain the lowest-order Hamilton–Jacobi equation from the WKB form; the fourth discusses the change in the equation at the next order of that WKB scheme.

Attributed Author of the Statements

All four quotations are attributable to the text of the review by C. J. Isham.

Content Stated Directly by the Quotations

The first quotation names the object whose construction Isham describes as difficult: a satisfactory time definition using only gravitational canonical variables. The second states the material constitution of physical clocks; the third specifies the lowest WKB order; the fourth states that the first-order Schrödinger form is lost at the next WKB order.

Explicit Domains of Application of the Statements

The first and second quotations concern internal time and material clocks in canonical quantum gravity. The third and fourth apply to the Wheeler–DeWitt equation, phase branch and WKB expansion orders used in the source.

Evidence Provenance

C. J. Isham, Canonical Quantum Gravity and the Problem of Time, arXiv:gr-qc/9210011, pp. 59, 79, 82.

Individual Audits

First Statement

Isham states the difficulty of obtaining a satisfactory time definition from the gravitational canonical variables alone. Section 4.2 examines canonical transformations for internal clocks, uniqueness of constraint solutions, inequivalent clock choices after quantization, functional evolution and foliation-independent event times. Claims 27–41 and Results 7–12 give the specific constructions, conditions and obstacles.

Second Statement

Isham describes real physical clocks as systems made of matter with definite properties. Section 4.3 examines coupling between clock matter and gravity, point clocks and clock clouds, and clock constraints in a Gaussian reference fluid. Claims 42–45 and Results 13–15 give the formal Schrödinger derivation, energy conditions and reference-coordinate domain.

Third Statement

Isham specifies the lowest WKB order using a scale related to the gravitational coupling and Planck length. Isham specifies a dimensionless expansion parameter formed from the coupling and a scale of the system. Claims 58–60 and Results 25–27 give the Hamilton–Jacobi equation from the selected real phase, WKB time and matter evolution.

Fourth Statement

Isham states that the simple first-order Schrödinger form of the lowest-order equation is lost at the next order of the specified WKB expansion. The time function defined by Eq. (5.4.11) is a local coordinate along the flow of the selected real phase; the simple WKB approximation breaks down at turning points of that phase. Claims 59–62 and Results 26–27 give the time construction and the approximation order and domain of the evolution equation.

Common Audit with Specified Domain of Application

The formal grounds and domains of the four statements are determined by the internal-clock, material-clock and WKB conditions in the corresponding claims, transfers and results.
