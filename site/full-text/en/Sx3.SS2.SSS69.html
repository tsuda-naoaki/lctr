Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the grounding obligation

The 24 time contents audited in this source are not generated from nontemporal physical inputs before use and are accepted as already-formed time inputs. The placement determination for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This determination follows by comparing the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation with the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation condition or formation condition as an input, a formation condition, an evaluation condition, or a failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete-generation and residual-root determinations concern physical-time admissibility and are distinct from the actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. The source’s formal evidence and the LCTR audit evidence do not establish the actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure determination is “indeterminate from the primary-source evidence”; actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of the grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream reach

The reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root–result dependency relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because the actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach determination from such a failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the primary-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair scope

On the basis of structural reach, the repair targets are the corresponding time contents and the generation conditions or formation conditions required before their use. The recalculation scope is limited to results to which an independently established actual failure reaches through dependency. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their domains of validity are restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

According to the audacious new theory of general relativity he published that year, time is also affected by gravity. The more strongly you feel the force of gravity, Einstein argued, the slower time passes for you.

Common Evidence with a Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Gravity and the Passage of Time

Interpretive possibilities

In the context of the subsequent clock tests, GPS, and height comparisons, the first statement describes gravitationally conditioned comparisons of clock rates and proper time in everyday language.

Independent grounds

Paragraph 5 describes clock comparisons under motion and gravitational conditions; Paragraph 6 gives the negative kinematic and positive gravitational GPS contributions; and Paragraphs 8 and 9 describe gravitational effects using optical clocks in 2010 and 2024.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

The gravitational rate comparison of stationary clocks depends on the gravitational potential difference; comparisons involving moving clocks also depend on motion. For the ISS, the magnitude of the negative kinematic contribution exceeds the positive gravitational contribution relative to a ground clock, giving a negative net rate difference[65].

Corresponding formal evidence

The correspondence is to time contents 4, 5, 10, and 12; Claims 4–6, 10, and 13; Results 2, 3, 6, and 8; and Proposition S.681. The ISS rate comparison corresponds to time content 17, and its plan to Claims 19 and 20 and Result 13.

Within-source comparison

Paragraph 6 distinguishes the kinematic, gravitational, and net GPS contributions. The explanation in Paragraph 11 that ISS clocks run faster than ground clocks is Claim 25; the gravitational contribution alone does not determine the sign of the net clock-rate difference. The two ISS clocks, their altitude of 370–460 km, and the approximately fiftyfold precision target constitute the experiment plan in Claims 19 and 20.

The Twins’ Age Difference at Reunion

Interpretive possibilities

The second statement assumes a reunion after either a near-light-speed spacecraft journey or a stay near a black hole. When initial ages are equal and elapsed ages follow proper time, a larger Earthbound proper elapsed time makes the Earthbound twin older at reunion.

Independent grounds

The original introduces the travel conditions with “if” and states the consequence “according to relativity.” The following paragraph on portable atomic clocks explains apparatus and measurements used to test relativistic clock predictions.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

The age difference at reunion is determined by the difference of proper elapsed times along the two worldlines between the corresponding departure and reunion events. Determining that difference requires the travel speeds, gravitational conditions, and worldlines.

Corresponding formal evidence

The correspondence is to time contents 1, 2, and 6–8; Claim 7; Transfer 1; Result 4; and Proposition S.681.

Within-source comparison

The twin example uses proper elapsed time along worldlines; the clock-test account uses clock-rate comparisons under motion and gravitational conditions. In each case, the temporal inputs accompany the specified objects and comparison conditions.
