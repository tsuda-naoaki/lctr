Supplement: Primitive-Time-Root Audit of 72 Sources

Each source division in this supplement separates formal evidence from statement evidence at the highest level. Formal evidence is divided into three roles: “formal description and location in the original source,” “audit decomposition and dependency reconstruction by this paper,” and “audit determination by LCTR.” The first presents definitions, formulas, propositions, operations, or conditions stated in the original source and their locations. The second presents the decomposition, typing, and dependency reconstruction of this paper under Definition 7.88. The third presents the LCTR determinations concerning primitive-time roots, generation reach, root-dependent results, evidence-effect domains and boundaries, and result-preservation domains. The second and third are not the terminology, classifications, or verbatim expressions of the original author.

Statement evidence is divided into two roles: “quotation in the source language and translation where required” and “statement-evidence record by this paper.” A quotation environment contains the text in the evidentiary source language. When an identified standard English edition is adopted for a classical source, the quotation is taken from that edition. When the evidentiary source is in another language, an English translation corresponding to the immediately preceding source-language quotation is supplied. The “statement-evidence record by this paper” records the statement target, minimal context, attribution, predicate, explicit application domain, interpretive possibilities, interpretation-specific evidential boundaries, and corresponding formal evidence.

For local keys within a source, the column heading states explicitly whether they refer to source locations, time-content sets, or evidence-support sets. A local key is not a theoretical symbol extending across sources. In source-specific tables, (i), (a), (p), (m), (k), and (j) identify rows of time contents, auxiliary objects, source claims or formal components, mathematical-object types, semantic components, and results, respectively. In lookup tables, (d), ℓ\ell, and (e) denote dependency-object, source-location, and formal-evidence keys, respectively. Each symbol ranges over the finite index set fixed by the corresponding definition or lookup table and has scope only within that table.

The “determination values” for each source present three values in the order “complete,” “local reach,” and “remaining roots,” as defined in Definition 7.41.
