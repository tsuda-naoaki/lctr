Grounds of Establishment

Grounds for Establishing the Results

Results 1–4 rest on the distinction between local and common time, the definition and assumed consistency of synchronization, and the construction of coordinate time. Results 5–7 rest on kinematic derivations under the relativity principle and light-speed postulate. Results 8–10 apply this coordinate construction and transformation to the source’s conditions for fields, radiation, charge and electrons.

Evidence Hierarchy

Directly Measured Objects

This reference reports new direct-measurement data for zero objects.

Directly Measured Predicates

This reference reports new direct-measurement data for zero predicates. The light-speed ratio is placed as an assumption with empirical universality, and the three electron relations belonging to result 10 are presented as predictive objects open to experimental examination.

Conditions and Domain of Application of Direct Measurement

Universality of the light-speed ratio is an assumption based on experience, and the three electron relations are presented for experimental examination. The paper reports no new measured values.

Objects of Formal Generalization

The domain of application of results 1–7 is a kinematical system equipped with the principle of relativity, the light-speed assumption, rigid rods, clock synchronization, and inertial coordinate systems. The domain of application of results 8–10 is a system that additionally includes the assumptions concerning electromagnetic fields, charge, current, radiation, and electron motion in §§6–10 of the original source.

Conditions and Domain of Application of Formal Generalization

Each result uses the temporal prerequisites and supporting claims in Table 47, together with the following conditions. The curved closed path in result 6 follows the source’s extension assumption; the equatorial and polar clocks are compared under otherwise identical conditions. The second-order clock-lag approximation omits fourth and higher orders in speed. The group property in result 7 concerns parallel coordinate transformations. Result 9 uses the distant-source plane-wave approximation, volume comparison for the same light complex, and energy balance at a moving mirror. Charge constancy in result 10 is conditional on constancy in the comoving frame. Longitudinal and transverse masses follow the source’s definitions using instantaneously comoving force and stationary-frame acceleration; kinetic energy assumes slow acceleration with negligible radiative loss.

Evidence Effect

Evidence-Effect Domain

The formal evidence in this reference directly supports 10 results concerning the definitions of clock synchronization and coordinate time, the Lorentz transformation under the principle of relativity and the light-speed assumption, simultaneity, length, clock rate, velocity composition, electromagnetic fields, Doppler relations, radiation, charge, and electron motion. The evidence types supplied by this reference itself are definitions, assumptions, formal derivations, and predictions.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The validity of formal derivations that take the principle of relativity, the light-speed assumption, synchronization conventions, and coordinate time as inputs does not imply that the 131 time contents have been generated from non-temporal physical inputs. Nor does the success of predictions or experiments in other references retroactively confer physical-time qualification on the time contents of this reference. These transitions lie beyond the evidence-effect boundary under Definition 7.115.

Result-Preservation Domain

Result-Preservation Domain

The 10 results are preserved when each time-content prerequisite is supplied by the corresponding generated time representation in the LCTR generation sequence and the principle of relativity, emission–propagation–reception relations, light-speed relation, coordinate transformations, fields, charges, forces, and the directions of dependence in the original source are retained. Results concerning clock rate, clock retardation, length, frequency, and velocity are preserved as comparison relations on generated time representations. Attribution that proceeds from comparison differences on physical bearers to changes in properties of time itself independent of physical bearers falls outside the preservation domain.

Placement of Grounding Obligations

Placement of Grounding Obligations

The source requires the meaning of time to be made clear for descriptions of motion to have physical meaning, and identifies the insufficiency of a local clock for comparing events at separated points. Its construction of coordinate time uses simultaneity between local events and clock indications, emission, reflection and return of light signals, and a convention equating time intervals. The 131 time contents, including these, are used before generation from non-temporal physical inputs; the LCTR classification of the placement of the physical-time grounding obligation is therefore “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

The complete-generation judgment and remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from the actual invalidation of a specific condition that determines the value or domain of application of a result. Neither the original-source formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence for this reference establishes the actual invalidation of such a target condition. The actual-invalidation judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence”; actual invalidation is not inferred solely from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

The reach to results that depend on primitive time roots is determined for this reference by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because the actual invalidation of a specific condition has not been established, the judgment on actual reach from that invalidation position to results is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the targets of repair are the corresponding time content and the generation or establishment conditions required before its use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual invalidation has been independently established and that are reached by dependence from the invalidation position. Because actual invalidation is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If the condition holds, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted.

Individual Statements

Statement Objects

The definitions of simultaneity and time, the relation between the time of an event and a simultaneous clock indication, and the speed of light VV and the time interval used in its definition as a speed.

Original Text (German)

eine Definition von „gleichzeitig“ und „Zeit“ gewonnen

Die „Zeit“ eines Ereignisses ist die mit dem Ereignis gleichzeitige Angabe einer am Orte des Ereignisses befindlichen, ruhenden Uhr, welche mit einer bestimmten, ruhenden Uhr, und zwar für alle Zeitbestimmungen mit der nämlichen Uhr, synchron läuft.

Jeder Lichtstrahl bewegt sich im „ruhenden“ Koordinatensystem mit der bestimmten Geschwindigkeit VV, unabhängig davon, ob dieser Lichtstrahl von einem ruhenden oder bewegten Körper emittiert ist. Hierbei ist

Geschwindigkeit=LichtwegZeitdauer,\text{Geschwindigkeit}=\frac{\text{Lichtweg}}{\text{Zeitdauer}}\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak

wobei „Zeitdauer“ im Sinne der Definition des § 1 aufzufassen ist.

English Translation

A definition of “simultaneous” and “time” has been obtained.

The “time” of an event is the indication, simultaneous with that event, of a stationary clock at the event’s location. This clock runs synchronously with one particular stationary clock—the same clock for all time determinations.

Every ray of light moves in the “stationary” coordinate system with the definite speed VV, independently of whether this ray of light is emitted by a stationary or moving body. Here,

speed=light pathtime interval\text{speed}=\frac{\text{light path}}{\text{time interval}}

and “time interval” is to be understood in the sense of the definition in § 1.

Original-Source Locations

The first and second quotations are from Einstein, Zur Elektrodynamik bewegter Körper, Annalen der Physik 17, p. 894, §1; the third quotation is from the same reference, p. 895, §2.

Minimal Context

Section 1 of the original source starts from the correspondence between an event at one point and a clock indication, introduces a relation for synchronizing clocks at two separated points by light signals, and then assigns a common time to events in the stationary system. Section 2 of the original source states the principle of relativity and the principle of constant light speed, and expressly gives the speed of light VV in the stationary coordinate system as the ratio of the light path to the time interval defined in §1.

Attribution

The three statements are attributed to §§1–2 of Einstein’s original German paper of 1905.

Predicates

The first quotation states that definitions of simultaneity and time have been obtained. The second defines the event’s time as the simultaneous indication of a stationary clock at the event’s location, synchronized with one particular stationary clock common to all time determinations. The third makes the speed of light VV in the stationary coordinate system independent of the emitting body’s motion and gives speed as the ratio of light path to the interval defined in §1.

Explicit Domain of Application

The domain of application of the first and second quotations is the stationary system in §1 of the original source, an event and clock at the same point, and a network of clocks at separated points synchronized by light signals. The domain of application of the third quotation is the stationary coordinate system in §2 of the original source, rays of light emitted by stationary or moving bodies, the light path, and the time interval defined in §1.

Individual Audit

Interpretation of the Statements

Together, the three statements specify event time by a simultaneous local-clock indication, synchronize distant clocks by light signals, and construct coordinate time using the synchronized stationary-clock network. The speed of light VV is expressed as the ratio of light path to the interval defined in this construction. LCTR imposes obligations for generation from non-temporal physical inputs and for consistency on the clock indications, signal events, records, comparisons and transport used here.

Independent Grounds

The three statements give formal and operational definitions of clock indication, synchronization convention, and the representation of the speed of light. The quoted scope contains no independent measurement showing that the time contents used by these definitions were generated from non-temporal physical inputs, and no new experimental result that directly tests the temporal qualification of the speed of light.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first and second quotations directly support the definitions of simultaneity, clock indication, and the time of the stationary system in §1 of the original source. The third quotation directly supports the original notation VV, independence from the state of motion of the emitting body, the definition of speed by light path and time interval, and the dependence of that time interval on the definition in §1. The three quotations do not establish that local-clock indication, light path, time interval, speed ratio, and synchronization relation themselves were generated from non-temporal physical inputs, or that the resulting coordinate time and representation of the speed of light possess physical-time qualification. These matters lie beyond the evidence-effect boundary of the three quotations under Definition 7.115.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The first and second quotations correspond to time contents 6–38 and 41–42 (location groups 3–11 and 13) in Definition S.19, formal-evidence support components 8–17 in Definition S.20, and results 1–4 in Definition S.22. The third quotation corresponds to time contents 39–42 and 45–49 in Definition S.19, formal-evidence support components 4–5 and 16 in Definition S.20, and results 2 and 7 in Definition S.22.

Within-Reference Comparison

The first and second statements construct coordinate time from local-clock indications and distant-clock synchronization, and the third statement represents the speed of light by using that time interval. The light-speed statement therefore depends on the preceding clock and synchronization definitions, but that dependence does not substitute for the physical generation of clock indication, recording, comparison, and transport.
