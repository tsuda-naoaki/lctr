Individual statements

Statement 1

Original (English):

Environment-induced decoherence and superselection have been a subject of intensive research over the past two decades, yet their implications for the foundational problems of quantum mechanics, most notably the quantum measurement problem, have remained a matter of great controversy.

The source location is the abstract. Schlosshauer summarizes the research accumulated over the two decades preceding the review and the continuing controversy over its foundational implications, including the quantum measurement problem.

Statement 2

Original (English):

In short, decoherence brings about a local suppression of interference between preferred states selected by the interaction with the environment.

The source location is Section I. Schlosshauer describes quantum correlations formed through environmental interaction and identifies local interference suppression between preferred states as their local consequence.

Statement 3

Original (English):

Thus, without supplying an additional physical process (say, some collapse mechanism) or giving a suitable interpretation of such a superposition, it is not clear how to account, given the final composite state, for the definite pointer positions that are perceived as the result of an actual measurement—i.e., why do we seem to perceive the pointer to be in one position |an⟩\lvert a_{n}\rangle but not in a superposition of positions?

The source location is the premeasurement discussion following equation (2.1) in Section II.A. Schlosshauer considers a physical process or interpretation for explaining a perceived definite pointer position from the correlated system–apparatus state after unitary evolution.

Statement 4

Original (English):

However, the crucial difficulty in the above reasoning is justifying the second step: How is one to interpret the local suppression of interference in spite of the fact that full coherence is retained in the total state that describes the system-environment combination? While the local destruction of interference allows one to infer the emergence of an (improper) ensemble of individually localized components of the wave function, one still needs to impose an interpretive framework that explains why only one of the localized states is realized and/or perceived.

The source location is Section IV.A. Schlosshauer examines the passage from preferred-basis selection and local interference suppression to definite outcomes, distinguishing coherence in the total state from the realization or perception of a single localized state.

Individual audit

Research history and continuing controversy

Statement 1 is Schlosshauer’s summary of research history. It concerns the studies and interpretive disagreements reviewed in the source. Sections I and IV present the development of the research and the issues associated with the respective interpretations.

Local suppression between preferred states

In Statement 2, local suppression is the process by which environmental overlaps make the off-diagonal elements of the reduced density matrix small. Claims 10–12, Transfers 1–2 and Results 4–7 represent this process using the subsystem partition, interaction, total unitary evolution and partial trace. The full state includes environmental correlations and retains its coherence.

Conditions for explaining a definite pointer position

Statement 3 asks how a correlated superposition in source equation (2.1) accounts for the definite pointer position perceived in an actual measurement. Claims 7–8 and Result 1 represent premeasurement and the definite-outcome problem. The alternatives specified by Schlosshauer are an additional physical process or a suitable interpretation of the superposition.

The full state and a single localized state

Statement 4 distinguishes coherence of the total system–environment state from the improper mixture in the local reduced state. Claims 9–11, 18, 45 and Results 3–6, 30 represent this state distinction and the interpretive conditions explaining realization or perception of one localized state.
