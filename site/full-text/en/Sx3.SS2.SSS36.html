Result-Preservation Domain

Placement of Grounding Obligations

Placement of grounding obligations

The six time contents audited in this source are not generated from nontemporal physical input before use; they are accepted as already-valid temporal inputs. The placement determination for the physical-time grounding obligations is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This determination follows by comparing, for each time content, its use in a law operation or result derivation with the absence, prior to that use, of a corresponding generation or validity condition supplied as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete determination and residual-root determination concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from an actual failure of a concrete condition governing the value or domain of application of a result. Neither the source’s formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure determination is “indeterminate from source evidence”; actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream reach

For primitive-time-root-dependent results, the reach is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because no actual failure of a concrete condition has been established, determination of actual reach from the failure location to a result is “indeterminate from source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair scope

On the basis of structural reach, the repair targets are the corresponding time contents and the generation or validity conditions required before their use. The scope of recalculation is restricted to results reached from a failure location when actual failure has been independently established. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their domain of validity is restricted. The value after recalculation is fixed as an audit result.

Common Evidence with Delimited Scope

The following four statements give the original text of Headley, Clayton, and Reed 1996, its source location, minimal context, and source attribution.

Individual Audit

Frequency difference between the pump wave and Stokes wave

The source identifies νB\nu_{B} as the frequency difference between the pump and Stokes waves and gives νB=2⁢n⁢vA/λp\nu_{B}=2nv_{A}/\lambda_{p} in Equation (1). This is a relation within the model of pump–Stokes–acoustic coupling.

Equation (1) takes the refractive index, pump wavelength, and acoustic-wave velocity as inputs. A relation between time-bearing velocity and frequency does not establish the physical generation of their time metric. The corresponding formal evidence is claims 1–7, transfer 1, and result 1.

Lorentzian profile

The source states that the Brillouin gain spectrum has a Lorentzian profile under exponential acoustic-wave decay and either CW or QCW operation. The QCW condition is T0≫TBT_{0}\gg T_{B}. Result 2 combines the CW and QCW statements with their respective applicability conditions. Time prerequisite 5 represents the pulse width in the QCW condition; it is not required for the CW-only specialization. The gain-spectrum variable ν\nu and its peak location νB\nu_{B} are distinct: ν=νB\nu=\nu_{B} holds at the peak.

The basis of this conditional statement is the gain-spectrum model presented with a reference to work [1] in the source. Gain reduction for short pulses, T0<TBT_{0}<T_{B}, is a phenomenon in an operating regime different from CW/QCW. The corresponding formal evidence is claims 8–11 and 14, and result 2.

Full width at half maximum

Δ⁢νB\Delta\nu_{B} is the ordinary-frequency full width at half maximum of the Brillouin gain spectrum and satisfies Δ⁢νB=(π⁢TB)−1\Delta\nu_{B}=(\pi T_{B})^{-1} in the source’s exponential-decay and Lorentzian model. The width definition and the lifetime relation describe the same quantity together.

Conversion to angular-frequency width gives Δ⁢ωB=2⁢π⁢Δ⁢νB\Delta\omega_{B}=2\pi\Delta\nu_{B}. The width–lifetime relation holds under the source’s exponential-decay convention and Lorentzian model. The corresponding formal evidence is claims 10–13, transfer 2, and result 3.

Reduction of SBS gain

The source reports the finding of earlier work [3] that, for short pulses T0<TBT_{0}<T_{B}, SBS gain is significantly reduced and other nonlinear effects become important.

“Significantly” does not set a numerical threshold for the gain reduction in this paragraph. The corresponding formal evidence is claims 14 and 15, which contrast this operating regime with the CW/QCW conditions of result 2.

Within-source correspondence among the four statements

The four statements concern, respectively, frequency difference, line-shape family, width measure, and regime-dependent gain change. Proposition S.340 determines the generative reachability and result dependence of the time contents corresponding to each statement.
