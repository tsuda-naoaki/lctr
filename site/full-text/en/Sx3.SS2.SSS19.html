Grounds of Establishment

Grounds for the Results
  1. 1.

    Metric and Clocks Events, coordinates, worldlines and the metric have distinct roles. Proper time along a timelike curve is given by a line integral and is stationary on a fixed-endpoint geodesic. Determination of the metric without standard clocks uses coordinate-time velocities and accelerations, light rays and free-fall paths, metric compatibility and a scale fixed at one event. The geodesic clock calibrates ticks from a fiducial timelike interval, timelike geodesics and light signals, then measures intervals under its curvature and resolution conditions.

  2. 2.

    Material Realization of Clocks and Aging Atomic and molecular clocks realize the ideal proper-time reading within their precision and operating domains. Universal velocity time dilation is distinguished from device-dependent responses to acceleration and tidal stress. The source states that human aging is governed by the same electromagnetic and quantum-mechanical laws as atomic and molecular processes and is tied to proper time. This correspondence holds under the biological and environmental conditions of the physical system.

    Hypersurfaces and Initial Data The ADM decomposition relates the slice label, lapse, shift and normal displacement. The twelve statements are attributable to Charles W. Misner, Kip S. Thorne, and John Archibald Wheeler. For each statement, its verbatim original, original-source location, and minimal context required for interpretation are given.

Individual Audit

Interpretations Allowed by the Original Text and Minimal Context

Statements 1 and 2 concern the time coordinate used to describe local inertial motion uniformly and the stability of that standard. Statement 3 gives the geometrized-unit convention; Statement 4 gives the relation among observer-measured photon quantities. Statements 5 and 6 relate cosmic expansion to Hubble time and age estimates in the specified model. Statements 7 and 8 concern theory-dependent position/time dependence of the Cavendish quantity. Statement 9 asserts the absence of spacetime, time, before, after and next in the domain of quantum geometrodynamics. Statement 10 distinguishes universal velocity time dilation from device-dependent acceleration responses. Statements 11 and 12 state that human aging obeys the same material laws as atomic and molecular processes and is tied to proper time.

Independent Grounds

The twelve statements are textbook definitions, quantitative relations and theoretical explanations. Time standards and geometrized units occur in Chapter 1; photon quantities in Chapter 2; cosmic ages in Boxes 27.1 and 27.3; the Cavendish quantity in §40.8; and the no-time assertion and classical-history construction in Chapter 43. Clocks and human aging are described as applications of material laws in §16.4; empirical gravitational-redshift comparisons are treated in Chapter 38.

Evidence-Effect Boundary for Each Interpretive Possibility

Statements 1 and 2 concern inertial motion, clock comparison and time standards; Statements 3 and 4 concern unit conversion and observer-specific photon quantities. Statements 5 and 6 distinguish the present expansion rate, linear extrapolation, stellar age and model age. The ratio of at least 1.5 is conditioned on the expansion regime of the specified closed Λ=0\Lambda=0 dust model. Statement 7 assumes that the quoted metric theory correctly describes gravity, and Statement 8 compares predictions of general relativity and other metric theories. Statement 9 belongs to the quantum-geometrodynamic domain; the classical EHJ construction and semiclassical packet conditions in the same chapter hold in their respective domains. Statements 10–12 hold under the material laws and the apparatus, biological and environmental conditions of their application.
