Individual Statements

Objects of the Statements

The incompleteness of quantum gravity theory; the nonuniqueness of the particle concept in curved spacetime; unit conventions and the curvature scale; the domain of validity of the semiclassical approximation in the early universe; conversion of the lifetime of the universe into units of Planck time; the heuristic status of the pair-creation picture near the horizon; positive frequency relative to a horizon time parameter; and failure of the quasi-stationary approximation in the Planck-mass regime.

Verbatim Original (English)

I think it would be fair to say that we do not yet have a fully satisfactory and consistent quantum theory of gravity.

Although it is obvious what the subspace spanned by the {fi}\{f_{i}\} is for an asymptotically flat region, it is not uniquely defined for a general point of a curved space-time.

Thus the uncertainty in the local energy density caused by the ambiguity in defining modes of wavelength longer than the local radius of curvature B−1/2B^{-1/2}, is of order B2B^{2} in units in which G=c=ℏ=1G=c=\hbar=1.

Thus, as far as we are concerned, the classical geometry–quantum matter treatment should be valid apart from the first 10−43⁢s10^{-43}\,\mathrm{s} of the universe.

Even though the effects of particle creation may be negligible locally, I shall show in this paper that they can add up to have a significant influence on black holes over the lifetime of the universe ∼1017⁢s\sim 10^{17}\,\mathrm{s} or 106010^{60} units of Planck time.

One might picture this negative energy flux in the following way.

It should be emphasized that these pictures of the mechanism responsible for the thermal emission and area decrease are heuristic only and should not be taken too literally.

Eventually, when the mass of the black hole is reduced to 10−5⁢g10^{-5}\,\mathrm{g}, the quasi-stationary approximation will break down. At this point, one cannot continue to use the concept of a classical metric.

Minimal Context

The first quotation belongs to the introduction of the semiclassical construction that treats quantum matter on a classical general-relativistic metric. The second concerns ambiguity in the local particle concept for modes whose wavelengths exceed the curvature scale; the third concerns the resulting estimate of local energy density. The fourth and fifth state the curvature limit of the semiclassical treatment and the time scale over which particle-creation effects accumulate. Immediately after introducing the negative-energy pair picture near the horizon, the sixth and seventh restrict that picture to heuristic status. The eighth gives the condition selecting modes on the quasi-stationary horizon in the final state; the ninth concerns the endpoint of the semiclassical description as mass decreases.

The third states an uncertainty estimate set by the curvature scale and a unit convention; the fourth, the domain of validity of the semiclassical treatment in the early universe. The fifth relates the lifetime of the universe in seconds to units of Planck time. The sixth and seventh introduce the pair-creation picture and restrict its status to that of a heuristic picture. The eighth specifies positive frequency relative to a given time parameter; the ninth states that the quasi-stationary approximation and classical-metric description fail in the Planck-mass regime.

Explicit Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first statement says that a consistent quantum theory of gravity remains incomplete. The second and third concern the nonuniqueness of the particle subspace in curved spacetime and the resulting uncertainty in local energy density. The fourth gives an expectation for the early-universe domain of the semiclassical treatment; the fifth gives the cosmic lifetime over which particle-creation effects accumulate. The sixth and seventh concern the heuristic pair picture; the eighth specifies positive frequency relative to the horizon time parameter; the ninth states the breakdown of the quasistationary approximation in the Planck-mass regime.

Independent Grounds

Sections 2 and 3 derive particle creation and thermal emission using field-mode expansions, Bogoliubov coefficients and finite packets. Section 4 establishes the negative horizon-energy flux from stress-tensor conservation, stationarity and agreement at infinity. The local particle concept depends on the curvature scale and the observer’s time choice; quasistationary evolution rests on separation between mass-evolution and light-crossing time scales.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

Ambiguity in particle number and local energy density is evaluated under the selected time coordinate and curvature scale. The convention G=c=ℏ=1G=c=\hbar=1 specifies unit conversion; seconds and Planck-time units express the cosmic lifetime. Pair creation is presented as a heuristic picture. Horizon modes are selected relative to the time parameter defined by KaK^{a}, and the classical-metric and quasistationary descriptions reach their applicability boundary as the mass approaches the Planck scale.
