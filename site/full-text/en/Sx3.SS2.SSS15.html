Not only the measurement of length but also that of time then appears as a derived operation.

we reject clocks as basic tools for setting up the space-time geometry

it claims the equality of gravitational and atomic time

The source location is p. 1590, §1, of the same paper, in the paragraph explaining the chronometric axiom of the prior construction using standard clocks. The quotation is conditional on the preceding interpretation of “clock” as an atomic clock.

Objects of the Statements

The derived-operational status of time measurement, the exclusion of clocks from the basic tools of spacetime geometry, and the equality claim concerning gravitational and atomic time.

Original-Source Locations

All three statements occur on p. 1590, §1, of the 2012 reprint.

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

The first and second statements concern the authors’ own EPS route, which reconstructs spacetime structure from light rays and freely falling particles. In the third statement, “it” refers to the chronometric axiom in the preceding account of Synge’s method. The equality interpretation is conditional on interpreting “clock” as an atomic clock.

Attribution of the Statements

Ehlers, Pirani, and Schild are responsible for the wording of all three quotations. The first and second describe their own construction; the third gives a conditional interpretation of the chronometric axiom in Synge’s method.

Predicates Assigned to the Objects

The first statement describes length and time measurement as derived operations in the EPS route. The second excludes clocks from the basic tools of that construction. The third states that the chronometric axiom claims equality of gravitational and atomic time when “clock” is interpreted as an atomic clock.
