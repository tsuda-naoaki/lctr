Original-Source Location Identifier Set

9-L1.

Assumption Identifier

9-A2.

Assumption Content

Weak field and point particles. In the weak-field expansion, metric deviations from the flat metric, their derivatives and the momenta are first order in a small quantity ϵ\epsilon in suitable coordinates; terms of order ϵ2\epsilon^{2} and higher are discarded in the constraints, and terms of order ϵ3\epsilon^{3} and higher in the Hamiltonian. In the model that sums point-particle contributions, interactions are purely gravitational. The particle expressions for the matter terms are derived without the weak-field approximation, but the source excludes their use in the exact theory because a point source produces a singularity at which the field equations cannot be satisfied accurately (pp. 340–341).

Original-Source Location Identifier Set

9-L1.

Assumption Identifier

9-A3.

Assumption Content

Fourier modes and energy. The spatial Fourier coefficients of real fields at opposite wave vectors are complex conjugates, and the mode decomposition using inverse wave numbers applies to nonzero wave vectors. The momentum-constraint reduction in the gravitational-wave section treats vacuum or a regime in which the momentum of slowly moving matter is negligible. Energy is identified with the weak-field Hamiltonian, and the derivation of Newtonian attraction assumes slowly moving particles. The proposed elimination by a contact transformation belongs to the weak-field regime and does not require slowly moving matter (pp. 341–343).

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The 5 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already-established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment follows by matching the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation against the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or validity condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

For each reference, the reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from that failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the objects of repair are the corresponding time contents and the generation or validity conditions required before their use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual failure is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that failure location. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual Statements

Verbatim original (English):

The basic concept in Hamiltonian theory is that of a state at a given time. In a relativistic theory this is to be understood as the state on a general three-dimensional space-like surface in space-time.

The source location is Dirac, The Theory of Gravitation in Hamiltonian Form, Proceedings of the Royal Society of London A 246, p. 333, Introduction.

Verbatim original (English):

Any dynamical theory must first be put in the Hamiltonian form before one can quantize it.

The source location is p. 333, Introduction, in the same paper.

Verbatim original (English):

The symmetry between the four x’s is completely destroyed

The quoted expression refers to the four coordinates xx.

The source location is p. 333, Introduction, in the same paper, in the paragraph describing the simplification obtained by choosing spacelike surfaces with constant x0x^{0}.

Verbatim original (English):

I am inclined to believe from this that four-dimensional symmetry is not a fundamental property of the physical world.

The source location is p. 343, Conclusion, in the same paper.

Verbatim original (English):

a physical state does not correspond to an individual solution of the equations of motion, but to a family of solutions all related to the same Hamilton’s principal function—it is such a family that corresponds to a wave function in the quantum theory, while the individual solution has no quantum analogue.

The source location is p. 343, Conclusion, in the same paper.

Objects of the Statements

The state at a given time and its generalization to a spacelike surface; the Hamiltonian form preceding quantization; loss of symmetry among the four coordinates upon the choice of a surface with constant x0x^{0}; the physical status of four-dimensional symmetry; and the correspondence between families of classical solutions and quantum wave functions.

Original-Source Locations

Quotations one through three are from Introduction, p. 333; quotations four and five are from Conclusion, p. 343.

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

The first quotation belongs to a context in which the state at a given time in Hamiltonian theory is understood, in a relativistic theory, as the state on a general three-dimensional spacelike surface in spacetime. The second quotation belongs to a context stating a formal requirement prior to quantization. The third quotation concerns the loss of symmetry among the four coordinates in the simplified scheme that considers only states on surfaces with constant x0x^{0}. The same paragraph explains that choosing the surface independently of the coordinate system can preserve the symmetry, at the cost of more variables and more complicated equations. In the fourth quotation, the author tentatively infers a claim about the physical status of four-dimensional symmetry from its loss in this simplified scheme. The same conclusion acknowledges that individual solutions of the equations of motion exhibit four-dimensional symmetry and restricts its abandonment by Hamiltonian methods to their simplest form. The fifth quotation belongs to a context in which a physical state corresponds, not to an individual solution, but to a family of solutions related to the same Hamilton’s principal function.

Attribution of the Statements

Dirac is the author of all five quoted statements.

Predicates Assigned to the Objects by the Statements

The first quotation identifies a state at a given time as the basic concept of Hamiltonian theory and states that its carrier domain in a relativistic theory is a general three-dimensional spacelike surface. The second quotation states that Hamiltonian form is a prerequisite for quantization. The third quotation states that symmetry among the four coordinates is completely destroyed under the adopted simplification. The fourth quotation states that the author is inclined to believe that four-dimensional symmetry is not a fundamental property of the physical world. The fifth quotation associates a physical state with a family of classical solutions and associates that family with a wave function in quantum theory.

Explicit Domains of Application of the Statements

The five quotations apply to theoretical accounts of the Hamiltonian form of general relativity, canonical quantization, the choice of surfaces, the interpretation of four-dimensional symmetry, and the correspondence between families of classical solutions and quantum wave functions.

Individual Audit

Interpretation

The first quotation identifies the basic concept of Hamiltonian theory as a state at a given time and, in relativity, understands it as a state on a general three-dimensional spacelike surface in spacetime. The second quotation requires Hamiltonian form before quantization for any dynamical theory. The third quotation states that choosing a surface of constant x0x^{0} as a simplification removes the representation in which the four coordinates are treated equally. The same introduction also explains that symmetry can be retained in a formulation that selects the surface independently of the coordinate system. The fourth quotation takes the loss of symmetry in the simplified Hamiltonian representation as the starting point for the author’s inclination to regard four-dimensional symmetry as not a fundamental property of the physical world. This is the author’s qualified conjecture. The fifth quotation associates a physical state with a family of classical solutions related to the same Hamilton’s principal function, states that this family corresponds to a quantum wave function, and says that an individual solution has no quantum analogue.

Independent Grounds

The grounds for the five statements are the source’s formulation of states at given times and on spacelike surfaces, its Hamiltonian reconstruction of gravitation, and its theoretical account of the correspondence between families of classical solutions and quantum wave functions. The introduction explains the relation between surface choice and the symmetry of the four coordinates; the conclusion states the author’s conjecture about the physical status of that symmetry based on the simplified formulation.

Evidential Scope

The first quotation supports the relation between a state at a given time and a state on a spacelike surface. The second supports Hamiltonian form as the prerequisite for quantization adopted by the author. The loss of symmetry in the third quotation belongs to the adopted simplification; the fourth quotation is a qualified conjecture that proceeds from this loss to a view about the physical world. The correspondence in the fifth quotation is between a family of solutions related to the same Hamilton’s principal function and a quantum wave function. The five statements provide formal and conceptual theoretical accounts in which the given time, surface order and evolution variables serve as temporal inputs.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The first quotation corresponds to time content 2 and auxiliary objects 2 and 3 in Definition S.70, components 6 and 7 in Definition S.71, and result 2 in Definition S.74. The second quotation corresponds to the prerequisite for quantization treated in auxiliary object 17 and component 5, and states the role of Hamiltonian form in result 1. The third quotation corresponds to the loss of symmetry under the adopted coordinate splitting treated in time contents 2 and 3, components 8 and 44, and result 19. The fourth quotation is the author’s qualified conjecture about the physical status of symmetry, drawn from the loss of symmetry under the simplification treated in component 44 and result 19. The fifth quotation corresponds to the relation between families of classical solutions and quantum wave functions in time contents 3 and 4, auxiliary objects 14–16, components 45–47, and result 20.

Within-Source Comparison

The first and second statements concern the carrier domain of a state and the form required before quantization; the third and fourth concern surface choice and four-dimensional symmetry; the fifth concerns families of classical solutions and quantum states. These relations are formulated using given times, surface order and evolution variables. Propositions S.77 and S.77 give the generation assessment and result dependence of the time contents.
