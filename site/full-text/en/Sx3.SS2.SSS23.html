Formal Objects

The audit uses 23 time contents, 61 claims, 15 transfers and 18 results. Derivative operators are included in the claims and distinguished from their temporal arguments. Rindler distinguishes the Galilean branch, which combines the relativity principle with absolute time, from the Lorentz branch using invariant light speed (pp. 23–25). His account of physical laws as models of a domain of nature, and his distinction between an exact theory in ideal inertial frames and approximate application to local inertial frames, describe his view of physical theory and its domain of application (pp. 25–26).

Rindler criticizes explanations that require general relativity because accelerated phenomena supposedly fall outside special relativity, and explanations that use general relativity to avoid the clock hypothesis. For the former, he states that special relativity applies to accelerated motion described in inertial frames; for the latter, he points out that the clock hypothesis remains a hypothesis in general relativity (p. 47).

Assumptions

Rindler’s coordinate lattice uses a standard clock at the origin, its replicas, a homogeneous and isotropic inertial frame, and Newton’s first law. Coordinate transformations use regularity excluding infinite coordinates for finite events, linearity, continuity and the relativity principle. Each transfer and result specifies the conditions of the derivation using invariant light speed or the derivation without the second postulate. The instantaneous rate of a nonuniformly moving ideal clock uses the clock hypothesis; the instantaneous length of an accelerating infinitesimal body uses the length hypothesis.

Non-temporal Physical Inputs

The initial generation inputs are the carriers of clocks, signals, particles, atoms, aircraft and records; event identities; spatial configurations; length standards; spatial field configurations; and material properties of apparatus in Claims 1–14.

Grounding Conditions

  1. 1.

    In a homogeneous and isotropic inertial frame, Rindler defines as simultaneous the arrival events of light or identical projectiles traveling from a midpoint to equidistant endpoints.

  2. 2.

    Observers at the separate positions use standard clocks and assign equal readings to events defined as simultaneous by symmetric signal propagation. Equal clock rates are presupposed.

  3. 3.

    For light emitted at origin time t0t_{0}, the lattice clock at distance r is set to t0+r/ct_{0}+r/c on reception. For projectiles, the origin emission and return readings t0t_{0} and t give distance u⁢(t−t0)/2u(t-t_{0})/2 and remote reception time (t+t0)/2(t+t_{0})/2. Projectile calibration presupposes Newton’s first law.

  4. 4.

    After spatial and temporal calibration of the lattice, auxiliary observers associate local clock readings with events at their lattice positions.

  5. 5.

    Using calibrated coordinates, coordinate regularity of finite events, linearity, the relativity principle, isotropy, continuity and invariant light speed, Rindler derives t′=γ⁢(t−v⁢x/c2)t^{\prime}=\gamma(t-vx/c^{2}) in standard configuration, with |v|<c|v|<c and positive γ\gamma.

  6. 6.

  7. 7.

  8. 8.

  10. 10.

  12. 12.

  15. 15.

Results and Evidence-Effect Domain

Rindler applies the standard-clock argument to muon decay, atomic vibration, expansion of charged-particle clusters, and human lifetimes expressed by a comoving clock. The experimental values for muons and airborne clocks are results of external experiments cited by Rindler. For the CERN storage ring, the book gives a Lorentz factor of about 12, precision of about 1%, and accelerations up to about 1019⁢g10^{19}g; it gives about 10% for Hafele–Keating and about 1% for the Maryland experiments of 1975–76 (pp. 44–45).

Result 10 concerns uniformly moving standard clocks, Result 15 concerns accumulated elapsed quantities of ideal clocks including nonuniform-motion segments, and Result 18 concerns twin asymmetry with extended free-fall legs. The instantaneous rate in Result 15 uses the clock hypothesis. Result 18 uses Rindler’s argument that extending the free-fall legs can make the effects of acceleration itself relatively small (pp. 45–47).

Individual Statements

Relativity of Simultaneity

Verbatim Original (English):

Thus simultaneity is relative!

The original-source location is Wolfgang Rindler, Essential Relativity: Special, General, and Cosmological, Revised Second Edition, p. 28, Section 2.4.

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

Rindler explains that a flash emitted at the center of the aircraft reaches its two ends simultaneously in the aircraft frame, whereas it reaches the rear end first in the ground frame.

The attributed author is Wolfgang Rindler in the text of the book.

Reality and Experimental Confirmation of Time Dilation

Verbatim Original (English):

Time dilation, like length contraction, is real. And this has been confirmed experimentally.

The original-source location is p. 44, Section 2.13 of the same book. The minimal context is the paragraph that places this statement immediately after an explanation of the reciprocal difference between readings of synchronized standard clocks fixed in two inertial systems, and then cites cosmic-ray muons and the CERN storage-ring experiment as examples. The attributed author is Wolfgang Rindler in the text of the book.

Application to Physical Processes and Human Lifetimes

Verbatim Original (English):

the time dilation argument applies to any temporal change or process and therefore also to muon decay and even human life times

The original-source location is the opening paragraph on p. 45, near the end of Section 2.13 of the same book. The minimal context raises the objection that a muon is not a clock, then supposes that a standard clock travels with the muon or space traveler and extends the domain of application of the time-dilation argument. The attributed author is Wolfgang Rindler in the text of the book.

Common Evidence with Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

First Statement

Rindler compares the arrival order of light emitted at the aircraft center in the aircraft and ground frames. Invariant light speed and the simultaneity convention in each frame show that reception events simultaneous in the aircraft are not simultaneous on the ground. The corresponding LCTR audit entries are Time Contents 2–4, Claims 15–16 and 21–22, Transfers 1–2, and Results 2–3.

Second Statement

Rindler discusses time dilation of standard clocks and cites cosmic-ray and CERN storage-ring muon results as experimental confirmation. The measured quantity in these examples is the lifetime of moving muons; the connection with standard-clock relations and the clock hypothesis belongs to the book’s theoretical account. The corresponding entries are Time Contents 14–18 and 22, Claims 40–46, and Results 10–12.

Third Statement

Rindler imagines a standard clock accompanying a muon or space traveler and applies the time-dilation argument to temporal changes and processes expressed by that clock. Application to human lifetimes is part of this theoretical generalization using a comoving clock. Result 10 concerns uniform-motion clock relations; Result 13 concerns application to physical processes; Result 15 concerns reunion comparison using nonuniformly moving ideal clocks; and Result 18 concerns asymmetry with long free-fall legs. Claims 40–42, 45, 48 and 50–51 support these distinctions.
