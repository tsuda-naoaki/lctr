Placement of Grounding Obligations

Placement of grounding obligations

The 20 time contents audited in this source are accepted as already formed temporal inputs rather than generated from nontemporal physical inputs before use. The placement determination for their physical-time grounding obligations is backgrounded by primitive-time input. This determination follows by comparing the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation with the absence, before that use, of its corresponding generation or formation condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete determination and residual-root determination concern physical-time admissibility and are distinct from the actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result value or applicability domain. Neither the original-source formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence for this source establishes such an actual failure of a target condition. The actual-failure determination is indeterminate from the original-source evidence, and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream reach

The extent of reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relation and minimal primitive-time-root support reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because no actual failure of a concrete condition has been established, the determination of actual reach from a failure location to a result is indeterminate from the original-source evidence. Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair scope

On the basis of structural reach, the repair targets are the corresponding time contents and the generation or formation conditions required before their use. The recalculation scope is limited to results reached from a failure location when actual failure has independently been established. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domains are restricted. Post-recalculation values are fixed by the audit result.

Individual Statements

Statement 1: Travel Speed and Experienced Time

Original text (English):

the faster you travel, the slower you experience time

Primary-source location: the body paragraph beginning What does this mean for time travel? in NASA Space Place, Is Time Travel Possible?. Minimal context: the explanation for a general audience that connects ordinary temporal passage, relativistic clock differences, GPS corrections, and time-travel vocabulary.

Statement 2: Upper Limit Imposed by the Speed of Light

Original text (English):

nothing can travel faster than the speed of light

Primary-source location: NASA Space Place, Is Time Travel Possible?, in the paragraph explaining relativity. Minimal context: the explanation that states the speed of light as an upper limit on motion and introduces high-speed motion and the rate at which clocks advance.

Common Evidence with a Specified Domain of Application

The two statements are attributed to the text of the NASA Space Place article. For each statement, the original text, primary-source location, and the minimal context required for interpretation are given.

Individual Audit

Travel Speed and Experienced Time

Interpretive possibilities

The first statement’s “experience time” corresponds to the airborne-clock reading difference at reunion and the GPS clock-rate differences explained subsequently in the article. For a traveler, it is expressed by proper elapsed time along the traveler’s worldline and readings of a comoving clock.

Independent grounds

The article connects high-speed motion with the passage of time and gives the example of two clocks starting with the same reading: after an eastward flight, the airborne clock is slightly behind the ground clock.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

The airborne-clock explanation is conditioned on eastward flight and comparison at reunion. The GPS comparison includes both kinematic and gravitational contributions; at the source’s stated altitude and speed, the gravitational contribution dominates.

Corresponding formal evidence

The correspondence is to time contents 8–13, Claims 11–17, Results 6 and 7, and Proposition S.651.

Within-source comparison

The article’s “real time travel” is explanatory language bringing together the airborne-clock reading difference and GPS rate differences and correction. Each comparison uses clock readings, a comparison reference, and temporal scales.

Upper Limit Imposed by the Speed of Light

Interpretive possibilities

The second statement explains the relativistic local upper limit on the propagation of matter, signals, and causal influence: the vacuum speed of light cc, as measured in a local inertial frame.

Independent grounds

The article states the speed-of-light limit in its explanation of relativity and then discusses travel speed and clock comparison.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

The numerical representation of speed uses spatial and temporal differences in a local inertial frame, clocks, a length standard, and a measurement procedure. Emission-before-reception order rests on a positive propagation time between identified emission and reception events.

Corresponding formal evidence

The vacuum speed of light corresponds to time content 7, Claim 10, and Result 5. Propagation time and emission–reception order correspond to time contents 3 and 4, Transfer 1, and Result 3.

Within-source comparison

The telescope explanation concerns receiving light emitted in the past; the speed-of-light explanation concerns a local speed limit. Their temporal representations use intervals or speed scales in addition to the light carrier and spatial path.
