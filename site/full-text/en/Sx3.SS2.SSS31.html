Formal Objects

The audit uses 34 time contents, 54 claims, 11 transfers and 24 results. The tables specify the constructions and conditions of microscopic reversibility, coarse graining, entropy, scale separation, typicality, initial boundaries, reversal and the quantum correspondence. Lebowitz uses Boltzmann’s statistical account to examine the compatibility of time-reversal-symmetric microscopic dynamics with the temporal direction of observed macroscopic nonequilibrium processes. He defines Boltzmann entropy from the macroregion containing an individual microstate and explains macroscopic evolution using scale separation, phase-volume typicality and a special low-entropy initial state. After examining Lorentz-gas diffusion, velocity reversal, preparation and the cosmic initial boundary, and the distinction from Gibbs entropy, he discusses the quantum correspondence and the arrows associated with measurement, psychology and electromagnetism.

Assumptions

The central dynamics is reversible Hamiltonian mechanics for an isolated classical Newtonian system. Macrostates require specified coarse graining and tolerances for particle densities, energy and other quantities. Typicality-based future prediction uses a special initial macroregion and a typical microstate within it. The hydrodynamic description uses many particles and scale separation; exact retracing uses perfect velocity reversal and isolation; Gibbs-entropy constancy uses Liouville volume preservation. The quantum correspondence uses the wavefunction representation, conjugation and unitary evolution specified in the source.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Claims 1–5 are the initial inputs for generation. They provide configurations and phase space, phase volume and coarse graining, boundary-state data, state data for experimenters, apparatus, resources and environment, and quantum-state representations and subspaces. The relations assigning instants, velocities, initial boundaries and evolution to these data are specified in the corresponding claims and transfers.

Evidence Hierarchy

Lebowitz discusses Boltzmann’s statistical account, derivations in models, earlier reversal experiments and numerical simulations, and the correspondence with quantum theory. The source reports zero directly measured objects and zero direct-measurement predicates from measurements of its own.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count and source-evidence-conditioned local reach count are both zero, leaving 34 primitive-time roots. The source explains the orientation of typical macroscopic evolution using given instants, intervals, microscopic evolution and a low-entropy initial boundary.

Result Dependence

Of the 24 results, 18 depend on primitive-time roots and 6 are reachable from the source formal-evidence environment. The latter are Results 1, 3–5, 8, 24, covering the scope, static entropy definitions and relations, the meaning of typicality, and Lebowitz’s assessment. No result is generated from nontemporal physical inputs alone. There are 89 root–result dependence pairs, of which 49 are necessary.

Evidentiary Effect

Lebowitz reconciles reversible microscopic dynamics with macroscopic irreversibility through scale separation, phase-volume typicality and a low-entropy initial boundary. The Lorentz-gas argument has the source’s geometry and scaling-limit conditions; exact velocity reversal requires perfect isolation and equal intervals; Gibbs-entropy constancy requires Liouville volume preservation. The quantum correspondence relates the specified states, evolutions, reversal and macro-subspaces. Explaining measurement through a single theory of system and environment is Lebowitz’s proposal, and the shared origin of psychological and electromagnetic arrows is his view.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Classical instants, velocities and evolution, intervals around reversal, preparation and cosmic boundaries, isolation, relaxation and recurrence timescales, and the temporal argument of quantum evolution are premises of the respective arguments. None of these 34 time contents belongs to the generation closure from nontemporal physical inputs or to the local closure with source formal evidence. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Common Evidence with Delimited Scope

The five statements above specify the original text of Lebowitz 1993, its source location, minimal context, and source attribution.

Individual Audit

First Statement

Lebowitz asks how microscopic time-reversal symmetry is compatible with the temporal direction of nonequilibrium processes observed in individual macroscopic systems. The source explains macroscopic evolution through scale separation associated with many degrees of freedom, phase-volume typicality, and low-entropy preparation and cosmic initial boundaries. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 8, 18, 21, 25, 29, 35, 38 and 40, Transfers 1, 5 and 7–10, and Results 2, 7, 9, 11, 15, 18 and 19.

Second Statement

Lebowitz maps microstates to wavefunctions, velocity reversal to complex conjugation, time evolution to unitary Schrödinger evolution, and macroregion phase volume to the dimension of a quantum subspace. The source states that this correspondence preserves the large-system equilibrium entropy correspondence and time symmetry. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 45–46, Transfer 11 and Result 21.

Third Statement

In 1993, Lebowitz assesses the inconsistency claims known to him as mistaken and regards alternative explanations as unnecessary, on the basis of Boltzmann’s later writings and the criticisms of his account known to Lebowitz. The third statement is Lebowitz’s own assessment arising from that comparison. The corresponding formal evidence consists of Claim 52 and Result 24.

Fourth Statement

In the source, time-reversal-symmetric typicality imposed at an intermediate macrostate gives the same prediction toward higher entropy for equal past and future displacements. Lebowitz suggests that the ordering of the experienced sequence derives from the assumption that it evolved from an initial state of still lower entropy. Microstates arriving from the smaller earlier macroregion occupy an atypical subset of the later region. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 32–33 and 35, Transfer 9 and Results 14–15.

Fifth Statement

Lebowitz states that, in an equilibrium system isolated for much longer than its relaxation time, fluctuations around a local entropy minimum are typically symmetric. He then explains that, when the history before and after the snapshots is unknown, experience of experimental preparation is used to infer that the low-entropy state was initially prepared. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 25 and 34–35, Transfers 7 and 9, and Results 11 and 14–15.
