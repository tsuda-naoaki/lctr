Definition S.411 (Rovelli 2002 Formal Objects and Conditions).

Rovelli distinguishes measurability from predictability and explains partial and complete observables through pendulums, field measurements, relativistic clocks and quantum observables. The pendulum uses the small-oscillation approximation and initial position and velocity; clock relations use a metric, worldlines and initial data. The general mechanical formulation concerns finitely many degrees of freedom and uses complete integrals, regular constraint surfaces, smooth local quotients and domains admitting the corresponding solutions. The quantum formulation uses an appropriate solution space and physical inner product, with probability amplitudes conditional on prior observations; its relation to the action uses a semiclassical approximation.

Evidence Hierarchy

Observable definitions and numerical-correlation examples distinguish measurement from prediction. Pendulum, field and clock examples give relational predictions using their respective initial conditions and time contents. The finite-dimensional constraint formulation describes relation surfaces and histories under regularity and solution-domain conditions. In the quantum formulation, amplitudes conditional on prior observations and the domain of the semiclassical approximation specify the predictions.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count and source-evidence-conditioned local-reach count are both zero, leaving 13 primitive-time roots. Transfers for the pendulum, relativistic clocks and quantum clocks take specified clock time or proper time as inputs. The action’s time parameter and the prior-observation order of quantum predictions also enter the source description as inputs.

Result Dependence

Of the 28 results, 15 depend on primitive-time roots. Thirteen are reached from source formal evidence concerning observable definitions, card correlations, geometric structures, operator correspondences and the general formulation. There are 19 root–result dependence pairs, of which 17 are necessary.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Rovelli’s relational predictions use clock readings, proper time, parameter time or observation order in their respective examples. Within a formulation requiring no single external time as the independent variable, these temporal uses carry the grounding obligation. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Initial data, physical clock arrangements, solution domains and approximation conditions specify the domain of each prediction. Actual failure of a specific physical-time generation condition and the resulting change in predicted values are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Structural reach is determined by 19 root–result dependence pairs. Actual reach from failure of a specific generation condition to a result is undetermined by the source evidence.

Repair Scope

The repair targets are each temporal use and the physical-time generation conditions required before it. If actual failure of a specific condition is established, recalculation concerns the results dependent on it.

Individual Statements

The problem of time measurement and observables

Original text:

Therefore time cannot be an observable [2]. But if time is not an observable, how can we measure it?

Source location: Section I. Rovelli introduces the tension between the argument concerning a self-adjoint time operator in ordinary quantum theory and measurement by clocks, then develops the distinction between partial and complete observables.

Clock readings and conditional prediction

Original text:

The theory cannot predict the value of t.

Source location: Section II. Rovelli treats pendulum position qq and clock reading tt as partial observables and explains that, given initial data, the theory predicts qq corresponding to tt.

Relational prediction between partial observables

Original text:

Observe that the predictions of a mechanical theory can always be expressed as relations between partial observables.

Source location: Section II. Rovelli presents the relation between pendulum position and time and states the general claim that mechanical predictions can be expressed as relations between partial observables.

A mechanical formulation without selecting external time

Original text:

There is no need to single out a specific partial variable as the time, nor to mention evolution. Mechanics is a theory of relations between partial observables. No distinction between dependent and independent partial observables is required. This distinction is an accident of non-relativistic theories, in which the constraint C⁢(qa,pa)=0C(q^{a}\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak p_{a})=0 happens to have the form (12).

Individual Audit

Distinction between measurement and prediction

In the first statement, Rovelli presents the tension between an operator representation of time and measurement by clocks. In the source distinction, clock readings are partial observables, while quantities predicted from a specified state are complete observables. A quantum-clock operator represents the relation of a physical clock to another clock. This distinction addresses the introductory question. Claims 4 and 19–26 and Results 1 and 13–17 correspond to it.

Prediction at a specified clock reading

The second statement says that the pendulum theory predicts the position corresponding to a clock reading rather than selecting the observed clock reading tt itself. The small-oscillation approximation and initial position and velocity determine q⁢(t)q(t). The inverse correspondence from pendulum position has periodicity and branch conditions. Clock time is an input to this prediction. Claims 7–8 and Result 4 correspond to the statement.

Prediction supplied by a relation surface

In the third statement, Rovelli expresses mechanical predictions as relations between partial observables. In the card example, a numerical correlation gives a conditional prediction; in the pendulum, Maxwell-field and relativistic-clock examples, temporal locations or clock readings enter the respective relations. A relation surface on extended configuration space determines combinations of values compatible with a specified state. Claims 6, 8–11, 16–18 and 28–30 and their corresponding results provide the examples and formulation of this general claim.

The general formulation and predictions using time

The fourth statement describes finite-dimensional mechanics by the pair (𝒞,C)(\mathcal{C}\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak C), giving relational predictions without choosing one external time as the independent variable. Characteristic curves of the constraint surface and their quotient are constructed geometrically; the source introduces a time parameter when explaining the action. Relativistic clock relations use proper clocks, and the predictive interpretation of quantum amplitudes uses prior-observation order. Claims 27–38 and Results 18–28 give the general formulation and the conditions of the predictions.
