Assumptions

Francis and colleagues note the absence of a single agreed meaning of expanding space and adopt the increase over time of separation between comoving observers as a working definition. They use the cosmological principle, FRW geometry, a comoving congruence and a common zero for its clocks. Local bound systems, the empty Milne model, released tethered particles, supported bodies and free-particle sequences each have their own matter, initial and support conditions. Distance denotes proper distance on the specified cosmic-time slice; peculiar velocity is relative to the local comoving frame. Light propagation specifies emission and reception events and the endpoint observers.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

The carriers, spatial configurations, distance and density records and signal media, and origin and endpoint markers in Claims 1–14 form the generation-initial set.

Individual statements

Statement objects

Synchronization of the clocks of comoving observers, the expanding-space explanation, the departure-time interval of a sequence of freely falling particles, and the local application of expansion on galactic scales.

Original text (English)

the watches of all privileged observers are synchronised

more space appeared, or ‘welled up’ between the two observers

the first particle leaving at time t0t_{0} and the last at t0+Δ⁢t0t_{0}+\Delta t_{0}

There is no expansion for the galaxy to overcome

Original-source location

The first and second quotations are in Section 2.1, p. 97; the third is in Section 2.6.2, p. 100; the fourth is in Section 2.6.3, p. 101.

Minimal context

Section 2.1 introduces the comoving congruence and cosmic time in the FRW model, states the synchronization of comoving clocks, and explains increasing proper separation between observers in terms of expanding space. Section 2.6.2 calculates a length ratio from departure and arrival intervals of a free-particle sequence without internal forces. Section 2.6.3 states that the applicability conditions of the purely homogeneous and isotropic FRW metric are not met on galactic scales and rejects a picture of gravity and expansion as opposing forces.

Attribution

The four statements are attributed to the main text by Matthew J. Francis, Luke A. Barnes, J. Berian James, and Geraint F. Lewis.

What the quotations state directly

The first quotation states that the clocks of all privileged observers are synchronized. The second explains that space appears or wells up between observers. The third assigns different departure times to the first and last particles. The fourth denies expansion as something for a galaxy to overcome.

Explicit applicability domain

The first and second quotations concern the comoving-observer congruence of the FRW model, the third concerns the specified finite sequence of particles, and the fourth concerns the local metric on galactic scales.

Individual Audit

Comoving-Clock Synchronization

The clocks synchronized by Francis and colleagues in the first quotation belong to the specified comoving congruence of the FRW model. Along a comoving worldline, d⁢τ/d⁢t=1d\tau/dt=1 gives τ=t+b\tau=t+b. Selecting a common zero for the clock family makes the readings agree on the same cosmic-time slice. The synchronization relation therefore depends on the comoving congruence, clock readings and zero. It corresponds to Time Contents 1,3,6,7, Claims 21–22, Transfers 1–2 and Result 5.

The Explanation That Space Appears

The object explained by the authors in the second quotation is the proper separation of observers with fixed comoving coordinates. An increasing scale factor increases the proper distance rp=R⁢χr_{\mathrm{p}}=R\chi and the proper volume of a comoving region. The authors describe this metric relation by saying that space appears. It corresponds to Time Contents 1–3,8, Claims 15,23–24, Transfer 3 and Results 1,6.

Departure Interval of a Free-Particle Sequence

The third quotation specifies the departure times of the first and last particles as t0t_{0} and t0+Δ⁢t0t_{0}+\Delta t_{0}. Equations (8)–(11) use a common integration constant CC to compare departure and arrival intervals, obtaining lf/l0=Rf/R0l_{\mathrm{f}}/l_{0}=R_{\mathrm{f}}/R_{0} to first order in the intervals. For a particle family whose CC varies with launch time, differentiating the propagation integral adds a term involving d⁢C/d⁢t0dC/dt_{0}; the stated length ratio therefore requires the common-CC condition. Departure and arrival times and the passage intervals are temporal inputs to the calculation. This corresponds to Time Contents 2,9,11,17–18,23, Claims 44–48, Transfers 11–14 and Results 20–22.

Local Metric on Galactic Scales

In the fourth quotation, the authors reject applying the pure FRW metric to a galaxy with concentrated matter and treating expansion and gravity as independent competing forces. Their domain argument assigns the motion to the local metric and binding conditions and distinguishes supported objects from free-particle sequences without internal forces. Temporal evolution of the local metric and bound state remains an input to this account. It corresponds to Time Contents 2,16,21, Claims 28,43,49–50 and Results 9,19,23.
