Assumptions

The mean-rate method uses the mean clock rates before and after flight and assumes a single clock-rate change at the flight midpoint. The correlated-clock-rate-change method uses the independence of individual clock-rate changes and attributes a change to a clock from slope changes of the same size and direction at the same instant in comparisons among three or more clocks. Further premises concern the greater stability of the long-term clock-ensemble mean rate than that of an individual clock, cancellation of common relativistic and environmental effects in internal differences, environmental sensitivity, the statistical limitations of the small sample, and the route-dependent prediction model.

Grounding Grounds

Grounding Grounds of the Results

The experimental and statistical grounds comprise the eastward and westward flights of four portable cesium clocks, comparison of their readings with MEAN(USNO), more than 5,000 pairwise clock-difference records, least-squares fitting before and after flight, correlated-clock-rate-change analysis, the individual values in Table 1, clock-ensemble statistics, and environmental-sensitivity evaluation.

Evidence Hierarchy

Direct Measurement Targets

The readings of the four flying cesium clocks, their reading differences relative to MEAN(USNO), and the reading differences among the flying clocks.

Direct Measurement Predicates

How much the readings of the compared clocks differ at each recorded measurement time.

Conditions and Domain of Direct Measurement

The domain comprises the eastward and westward flights of the four clocks, comparisons with MEAN(USNO) before and after flight, internal comparisons before, during, and after flight, and more than 5,000 difference records over 636 hours. The differences were measured to the nearest nanosecond with an electronic time-interval counter, with measurement times uncertain by about ±5\pm 5 minutes.

Targets of Formal Generalization

Relative clock rates and their change histories are inferred from pairwise clock-difference trajectories. Extrapolation anchored by comparisons before and after flight yields the final direction-specific reading deviations, and the clock-ensemble means are compared with route-dependent relativistic predictions. The aging of human or nonhuman organisms is neither a direct measurement target nor a target of formal generalization in this reference.

Conditions and Domain of Formal Generalization

The mean-rate method uses 25-hour fits before and after flight and assumes a single clock-rate change at the midpoint. The correlated-clock-rate-change method uses the independence of individual clock-rate changes and inductive attribution from slope changes of the same size and direction at the same instant among three or more clocks. Piecewise extrapolation accounting for the attributed changes yields the values in Table 1. The domain is fixed by the flight conditions shared by the four clocks, comparisons with MEAN(USNO) before and after flight, common-mode cancellation in internal differences, clock-rate sensitivity of 0.4⁢ns/h0.4\,\mathrm{ns/h}, environmental-test conditions, the statistical limitations of the small sample, and the route-dependent prediction model. All twelve results use one or more time contents as dependent inputs.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The 106 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already-established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment follows by matching the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation against the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or validity condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

For this reference, the reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from that failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Individual Statements

Objects of the Statements

Empirical resolution of the clock paradox with macroscopic clocks; the relation between experimental facts and theoretical arguments; unpredictable rate changes in real clocks; selection of an elapsed-time axis; agreement between predicted and measured values; and statistical constraints imposed by a small sample.

Verbatim Original (English)

These results provide an unambiguous empirical resolution of the famous clock ”paradox” with macroscopic clocks.

In science, relevant experimental facts supersede theoretical arguments.

These unpredictable changes in rate produce the major uncertainty in our results.

The axis of abscissas in Fig. 1 is the running time for the data period, and, because at any given instant all clocks involved read the same time to within much better than the nearest second, it is immaterial whether the running time is the time of MEAN(USNO) or any member of the flying ensemble.

The agreement between the mean of the measured values and the predicted values in Table 1 is very satisfactory.

However, the number of measured values is too small for a good statistical analysis.

Original-Source Locations

The first quotation is from the abstract of Hafele–Keating, Around-the-World Atomic Clocks: Observed Relativistic Time Gains, Science 177, p. 168. The second is from the opening paragraph on p. 168; the third, from the clock-stability paragraph on p. 168; the fourth, from the paragraph explaining Figure 1 on p. 169; and the fifth and sixth, from the discussion following Table 1 on p. 170.

Minimal Context

The first and second quotations belong to the abstract and introduction of the experimental report in which four portable cesium clocks were flown eastward and westward and their reading differences after return relative to MEAN(USNO) were compared with relativistic predictions. The third belongs to the explanation that rates of real clocks change irregularly, requiring analysis of difference data and evaluation of uncertainty. The fourth belongs to the explanation of Figure 1, which plots pairwise clock differences against the elapsed time of the data period under conditions in which continuous comparison with an external reference during flight was unavailable. The fifth and sixth belong to the same discussion: immediately after evaluating agreement between the observed mean and the predictions in Table 1, the text states the limitation imposed by the number of samples.

Attribution

All six statements are attributable to the abstract or main text by J. C. Hafele and Richard E. Keating.

Contents Directly Stated by the Quotations

The first states an empirical resolution of the clock paradox with macroscopic clocks. The second states that relevant experimental facts supersede theoretical arguments. The third identifies unpredictable clock-rate changes as the major uncertainty. The fourth states that either MEAN(USNO) time or the time of any clock in the flying ensemble may serve as the elapsed-time axis. The fifth evaluates agreement between the measured mean and the predicted values; the sixth limits that evaluation by stating that the number of measured values is too small for a good statistical analysis.

Explicit Domain of Application

The explicit domain of the six quotations comprises the four portable cesium clocks; eastward and westward flight; comparison with MEAN(USNO); pairwise clock-difference data; clock-rate-change analysis; the measured and predicted values in Table 1; and the statistical limitations of four samples.

Evidence Provenance

The quotations are from the published article in Science 177, pp. 168–170.

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first statement describes agreement between relativistic predictions and the post-reunion reading differences of macroscopic clocks flown eastward and westward relative to the ground reference as an empirical resolution of the clock paradox. The second is a methodological claim that experimental facts take precedence over theoretical arguments. The third is a measurement-theoretic explanation that treats unpredictable rate changes in real clocks as the principal uncertainty in the results.
