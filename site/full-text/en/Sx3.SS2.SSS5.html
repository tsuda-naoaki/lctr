The original-source formal evidence and LCTR audit evidence for this reference do not establish the actual invalidation of such a target condition. Original-Source Location

In Einstein, Die Grundlage der allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie, Annalen der Physik, Vierte Folge, Band 49, the first quotation is on p. 770, §1; the second on p. 773, §2; the third and fourth on p. 775, §3; and the fifth in equations (68a)–(69) on p. 818, §21.

Minimal Context

Section 1 of the original source presents the constancy of the vacuum speed of light as a postulate distinguishing special relativity from classical mechanics, and connects it to the relativity of simultaneity, the Lorentz transformation, and the laws governing moving rigid bodies and clocks. Section 2 states that the same principle requires modification in an accelerated coordinate system and that a light path is generally curved. Section 3 places clocks of identical construction at the center and on the circumference of a disk at rest in the rotating coordinate system, and compares the clock indications received at the center. The attribution of the difference to clock rates is conditional on not taking the light speed on the path considered to be explicitly time-dependent. A time meeting physical requirements cannot be introduced through the indications of identically constructed clocks at rest in the rotating frame; a definition of time allowing their rates to depend on position is required. Section 21 explicitly gives the selected unit of time used in comparison with the Newtonian limit, and connects the original notation KK to the field coupling ϰ\varkappa in equation (69).

Attributing Source

The five statements are attributable to §§1–3 and 21 of Einstein’s original German paper of 1916.

Predicates

The first quotation states the constancy of the vacuum speed of light as a postulate of special relativity. The second states that the same principle requires modification in an accelerated-coordinate description. The third states that one cannot introduce a time corresponding to physical requirements that is indicated by clocks of identical construction at rest in the rotating coordinate system. The fourth assigns to the clock placed on the circumference a lower rate of progress in comparison with the clock at the center. Under the selected unit of time, the fifth quotation denotes the ordinary gravitational constant by the original notation KK and gives its relation to the field coupling ϰ\varkappa.

Explicit Domains of Application

The domain of the first quotation is special relativity in §1 of the original source; the domain of the second is the accelerated-coordinate description in §2. The domain of the third and fourth quotations is the rotating coordinate system in §3, the clocks of identical construction at the center and on the circumference of the disk, and the comparison of clock indications received at the center. The domain of the fifth quotation is the weak-field, low-velocity, and quasi-static approximation in §21, together with the comparison with Newtonian theory using the unit of time selected there.

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first and second quotations distinguish the special-relativistic vacuum-light-speed postulate from coordinate light speed that varies in accelerated-coordinate descriptions. The third and fourth concern indications and rates of identically constructed clocks at rest in the rotating frame. The central observer’s attribution to clock rate is conditional on not treating light speed on the considered path as explicitly time-dependent. The fifth connects the physical role of gravitational coupling to its numerical representation in the selected time unit. Using these clock indications in a construction of time requires concrete recording, comparison and transport relations, together with loop consistency and gluing conditions over any multiple paths or regions involved.

Independent Grounds

The first and second statements rest on the special-relativistic postulate and accelerated-coordinate relations; the third and fourth, on the rotating-frame clock comparison; and the fifth, on coefficient comparison in the Newtonian limit. These are theoretical statements in the source, distinct in evidence type from the connection to spectral observations discussed in §22.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first and second quotations concern the light-speed postulate and coordinate descriptions; the third and fourth, clock indications and rates; and the fifth, time units and coupling constants. The time variable in a speed representation, clock indications and the time unit are prerequisites of these relations. Attribution of the physical responses of clocks or oscillators to property changes of time itself independently of those bearers, and physical-time qualification of these temporal inputs, lie beyond their evidence-effect boundary under Definition 7.115.
