Individual statements

Statement objects

The source reports on the existence and passage of time; the priority of proper time; the common time parameter supplied by clocks; the relative flow of time between worldlines; the time parameter and proper time on fundamental flow lines; the rate of temporal passage stated as one second per second; the connection of time to physics, astronomy, geology, technology, and biology; the preferred time parameter in standard physics; and the emergence of effective time from a timeless substratum.

Original text (English)
  1. 1.

    I argue that (i) time both exists and flows;

  2. 2.

    What really matters is proper time τ\tau measured along timelines xi⁢(v)x^{i}(v), determined from the metric tensor gi⁢j⁢(xk)g_{ij}(x^{k}) by the basic formula [49, 34]

  3. 3.

    The whole edifice of physics is built on the assumption that we can build such clocks to a good approximation, giving a time parameter τ\tau that appears equally in all dynamical equations of physics: Newton’s equation of motion [36], Maxwell’s equations [37], the Schrödinger equation [38], General Relativity expressed in a 1+3 covariant formalism [21], and so on;

  4. 4.

    The relative flow of time along different world lines may be different: that is the phenomenon of time dilation, caused by the varying gravitational potentials represented by the metric tensor [78]. But this does not mean it is not well defined along each world line.

  5. 5.

    We determine the lapse function N⁢(xi)N(x^{i}) by the condition that the time parameter tt measures proper time τ\tau along the fundamental flow lines.

  6. 6.

    Conclusion: time flows at the rate of one second per second, with the metric tensor determining what this rate is for clocks and every other physical system (the choice of units is of course arbitrary, but can be done in a way that makes sense).

  7. 7.

    The usual choice as in (40), agreeing with the proposal made here in §(3), ties time τ\tau in to all the rest of physics, astronomy, geology, technology, and biology.

  8. 8.

    In conclusion: Standard physics is based in choice of a preferred time parameter τ\tau along matter world lines. General Relativity both allows such a choice, and can itself be written in terms of that choice.

  9. 9.

    There are a number of proposals for an effective time to emerge somehow in the context of a timeless substratum.

Minimal contexts

The first quotation summarizes Ellis’s central claim that time exists and flows. The second belongs to the context in which proper time along a worldline is determined from the metric in place of distant simultaneity. The third moves from the assumption that ideal clocks can be constructed approximately to a common τ\tau across multiple physical equations. The fourth belongs to the context in which differences in proper time between worldlines and differences in gravitational potential are called time dilation. The fifth belongs to the selection of fundamental flow lines and the alignment of their coordinate parameter with proper time. The sixth concludes a rate of temporal passage from correlations among the metric, clock readings, and particle motion. The seventh and eighth connect the selection of a proper-time coordinate to general relativity and temporal descriptions in other fields. The ninth introduces the ensuing examination of several proposals that posit a timeless substratum.

Agents of attribution

Statements 1–8 are attributed to the position taken by G. F. R. Ellis in his 2012 paper. Statement 9 is Ellis’s introduction to existing proposals; the following sections address Mott-type, thermal-time and Wheeler–DeWitt-based proposals.

Predicates and explicit domains of application

The first quotation attributes existence and passage to time. The second gives priority to worldline proper time determined from the metric. The third extends the common domain of application of the time parameter supplied by clocks to Newtonian mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and general relativity. The fourth describes differences between worldlines as a relative flow of time. The fifth states that tt measures τ\tau along the fundamental flow lines. The sixth takes clocks and every other physical system as the objects of the rate of temporal passage. The seventh explicitly takes the domain from physics through biology. The eighth explicitly takes standard physics and general relativity as its domain. The ninth reports effective time emerging from a timeless substratum as its object.

Individual audit

Interpretive possibilities

Proper time in Quotation 2 is a quantity determined by the metric and a world line; in Quotation 3 it serves as a time parameter linking clock readings and several evolution equations. Quotations 5 and 8 concern selection of a fundamental congruence and coordinate time. Quotations 1 and 6 express Ellis’s position on the reality and passage of time, while Quotation 9 introduces proposals for effective time.

Independent grounds

The corresponding grounds are the proper-time integral in Eq. (20), the fundamental congruence in Eq. (21), the clock-rate, ADM and matter equations (24)–(37), the reparameterization and FLRW time relations (38)–(41), and the comparison of proposals in §5.

Boundary of evidential force

Quotation 4 concerns relative passage of time on different world lines; Quotation 7 connects a proper-time coordinate to physics, astronomy, geology, technology and biology. The proper-time integral uses a metric and curve; present surfaces use a selected congruence and common origin; lapse–shift decomposition uses a foliation and a flow with a specified scale. These uses of time and Ellis’s claims about its reality and passage constitute the argument of his paper.
