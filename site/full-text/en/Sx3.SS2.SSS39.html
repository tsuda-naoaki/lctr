Definition S.366 (Riess et al. 1998 Formal Objects and Conditions).

Riess et al. use time-indexed photometry and spectroscopy of Type Ia supernovae to obtain distances through MLCS, decline-rate templates and the snapshot method, with K corrections and treatment of extinction. They specify training, samples, calibration and errors for each method and fit the distance–redshift relation to an FRW model containing matter, curvature and a cosmological constant. The cosmological analysis adopts nonnegative matter density and an expanding big-bang branch, with Gaussian distance errors, independence apart from systematics, a likelihood and a prior domain. Present acceleration, future evolution and cosmic age are obtained from their respective model relations and the inferred parameter distribution.

Evidence Hierarchy

Time-indexed photometry and spectroscopy form the observational material. Riess et al. infer supernova phases and distances through object identification, calibration, K corrections and the MLCS, template and snapshot methods. Under the specified errors, samples and prior domain, they fit an FRW model to the distance–redshift relation to obtain distributions of matter density, the cosmological constant and the Hubble constant. These distributions and FRW temporal-evolution relations support inferences about present acceleration, cosmic age and future expansion.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count and source-evidence-conditioned local-reach count are both zero, leaving 16 primitive-time roots. Transfers to light-curve phase, decline and width and to model acceleration, age and future evolution take observation epochs, template time, cosmic time or Hubble rate as inputs.

Result Dependence

Of the 24 results, 24 depend on primitive-time roots. There are 114 root–result dependence pairs, of which 114 are necessary.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Riess et al. use observation epochs, light-curve phases and widths, astronomical velocities, propagation, expansion history and ages in the analysis. Temporal-width conversions and model calculations take existing time contents as inputs, leaving all 16 as primitive-time roots. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Sample selection, calibration, corrections, covariance, errors, prior domain and model family specify the conditions of each result. The source tests changes in estimates and robustness under variations of these conditions. Actual failure of a specific audited physical-time generation condition and the resulting change in result values are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Structural reach is determined by 114 root–result dependence pairs. Actual reach from failure of a specific generation condition to a result is undetermined by the source evidence.

Repair Scope

The repair targets are each temporal use and the physical-time generation conditions required before that use. If actual failure of a specific condition is established, recalculation concerns the results dependent on it.

Individual Statements

Model-conditional excess distance

Original text:

The distances of the high-redshift SNe Ia are, on average, 10% to 15% farther than expected in a low mass density (ΩM=0.2\Omega_{M}=0.2) Universe without a cosmological constant.

Source location: Abstract. Riess et al. compare high-redshift supernova distances obtained by two methods using light-curve shape with the expectation for a low-matter-density model without a cosmological constant.

Principal sources of uncertainty in supernova cosmology

Original text:

As the use of SNe Ia for measuring ΩM\Omega_{M} and ΩΛ\Omega_{\Lambda} progresses from its infancy into childhood, we can expect a similar shift in the discussion from results limited principally by statistical errors to those limited by our depth of understanding of SNe Ia.

Source location: Section 1. In discussing the development of supernova cosmology, Riess et al. explain the growing importance of photometric calibration, uniform treatment of nearby and distant samples, extinction and understanding of supernova physics as samples increase.

Difference between two fitting methods

Original text:

Until then, we must consider the difference between the cosmological constraints reached from the two fitting methods to be a systematic uncertainty.

Source location: Section 5.6. Riess et al. compare the scatter and mean difference of MLCS and template distances for the same high-redshift sample and retain the difference as a systematic uncertainty until future samples can distinguish the methods.

Individual Audit

Comparison model and inferred distances

The first statement is Riess et al.’s inference that their estimated high-redshift supernova distances exceed the comparison model with ΩM=0.2\Omega_{M}=0.2 and Λ=0\Lambda=0 by 10%–15% on average. Its ground is the distance–redshift relation obtained from time-indexed photometry and spectroscopy, nearby calibration, phase-dependent corrections and two distance-fitting methods. The adopted samples, calibration, corrections and FRW comparison model define the domain of this excess distance. Claims 17–22 and Results 7–9 correspond to the statement.

Supernova understanding and the error budget

The second statement is Riess et al.’s expectation of a shift in the principal limiting factors as supernova cosmology develops. Its ground is the relation among sample size, photometric precision, calibration, extinction and supernova physics discussed in Section 1. Understanding of supernova and population evolution, observation plans and calibration conditions this expectation and the assessment of systematics. Claims 31–37 and Results 16–22 correspond to the statement.

Fitting-method differences for a shared sample

The third statement gives Riess et al.’s requirement to treat the difference between MLCS and template cosmological constraints as a systematic uncertainty. Its ground is the mean distance difference and scatter for the same high-redshift sample and their propagation into cosmological constraints. The difference is retained as an uncertainty under the shared-object and calibration conditions and until future samples distinguish the methods. Claim 24 and Result 15 correspond to the statement.
