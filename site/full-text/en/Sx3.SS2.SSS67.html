Formal Objects

The audit uses 14 time contents, 33 physical and formal claims, 9 transfers and 19 results.

Assumptions

Pössel treats two stations drifting freely far from large masses as inertial frames and applies the two postulates of special relativity. The mirror separation is constant in each clock’s rest frame and perpendicular to relative motion. Good clocks on each station have beats corresponding to its light clock. Comparisons use event times in the respective inertial frames.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

The physical carriers of the mirrors, pulse, detector, counter and stations, and the spatial arrangement and path in Claims 1–9 form the generation-initial set. Motion, arrival order, clock readings and signal delays belong to the time contents of the claims that use them.

Individual Statements

Reading Rate of a Moving Light Clock

Original text (English):

Apparently, from my point of view, the moving light clock moves much slower than my own identical light clock:

The primary-source location is the paragraph immediately following the animation comparing two light clocks in Pössel, From Light Clocks to Time Dilation. The article explains that the one interval delimited by two moving-clock flashes corresponds to the two intervals delimited by three rest-clock flashes. The statement is attributed to the text of Markus Pössel’s article.

Generalization to All Clocks on the Moving Space Station

Original text (English):

Judged from my space station all clocks of the space station moved relative to me run slower than my own clocks.

The primary-source location is the paragraph following the comparison of the two light clocks in the conclusion of the same article. The minimal context is the explanation that treats light clocks as only one example of the more general special-relativistic time dilation and relates the beats of all clocks at rest on each space station to the beats of its light clock. The statement is attributed to the text of Markus Pössel’s article.

Generalization to All Processes on the Moving Space Station

Original text (English):

Just as the moving clocks run slower, all processes on the other space station also run slower for me – five-minute eggs take longer to cook and still have the right consistency in the end, and the pianist on board the other station who plays the Minute Waltz takes considerably more time to do so than is normal performance practice.

The primary-source location is the same concluding paragraph that generalizes to all clocks. The minimal context is the explanation that, after the reading-rate relation for moving clocks, gives cooking a five-minute egg and performing the Minute Waltz as examples of processes on the other space station. The statement is attributed to the text of Markus Pössel’s article.

Common Evidence with a Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Reading Rate of a Moving Light Clock

Interpretive possibilities

The first statement concerns the reading rates of two identically constructed light clocks compared using event times in the observer’s inertial frame.

Independent grounds

Transverse mirror separation, constant relative velocity and light-speed invariance give a longer round-trip interval and a lower reading rate for the moving clock. Two and three flashes delimit one and two intervals respectively.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

The comparison concerns upper-arrival events of the two light clocks, using their intervals and reading rates in the inertial frame. The speed–interval relation is given in Definition S.658.

Corresponding formal evidence

Time contents 1–7 in Definition S.655, Claims 20–23 in Definition S.656, and Results 8–10 in Definition S.660.

All Clocks on the Moving Space Station

Interpretive possibilities

The second statement relates good clocks at rest on each station to its light clock and compares the reading rates of all clocks on the other station in the observer’s inertial frame.

Independent grounds

The grounds are the correspondence between each station’s clocks and its light clock, and the rate relation between the two frames’ light clocks.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

Every comoving good clock satisfying the article’s correspondence conditions has the same comparative rate, irrespective of its mechanism.

Corresponding formal evidence

Time contents 4, 7–8 in Definition S.655, Claims 19, 22, 24 in Definition S.656, and Results 10–11 in Definition S.660.

All Processes on the Moving Space Station

Interpretive possibilities

The third statement compares processes on the other station as described in their comoving frame and in the observer’s inertial frame.

Independent grounds

Pössel generalizes the rate correspondence with clocks to all processes and gives egg cooking and piano performance as examples.

Boundary of evidential force under each interpretive possibility

Expressing the duration of the same comoving process in the comparison frame’s time gives the same interval ratio as the light clock. The local physical laws and apparatus and material conditions are those of that process in its comoving frame.

Corresponding formal evidence

Time contents 7–9 in Definition S.655, Claims 24–25 in Definition S.656, and Results 11–12 in Definition S.660.

Common Audit with a Specified Domain of Application

The three statements successively concern the rate relations of light clocks, comoving good clocks and comoving physical processes under the same inertial-frame comparison conditions. Light speed, event times, tick intervals and rates supply the temporal premises of the respective derivations.
