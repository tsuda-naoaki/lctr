Definition S.571.

Evidential hierarchy

The source presents NIST time-standard operations, the 1971 airborne atomic-clock comparison, and astronomers’ observations of accelerated expansion. It reports zero new quantitative measurements performed by the program itself. Results 1–4 and 6–11 are operational or theoretical explanations; Result 5 is a secondary account of an existing experiment. Result 12 comprises cosmic-age and accelerated-expansion explanations and model-dependent far-future scenarios; Result 13 is conditional on the existence of a wormhole. The equal reality of past, present, and future, discussed after the geometric account of simultaneity, is an interpretation advanced in the program; its conclusion states that they “may all exist on equal footing” (lines 416–417). The low-entropy-boundary account of the arrow of time is presented as the source’s best understanding, while the origin of that boundary remains unresolved (lines 371–382). The far-future scenario is qualified by “one theory suggests” (line 405), and the feasibility of past-directed travel remains unresolved (lines 279–282).

Evidential force

The evidence concerns clock realization, remote synchronization, relativistic clock/worldline comparison, relativity of simultaneity, the statistical arrow of time, and conditional accounts of cosmic history and past-directed travel. The building example compares clock rates at the specified positions and worldlines. The black-hole example treats remotely received signals and elapsed quantities along a round-trip worldline under their respective comparison conditions. Entropy evolution uses temporal ordering and an initial boundary; cosmic age and far-future scenarios depend on observational input and the adopted cosmological model.

Result-preservation domain

The 13 results are preserved insofar as each time-content premise is generated through the LCTR generation sequence and the preservation conditions in Table 539 and the source-specific realization, comparison, and cosmological-model conditions are satisfied.

Placement of grounding obligations

Placement of grounding obligations

The 17 time contents audited in this source are accepted as already formed temporal inputs rather than generated from nontemporal physical inputs before use. The placement determination for their physical-time grounding obligations is backgrounded by primitive-time input. This determination follows by comparing the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation with the absence, before that use, of its corresponding generation or formation condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of actual failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete determination and residual-root determination concern physical-time admissibility and are distinct from the actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result value or applicability domain. Neither the original-source formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence for this source establishes such an actual failure of a target condition. The actual-failure determination is indeterminate from the original-source evidence, and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream reach

Downstream reach

The extent of reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relation and minimal primitive-time-root support reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because no actual failure of a concrete condition has been established, the determination of actual reach from a failure location to a result is indeterminate from the original-source evidence. Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair scope

Repair scope

On the basis of structural reach, the repair targets are the corresponding time contents and the generation or formation conditions required before their use. The recalculation scope is limited to results reached from a failure location when actual failure has independently been established. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domains are restricted. Post-recalculation values are fixed by the audit result.

Audit conclusions

Definition S.572 (Finite dependency graph of the Greene source).

The finite dependency graph of source RR comprises 21 semantic components, 17 time contents, 10 time-content transfers, and 13 results. It has 51 nodes and 23 transfer and result edges in total.

Proposition S.573 (Complete audit of time use in the Greene source).

The numbers of time contents generated by the complete determination and reached by the local-reach determination are both zero. All 17 time contents remain primitive-time roots, and each of the 13 results depends on at least one of them.

Proof.

Each of the ten transfers in Table 538 and thirteen results in Table 539 takes at least one time content as input. Even after adding the source formal-evidence environment to the nontemporal initial set, no edge can output the first time content. Time-generation reach is therefore empty, and each result’s time premises yield its dependence on primitive-time roots. ∎

Proposition S.574 (Minimal primitive-time-root support in the Greene source).

Table 540 gives the minimal primitive-time-root support for the 13 results. There are 34 family members in total.

Proof.

Updating the support antichain to a fixed point over the 10 transfer edges and 13 result edges yields the 34 family members in the table. ∎

Individual statements

Statement evidence 1

Original text (English):

It turns out time itself can speed up or slow down.

The source location is line 35 of the official PBS subtitles. The minimal context is the opening introduction of the concept of time. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is Brian Greene.

The object of the statement is the speeding up and slowing down of “time itself,” explained in the program through relativistic comparisons of clocks and physical processes. The predicate is that time itself can speed up or slow down. The explicit domain of application is the program’s opening general account introducing relativistic differences in clock rates.

Statement evidence 2

Original text (English):

That is, Einstein realized that if a stationary observer carefully analyzes what he sees, he’ll find that time itself elapses more slowly for me than it does for him.

The source location is line 137 of the official PBS subtitles. The minimal context is the account of relative motion and clock-rate comparison. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is Brian Greene.

The statement object is the passage of time for a stationary observer and for the relatively moving speaker as compared by the stationary observer. The predicate is that the observer finds time itself to elapse more slowly for the speaker than for the stationary observer. The explicit domain of application comprises the relative motion, inter-observer comparison, and clock-rate difference exemplified by the program.

Statement evidence 3

Original text (English):

It’s as if gravity can pull on time, slowing its passage.

The source location is line 244 of the official PBS subtitles. The minimal context is the explicitly figurative account of gravity and clock-rate differences. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is Brian Greene.

The statement object is an explicit figure for the relation between gravity and the passage of time. The predicate is that it is as if gravity pulls on time and slows its passage. The explicit domain of application is the program’s account of clock-rate differences induced by differences in gravitational field.

Statement evidence 4

Original text (English):

Compared to someone living on the top floor of a skyscraper, someone living on the bottom experiences time elapsing a little slower because gravity is just a tiny bit stronger closer to the ground.

The source location is line 247 of the official PBS subtitles. The minimal context is the application of the account of differences in gravitational field to people at different heights in a building. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is Brian Greene.

The statement object is a comparison of the passage of time for people living on the top and bottom floors of a skyscraper. The predicate is that the person on the bottom floor, where gravity is slightly stronger, experiences time as elapsing slightly more slowly. The explicit domain of application is the weak difference in gravitational field corresponding to a difference in height within a building and the comparison of people located there.

Statement evidence 5

Original text (English):

You near that black hole will appear to your friend far away to be moving slowly, talking slowly, biologically aging slowly.

The source location is line 251 of the official PBS subtitles. The minimal context is the extension of the comparison between locations near and far from a black hole to biological aging. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is Janna Levin.

The statement objects are the motion, speech, and biological aging of a person near a black hole as observed by a distant friend. The predicate is that the nearby person appears to the distant friend to move slowly, speak slowly, and age biologically slowly. The explicit domain of application comprises the two worldlines near and far from the black hole and remote observation between them.

Individual audits

The speeding up and slowing down of time itself

The first statement previews the later account of differences in elapsed quantities for clocks and physical processes under relative motion and gravity. Its independent grounds are clock comparisons between relatively moving observers, the account of the 1971 airborne atomic-clock experiment, and worldline comparisons in gravity. Its evidential force concerns differences in elapsed-time rates under the respective worldline, synchronization, and signal-comparison conditions. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Time Contents 5–8, 11, and 12, Transfers 4, 5, 8, and 9, and Results 4, 5, 7, and 8.

Elapsed-time-rate comparison between relatively moving observers

The second statement is conditional on a stationary observer carefully analyzing what is seen; it states that time elapses more slowly for the relatively moving speaker. Its independent grounds are the program’s account of remote synchronization and moving-clock rate differences. Its evidential force concerns clock-rate comparison under the specified observers, synchronization convention, and motion conditions. The comparison concerns rates: agreement of rates after the motion ends and a difference in previously accumulated elapsed quantities are distinct quantities. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Time Contents 4, 6, and 7, Transfer 4, and Result 4.

The explicit metaphor of gravity pulling on time

The expression “as if” in the third statement explicitly marks the metaphor of gravity pulling on time and slowing its passage. Its independent grounds are the subsequent comparison of clocks on lower and upper floors. Its evidential force concerns elapsed-time-rate differences at the specified positions and worldlines. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Time Contents 3, 5, and 11, Transfer 8, and Result 7.

Time elapsed for people on lower and upper floors

In the fourth statement, “experiences” belongs to a relativistic comparison of elapsed quantities for clocks and bodily processes accompanying people on lower and upper floors. Its independent grounds are the relation between those worldlines and clock rates. Its evidential force concerns gravitational clock comparison at those positions. In a general gravitational clock comparison, spacetime geometry, worldlines, and signal paths specify the elapsed quantities being compared. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Time Contents 3, 5, and 11, Transfer 8, and Result 7.

Remote observation and aging near a black hole

The fifth statement says that motion, speech, and bodily processes appear slow to a distant friend observing signals received from a person near a black hole. Its independent grounds are the source’s comparison of worldlines and signal arrival in black-hole spacetime. If bodily processes follow proper time along each worldline and the source’s comparison conditions hold, the corresponding rate difference also applies to those processes. The source then considers a round trip, comparing the elapsed quantity near the black hole with that on Earth. This comparison requires the worldline through the outward journey, stay, and return, together with the reunion event. Its evidential force concerns theoretical consequences under the specified worldline and signal conditions. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Time Contents 5 and 12, Transfer 9, and Result 8.

Common audit with specified domain of application

Time-content, transfer, and result indices refer respectively to Definitions S.569, S.570, and S.571. The five statements describe relativistic comparisons of clocks and physical processes through a general explanation, a motion-conditioned comparison, a gravitational metaphor, a lower/upper-floor comparison, and remote observation near a black hole. Each comparison uses time contents of cycles, elapsed quantities, synchronization, and worldlines. Time-generation reach and result dependence on primitive-time roots are given in Proposition S.573.
