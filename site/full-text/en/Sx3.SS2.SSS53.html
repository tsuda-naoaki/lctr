Assumptions

The RW model uses a positive scale-factor history and a comoving observer family. The local Doppler approximation applies in a low-speed, weak-curvature neighborhood, with distinct curvature and expansion scales. The low-density approximation requires controlled curvature errors along the entire chosen light path. Distant velocity uses the endpoints of the same photon, normalized four-velocities, a selected null path and parallel transport. In general spacetime, the Doppler family is freely falling and the gravitational family satisfies neighboring rest at photon passage. Each family matches the emitter and absorber velocities at the endpoints.

Individual Audit

First Quotation: Coordinate Velocity with Respect to Cosmic Time

The quantity defined by Bunn and Hogg in the first quotation is the derivative of a selected distance history with respect to cosmic time. The distance rate on the present cosmic-time slice and the velocity comparing the galaxy at emission with the observer at reception along the observed light path use different endpoints and comparison conventions.

This derivative corresponds to Time Contents 1 and 16–17 and Transfer 3; the distinction between the present slice and past light cone corresponds to Time Content 19 and Result 12.

Second Quotation: Grounds for Choosing the Doppler Interpretation

Bunn and Hogg regard the Doppler interpretation as the most natural because the comoving family preserves cosmological symmetry and simplifies the description. Source Equations (4)–(5) accumulate infinitesimal frequency ratios along the same light path, while Equations (6)–(7) give relative velocity through light-path transport and successive velocity composition. The gravitational decomposition in Section IV uses a different family satisfying endpoint matching and neighboring rest at photon passage.

Frequency accumulation, the scale-factor ratio and velocity comparison correspond to Transfers 5–16 and Results 8, 10–11 and 13–16; the grounds for choosing the comoving family correspond to Result 17.

Third Quotation: Criticism of Rubber-Sheet Reification

Bunn and Hogg criticize treating the expanding rubber sheet as a material entity. The RW metric used by the same source specifies geometry through cosmic time and the scale-factor history and supports comparisons in local coordinates and observer-family decompositions.

This criticism corresponds to Claims 15–17 and 62–64 and Results 2 and 18. Cosmic time, the scale-factor history, proper time, endpoints, the light path and frequency comparison belong to the generation-reach and root-dependence judgments for the 24 time contents.
