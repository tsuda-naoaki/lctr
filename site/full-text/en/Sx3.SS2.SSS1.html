Assumption Content

The argument uses continuity of magnitude, the correspondence whereby change follows magnitude and time follows change, and the premise that what is bounded by the now is time (219a10–29). Measurement requires a change serving as a unit; common time requires equality and simultaneity; the periodic scale uses uniform circular motion. The discussion of soul is conditional on restricting the capacity to count to soul and intellect (223a21–29).

The operations and original-source locations of the transfers are as follows.

1

Distinguish two nows as before and after and recognize the elapsed interval between them (219a22–219b9).

2

Relate before and after in magnitude and place to before and after in change. The latter belongs to Time Content 2 (219a10–21).

3

Count nows distinguished as before and after, taking time to be the number of change in that respect (219a22–219b9; 223a16–29).

4

Use a specified change as the unit for mutual measurement of counted temporal number and quantity of change (220b14–221a17).

5

Describe past, future, nearness, remoteness and suddenness through intervals relative to a reference now (222a10–223a16).

6

Assign the same time to multiple changes whose numbers are equal and which reach a common limit together (223a29–223b12).

7

Measure time using the periodic scale of uniform circular motion (223b12–224a2).

Placement of Grounding Obligations

Placement of Grounding Obligations

The eight time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already established time inputs. The judgment on the placement of the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment is grounded in a comparison between the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation and the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or establishment condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or invalidation condition.

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

The complete-generation judgment and remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from the actual invalidation of a specific condition that determines the value or domain of application of a result. Neither the original-source formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence for this reference establishes the actual invalidation of such a target condition. The actual-invalidation judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence”; actual invalidation is not inferred solely from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

The reach to results that depend on primitive time roots is determined for this reference by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because the actual invalidation of a specific condition has not been established, the judgment on actual reach from that invalidation position to results is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the targets of repair are the corresponding time content and the generation or establishment conditions required before its use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual invalidation has been independently established and that are reached by dependence from the invalidation position. Because actual invalidation is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If the condition holds, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual Statements

Statement Objects

The relations between time and change and between before and after and temporal number; the distinction between the object counted and the means of counting; judgment of elapsed time by two nows; and the relations between coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be and existence in time.

Verbatim Original

The verbatim originals are from Hussey’s English translation.

a number of change in respect of the before and after

Time is that which is counted and not that by which we count.

Original-Source Locations

The first quotation is from Aristotle, Physics, IV.11, Bekker 219b1, Hussey’s English translation, p. 44. The second quotation is from the same work, IV.11, Bekker 219b1–9, Hussey’s English translation, p. 44.

Minimal Context

In the same work, IV.11, Bekker 218b21–219a9 distinguishes time from change itself without separating time from change; Bekker 219a22–219b1 speaks of time when the soul distinguishes two nows as before and after. IV.13, Bekker 222b16 states that coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be occur in time.

Attribution

The statements are attributed to Aristotle, Physics, Book IV, Chapters 11 and 13, and the English wording of the quotations is attributed to Hussey’s translation.

Predicates

The first quotation assigns to time the predicate of being a number of change in respect of before and after. The second quotation assigns to time the predicate of being the object counted and distinguishes it from the means of counting. The minimal context states the object-level distinction and inseparability of time and change, judgment of elapsed time by two nows, and the existence of coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be in time.

Explicit Domain of Application

The domain of application of the first and second quotations is the relation among time, change, before and after, the now, number, and counting in Physics IV.11. The domain of application of the minimal context extends to judgment of elapsed time and existence in time in IV.11 and IV.13.

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first quotation defines time as a number of change in respect of before and after. The second identifies time as what is counted, distinguishing it from the number used for counting. In the context concerning two nows, the soul speaks of elapsed time when it distinguishes the nows as before and after.

Independent Grounds

In 219a22–219b9, the argument concerning elapsed time through two nows and an intermediate is followed by the distinction between number counted and number used for counting. What is counted includes both what has been counted and what is countable.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The evidential domain of the two quotations is the conceptual relation among time, change, before and after, and counting in IV.11. The condition for the existence of time when the capacity to count is restricted to soul and intellect belongs to the conditional argument in IV.14, 223a21–29.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The first quotation corresponds to time content 4 in Definition S.6 and result 5 in Definition S.9. The second quotation corresponds to time content 4 and result 4. The minimal context corresponds to time contents 1, 2, and 3 and results 1, 3, and 8.

Within-Reference Comparison

The first quotation supplies the definition of time; the second distinguishes the role of the number used to count time from that of the time counted. The relations among before and after in two nows, countability, counting and measurement correspond to Time Contents 1–5 and their respective source locations.
