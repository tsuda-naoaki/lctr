Conditions of Formation

Conditions under which a Dependence or Transfer Applies

Each transfer takes the tabulated time contents, auxiliary objects and supporting claims as inputs and holds under the following conditions.

  1. 1.

    The order of measurement operations associated with a time concept is supplied as an input.

  2. 2.

    The observer-relative assignment of times used in relativity is presupposed.

  3. 3.

    Mirrors are placed at both ends of a meter stick, and the interval of one round trip is chosen as the unit.

  4. 4.

    The same unit interval is identified, and the number of such intervals is recorded.

  5. 5.

    An experiment is chosen in which the effect on the target phenomenon exceeds the anticipated effect on the clock; corrections are iterated using approximate laws and uncorrected time measurements. The source leaves the feasibility of this procedure unanalyzed.

  6. 6.

    The remote-clock rate is adjusted so that the arrival intervals of signals sent by the master clock every second are also read as one second on the remote clock.

  7. 7.

    When a signal emitted at the master-clock zero arrives, the remote zero is set so that distance divided by the remote-clock indication equals the known speed of light.

  8. 8.

    The empirical condition is that, in the limit of approaching the clock, time readings obtained through light, acoustic and tactual signals show no measurable difference.

  9. 9.

    The specified spatial measurements and conventions for synchronization and zero setting are used.

  10. 10.

    Short-time values are substituted into optical and electrodynamic equations and related to agreement with observed facts.

  11. 11.

    The direction of temporal progression implicit in the performed operations is defined as forward.

  12. 12.

    The intervention constituting the cause can be cancelled, and the effect is assumed to occur only if that cause occurs. A return signal from the effect cannot arrive before the controllable cause.

  13. 13.

    The clocks at the two positions belong to the specified extended-time system, and distance is divided by their reading difference. Variable velocity uses the limiting operation stated in the source.

  14. 14.

    The remote clock is set to zero at the instant it receives the signal sent at the emission-clock zero.

  15. 15.

    The signal round trip and reversal of direction are measured with a single local clock. Changing the remote-clock zero convention does not change this local measurement.

  16. 16.

    The present time is assigned to observations made at the zeros of synchronized clocks. The present physical state additionally includes physical measurement information collected at each location.

  17. 17.

    The additional assumption that signals cannot propagate faster than light, relativistic synchronization and spatial separation are used.

  18. 18.

    The interval on a clock carried by the moving object is used together with distance measured by a road-fixed measuring tape or log line.

  19. 19.

    A disturbance travelling along a string stretched by weights on a board serves as the standard; running together, overtaking and lagging behind are compared.

  20. 20.

    The equality of duplicated standards is checked by running together, and velocities are composed by making the second board itself move with the first disturbance.

Objects of Formal Generalization

Results 1–4 concern the operational meaning of time concepts and observer relativity; 5–14, clocks, time units, local measurement and remote synchronization; 15–18, scale, event identity, causal order, velocity definitions and alternative synchronization; and 19–20, operational applicability and empirical revisability.

Conditions and Domain of Application of Formal Generalization

Choosing the light-clock unit and sending one-second master-clock signals are different procedures. Neighborhood agreement depends on comparison of light, acoustic and tactual signals. Causal order uses possible interventions and the system background; the causal cone adds the light-signal bound. In the radiation example, a present state specified by positions, velocities and similar mechanical quantities lacks information needed to determine the future. A present state incorporating physical information throughout space is therefore considered. Direct comparison and composition of velocity standards use simultaneity of running together and overtaking order. The quantum-domain boundary holds if the ordinary measurement operations cannot be performed there.

The 57 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment is grounded in the comparison between the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation and the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or establishment condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or invalidation condition.

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

The complete judgment and remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from the actual invalidation of a specific condition that determines a result’s value or domain of application. The original-source formal evidence and LCTR audit evidence for this reference do not establish the actual invalidation of such a target condition. The actual-invalidation judgment is “indeterminate from original-source evidence”; actual invalidation is not inferred from the backgrounding of the grounding obligation alone.

Boundary between Operational Definition and Physical-Time Generation

Corresponding concepts to actual operations and making the relativity of temporal operations explicit supply operational conditions for time measurement. A construction that takes a hypothetical clock, a prescribed unit of time, clock rates, signal travel time, and law time variables as inputs, however, does not generate physical time from physical inputs containing no temporal role.

Conditions of Formation of Time-Containing Physical Quantities

Clock rates, acceleration histories and velocities defined by distance and an interval use temporal indications, intervals and courses of motion. Those temporal roles require their corresponding generation and physical-time qualification. For direct comparison and composition of velocity standards, the corresponding obligations concern simultaneity and order in running together and overtaking.

Domain of Formation of a Light-Speed-Invariance Proposition

A proposition comparing a common finite light-speed value presupposes the reference frames, clock units, distance measurements and synchronization method used in the comparison. The source’s reception-zero convention changes the time-coordinate setting and assigns an infinite velocity value to light, while its round-trip time measured by a single local clock remains finite.

Individual Statements

Statement Objects

The relativity of time-measurement operations, the relation between physical concepts and actual operations, the absence of critical examination of time-measurement operations, the absence of specifications for clock construction, and the analytical status of local time.

Verbatim Original

all such operations are relative operations

the proper definition of a concept is not in terms of its properties but in terms of actual operations

The physical operations at the basis of the measurement of time have never been subjected to the critical examination which seems to be required.

A rigorous answer to this question involves giving the specifications for the construction of a clock, which we have seen has not yet been done.

Local time is, therefore, a concept treated by the physicist even now as simple and unanalyzable.

Original-Source Location

The first and second quotations are from Bridgman, The Logic of Modern Physics, p. 6, Broad Points of View. The third quotation is on p. 69 of the same reference, the fourth on p. 74, and the fifth on p. 75.

Minimal Context

In the first quotation, “such operations” refers to time-measurement operations in the discussion of the meaning of absolute time. The second quotation is part of a sentence stating the consequences of adopting the view that concepts are defined in terms of actual operations rather than their properties. The third states that the operations underlying time measurement have not undergone the critical examination that seems to be required. In the fourth, “this question” is whether local time also involves the measurement of space; the quotation states that the specifications for clock construction needed for a rigorous answer have not been supplied. The fifth describes the treatment of local time as a simple and unanalyzable concept in physical practice at the time of writing.

Attributing Source

All five statements are attributable to Bridgman’s book.

Predicates

The first quotation attributes relativity to time-measurement operations. The second states the view that a physical concept is properly defined in terms of actual operations rather than its properties. The third states that the physical operations underlying time measurement have not undergone the critical examination that seems to be required. The fourth states that specifications for clock construction have not yet been supplied. The fifth states that, at the time of writing, local time is still treated as a simple and unanalyzable concept.

Explicit Domains of Application

The domain of the first quotation is the time-measurement operations discussed in the examination of absolute time on p. 6 of the original source. The domain of the second is physical concepts for which the operational view of definition is adopted. The domain of the third is the physical operations underlying time measurement; that of the fourth, the specifications for constructing a clock; and that of the fifth, the concept of local time.

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

First, the statements permit interpretation as operational definitions that give the empirical meaning of physical concepts through executable operations. Second, they permit a scale- and apparatus-relative interpretation under which concepts bearing the same name have different empirical contents when their sets of operations differ. Third, they permit an interpretation that uses clocks, signals, records, and comparison operations as generative components for the concept of time, while leaving the generation obligations for order, interval, synchronization, and velocity already contained in those operations. Fourth, the third through fifth quotations permit the interpretation that unresolved obligations remain in the analysis of time-measurement operations, specifications for clock construction, and local time.

Independent Grounds

The first statement describes the relativity of time-measurement operations; the second states the view that physical concepts are specified through actual operations. The third through fifth describe the state, at the time of writing, of critical examination of time-measurement operations, specifications for clock construction and analysis of local time. The calibration proposal on p.71 leaves its feasibility unanalyzed; on pp.74–75, the involvement of spatial measurement in constructing a local clock is considered probable.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first quotation supports the relativity of time-measurement operations; the second supports specifying concepts through actual operations rather than their properties. The third through fifth support the incomplete state of critical examination of time-measurement operations, provision of clock-construction specifications and local-time analysis described by the source. The source’s temporal operations use postulated clock indications, local time, clock rates, unit intervals and law time variables. The generation and physical-time qualification required for their use lie beyond the statements’ evidence-effect boundary under Definition 7.115.

Within-Reference Comparison

The first statement describes the relativity of time-measurement operations; the second presents the operational view of concept definition; and the third through fifth describe the incomplete state, at the time of writing, of time-measurement operations, clock specifications, and the analysis of local time. Comparing these statements aligns the requirement of a single methodology with the points identified as unmet in the source. The use of time inputs by the temporal formalism in the same reference does not discharge this unmet requirement.
