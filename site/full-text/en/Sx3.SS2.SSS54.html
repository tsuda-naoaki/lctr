Primitive-time-root audit of Kiefer 2009

Bibliographic identification

Let R=𝖱𝖾𝖿Kie09TQGR=\mathsf{Ref}_{\mathrm{Kie09TQG}} denote Claus Kiefer, Does Time Exist in Quantum Gravity?, arXiv:0909.3767v1 [gr-qc] (2009). The audit domain comprises the original-source abstract, original-source §§1–4, original-source footnotes, and original-source equations.

Formal Objects

Time parameters, temporal relations, temporal boundaries and references to absent time in the physical arguments and historical account of time concepts are represented by the 35 time contents in Table 511. Repeated references to the same model parameter or temporal relation have the same index. The associated descriptions and claim nodes specify the subsystems, branches, approximation domains and relations in use. Physical carriers, operations, boundary conditions and interpretive assessments are represented by 69 claim nodes; no separate auxiliary objects are indexed.

Assumptions

The Wheeler–DeWitt representation uses quantum universality, the classical limit of general relativity and the adopted canonical formalism. The Mott and semiclassical constructions use the specified subsystem separation, phase and approximation. Interference suppression uses environmental degrees of freedom and selected model parameters; the time direction uses the small-scale product boundary and semiclassical approximation. The recollapsing-model conclusions use their large-scale boundary and interference condition near the classical turning point. The transfer and result tables specify where each condition is required.

Non-Temporal Physical Inputs

Claims 1–10 in Table 513 place the non-temporal configurations and records of the spatial metric, non-gravitational fields, heavy and light subsystems, gravitational degrees of freedom, environment, subsystem partition, spatial scale, boundaries and physical correlations in the physical initial set. Claims 11–69 retain the source equations, model conditions, interpretations and audit distinctions as evidence-only components.

Time Contents

External time, dynamical spacetime, model clocks, time direction, histories and temporal ends are distinguished. Mentioning time in a statement about its absence or in a historical account does not by itself make that time a necessary input to a physical operation.

Evidential Force

Recognizing the source’s time constructions leaves dependencies on quantum and relativistic time inputs and on the temporal meanings of the speed of light and the Hubble constant. The absence of external time from an equation does not prove the nonexistence of physical time. Construction of a model parameter is not by itself complete LCTR generation. Numerical scale and entropy ordering is treated as a relation prior to its assignment to time; the connection uses the semiclassical approximation. The future classical end in the recollapsing universe is relative to the specified model and boundaries.

Result-Preservation Domains

Each result is preserved under the inputs in its dependency table and the source’s conditions. The relevant results retain the Mott proportionality relation; the subsystems, branches and domains of the semiclassical approximation; the environment and partition for decoherence; the product boundary for time direction; and the large-scale boundary and interference conditions for recollapse. Verifying historical accounts or statements about absent time does not require generation of a physical clock. Physical uses of time contents require separate assessment of their generation, comparison and domain qualifications.

Placement of Grounding Obligations

Placement of grounding obligations

For physical uses dependent on primitive-time inputs, the obligations are classified as backgrounded through primitive-time inputs. The source supplies construction procedures for model time parameters and directions, but not complete generation from non-temporal physical inputs alone. Mere mention of absent or historical time is not classified as a physical time input.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of actual failure

The source makes wave-packet tracking of the classical trajectory without significant spreading a necessary condition for viable semiclassical time. If that condition fails, the semiclassical approximation is not viable. Results 11 and 14 state the conditional consequences of the specified big-brake and recollapsing models. Actual failure of LCTR’s physical-time grounding conditions in the universe is not determined by this source evidence. Failure of a model approximation is distinguished from such actual failure.

Downstream Reach

Downstream reach

Repair Scope

Repair scope

If actual failure of a concrete grounding condition is established, repair the relevant time construction, comparison and domain and reassess the results dependent on that input. Specific recalculated values require a specified model and input values. Interpretation of absent time and historical accounts are distinguished from this physical recalculation.

Individual statements

  1. 1.

    Original text (English)

    Time is absolute in standard quantum theory and dynamical in general relativity.

    Original-source location Abstract, p. 1. Minimal context This sentence opens the abstract and is followed immediately by the statement that combining the two theories produces the problem of time.

    The statement object is the theoretical status of time in standard quantum theory and general relativity. Its predicate is that time is absolute in standard quantum theory and dynamical in general relativity. Its explicit applicability domain is standard quantum theory and general relativity as contrasted in the abstract, together with the problem of time that arises when the two theories are combined.

  2. 2.

    Original text (English)

    It is generally argued that quantum-gravity effects can only be seen at a remote scale – the Planck scale, which originates from the combination of the three fundamental constants cc (speed of light), GG (gravitational constant), and ℏ\hbar (quantum of action).

    Original-source location p. 3, §2. Minimal context This sentence reports the common argument restricting quantum-gravity effects to the Planck scale. After the Planck-length formula, the same paragraph rejects that restriction as misleading.

    The statement objects are the scale at which quantum-gravity effects are expected to be observable and the constants composing the Planck scale. Its predicate is that quantum-gravity effects are generally argued to be visible only at the Planck scale and that this scale arises from the combination of cc, GG, and ℏ\hbar. Its explicit applicability domain is the scale-restriction argument reported and rejected in the same paragraph of §2, together with the dimensional construction of the Planck length given there. The author does not endorse restricting quantum-gravity effects to the Planck scale.

    Time emerges from the separation into two different subsystems: one subsystem (here: the gravitational part) defines the time with respect to which the other subsystem (here: the non-gravitational part) evolves.

Its explicit applicability domain is the Mott analogy in §3, the separation of gravitational and nongravitational subsystems, the selected semiclassical branch, and the approximation order.

Available interpretations

The restriction to the Planck scale reported in the second statement is rejected by the author in the same paragraph. The dimensional construction of the Planck length is distinct from the claim that quantum-gravity effects are confined to that scale. The third and fourth statements concern semiclassical time obtained through subsystem separation and its approximate character. The sixth states the author’s claims of fundamental timelessness and the emergence of classical appearance in subsystems through decoherence. Under the specified cosmological model and boundary conditions, the seventh defines the direction of time by the increase in entanglement accompanying an increase in the scale factor, in the domain where the semiclassical approximation holds. These statements belong to different equations, approximations, interpretations, and model conditions in the source. Obtaining a subsystem evolution parameter or a direction of time and establishing LCTR physical-time qualification from nontemporal physical inputs have different generation conditions.

Hubble constant, scalar-field mass, and selected numerical values

The fifth statement makes explicit that the Hubble constant H0H_{0}, scalar-field mass mm, speed of light cc, action constant ℏ\hbar, and selected standard numerical values are taken as inputs to evaluate the interference-suppression quantity in the original Eq. (6). The independent basis is the numerical calculation in the original source that uses H0H_{0}, mm, cc, ℏ\hbar, and the selected values in Eq. (6) to evaluate the amount of interference suppression between complex components. Its evidential force belongs to that model, Eq. (6), the selected values, and their numerical evaluation.

Formally, it corresponds to source-form description 38 in Definition S.535, the reproduced original Eq. (6), the corresponding result in Definition S.537, and the root determination in Proposition S.539.
