Individual Statements

Statement object

The influence relation between gravity and speed and what the article calls time, together with the explanatory move from the results of comparisons between clocks at different altitudes to the passage of time.

Original text (English)
  1. 1.

    Albert Einstein’s general theory of relativity predicted that gravity and speed influences time, the faster you travel the more time slows down, but also the more gravity pulling on you the more time slows down.

  2. 2.

    Clocks at higher altitude show time passes faster with respect to a clock on the Earth surface as there is less gravity from Earth the farther you are from our planet.

Primary-source location

In European Space Agency, Clocks, gravity, and the limits of relativity, 23 May 2019, the first quotation occurs in the opening paragraph explaining relativity, and the second occurs in the paragraph explaining gravitational time dilation.

Minimal context

Before introducing the purpose of ACES, the first quotation presents the article’s opening explanation connecting gravitational and kinematic conditions with a slowing of time. The second follows the statement that comparisons between clocks at different altitudes have been experimentally demonstrated and describes the relative rate of a higher-altitude clock as the rate at which time passes.

Attribution

Both statements are attributed to the text of the official European Space Agency article.

Predicate and explicit domain of application

The first quotation states that gravity and speed affect time and that time passes more slowly at greater speed and under stronger gravity. The second concerns clocks at different altitudes and states that a higher-altitude clock shows that time passes faster relative to a clock at Earth’s surface.

Individual Audit

Interpretive possibilities

First, the expressions can be read as explanatory shorthand describing gravitational and kinematic differences in clock readings or clock rates as an influence on time or a difference in its rate of passage. Second, they can be read as theoretical expressions describing the conditional dependence of metric and proper-time relations in general relativity as an influence on time or a difference in its rate of passage.

Boundary of evidential force

The two quotations explain time dilation under gravitational and motion conditions and the relative rate of a higher-altitude clock with respect to a surface clock. The passage immediately preceding the second quotation describes confirmation of the effect by comparing clocks at different altitudes.

Independent grounds

The grounds are the article’s comparisons of clocks at different altitudes, its account of relative rates with a surface clock as the reference, and the ACES configuration of ground and ISS clocks and two-way links.

Corresponding formal evidence

The quotations correspond to time contents 4–9, Claims 3 and 8–10, Results 2 and 3, and Proposition S.643. The time contents, claims, and results are given in Definitions S.637, S.638, and S.641, respectively.

Within-Reference Comparison

The first quotation describes the influence of gravity and speed on time; the second describes the comparison of clocks at different altitudes in terms of the passage of time.

Common Audit with a Specified Domain of Application

Under either the clock-comparison reading or the relativistic-time reading, comparison values, scales, and clock correspondences supply the temporal inputs to the explanation. Their generation dependencies are the subject of Proposition S.643.
