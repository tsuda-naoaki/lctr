Relocation into the LCTR Generation Order

This judgment follows by matching the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation against the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or validity condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The 25 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already-established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

For each reference, the reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from that failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the objects of repair are the corresponding time contents and the generation or validity conditions required before their use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual failure is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that failure location. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Common Evidence with Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first statement reports a measurement in which the apparent frequency difference of electromagnetic radiation corresponding to a gravitational-potential difference was inferred from detector counts through calibration, correction, and statistical processing. The second statement uses the lifetime of the Fe57 level as part of the spectroscopic conditions for obtaining a narrow resonance line. The third statement gives the Lorentzian shape and full-width at half-height obtained under the specified conditions of source preparation. The fourth statement shows that the causal attribution of the observed frequency difference is not unique. The fifth statement makes knowledge and correction of the temperature difference a condition for extracting meaning from the observed change. The sixth statement gives the agreement between the corrected measured shift and the weak-gravitational-field prediction. None of the six statements names time itself independently of a physical carrier as the object of measurement.

Independent Grounds

Detector counts, configuration reversal, Doppler calibration, temperature correction, statistical uncertainty, and the ratio of measured value to predicted value provide independent experimental grounds for the apparent frequency shift of electromagnetic radiation. Those grounds do not directly measure a change in an attribute of a time entity independent of clocks or other physical carriers.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first statement directly supports that the correspondence between gravitational potential and the apparent frequency difference of electromagnetic radiation was the measurement object; the directly observed quantities are detector counts, whereas the frequency shift is an inferred quantity after calibration and correction. The second and third statements directly support the level lifetime, resonance-line shape, and linewidth as conditions of measurement sensitivity, but do not supply the lifetime or linewidth as a starting point for time generation. The fourth statement directly supports the non-uniqueness of attribution of the frequency difference, and the fifth directly supports the need for temperature correction; they do not permit an uncorrected difference to be attributed uniquely to the gravitational effect. The sixth statement supports agreement between the measured inferred quantity and predicted quantity within the domain of the original source’s apparatus, configuration reversal, calibration, temperature correction, and uncertainty. That agreement does not yield the physical generation of the time contents used, physical-time qualification, or a change in a time attribute independent of a physical carrier.

Within-Source Comparison

The first statement gives the measurement object; the second and third give the level lifetime, line shape, and linewidth; the fourth and fifth give causal attribution and correction conditions; and the sixth gives the agreement after correction. Taken together, the six statements make explicit the experimental dependence sequence from apparatus conditions to comparison of the inferred and predicted quantities, but physical generation of the time contents is not contained in that sequence.
