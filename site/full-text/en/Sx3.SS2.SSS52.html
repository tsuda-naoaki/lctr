Statement evidence 1

Original text (English):

The idea of an expanding universe can easily lead to confusion, and this note tries to counter some of the more tenacious misconceptions. The worst of these is the ‘expanding space’ fallacy.

Original-source location: Peacock, A Diatribe on Expanding Space, arXiv:0809.4573v1, opening of §1. Minimal context: This is the introduction to §1, which enumerates misconceptions concerning cosmic expansion. Attribution: J. A. Peacock.

The statement object is the set of misconceptions accompanying explanations of an expanding universe, particularly the “expanding space” explanation. The predicate is that the idea of an expanding universe readily causes confusion, that the source opposes those misconceptions, and that it identifies “expanding space” as the worst fallacy among them. The explicit applicability domain comprises the Robertson–Walker, Milne, and de Sitter representations compared in the original source, together with its explanations of local dynamics and cosmological redshift.

Statement evidence 3

Original text (English):

It should be clear that Minkowski spacetime does not expand – indeed, the very idea that the motion of distant galaxies could affect local dynamics is profoundly anti-relativistic: the equivalence principle says that we can always find a tangent frame in which physics is locally special relativity.

Original-source location: Peacock, A Diatribe on Expanding Space, arXiv:0809.4573v1, §1, the paragraph discussing local dynamics. Minimal context: This belongs to the examination of whether the global description of “expanding space” entails a local dynamical effect. Attribution: J. A. Peacock.

The statement object is whether Minkowski spacetime expands and the explanation that attributes an effect on local dynamics to the motion of distant galaxies. The predicate is that Minkowski spacetime does not expand and that the idea that the motion of distant galaxies affects local dynamics conflicts with the equivalence principle. The explicit applicability domain is Minkowski spacetime, the local tangent frame supplied by the equivalence principle, and local physics described as special relativity in that frame.

Individual Audits

The expanding-space fallacy

Peacock criticizes the treatment of a description of cosmic expansion as an independent dynamical mechanism that pushes local bodies. The source recognizes global growth of closed-slice volume, while calculating local motion from matter and vacuum-energy terms and initial conditions, and explaining redshift through an infinitesimal Doppler chain along the light path.

Claims 16–24 and 42, and Results 1–7 and 12–13, express these distinctions among coordinates, volume, local motion and redshift.

Increase in the total volume of a closed universe

Peacock’s second statement treats increasing closed-slice volume as a global expansion quantity. For a fixed comoving slice, V=V0⁢a3V=V_{0}a^{3} and V˙=3⁢H⁢V\dot{V}=3HV, so volume grows on intervals with H>0H>0. The family of slices and a⁢(t)a(t) used here are already temporally ordered.

Claims 23–24 and Results 4–5 give this volume change and its distinction from local dynamics.

Minkowski spacetime and local dynamics

In the third statement, Peacock uses the Milne representation of Minkowski spacetime and local tangent frames to criticize the treatment of distant-galaxy motion as an expansion force acting directly on local bodies. The special-relativistic tangent-frame metric is local to a point; tidal relative acceleration over a finite region depends on curvature.

Claims 20, 25–26 and 42, and Results 2 and 6, relate this geometry and approximation domain to the local equation of motion.
