Formal Objects

The audit uses 23 time contents, 47 claims, five transfers and 18 results. The claim table and formal-input definition specify the closed systems, states, Hamiltonians, clock projections, conditional expectations and relations. Page and Wootters consider a closed system including its observers, exclude dependence on external coordinate time from its observables, and describe dynamical evolution through the dependence of other variables on internal clock readings. The spin model in Section II uses a prescribed frequency and initial product state. The noninteracting ideal clock in Section III takes a stationary global state and a clock-state orbit as premises and derives agreement between clock-conditioned expectations and Heisenberg evolution. Real clocks, the thermodynamic arrow and intrinsic geometry are discussed under their respective conditions.

Assumptions

Page and Wootters’ observability argument concerns a closed system with a well-defined Hamiltonian, internal observers and a specified observable class commuting with the Hamiltonian. An asymptotically flat universe is presented as one consistent model. The spin model uses finitely many spins and a specified preparation state. The ideal-clock derivation uses noninteraction, an additive Hamiltonian, a stationary global state, a normalized clock zero state and positive conditioning weight.

Individual Audit

First Statement

Page and Wootters’ first statement asserts that assuming a stationary state for a closed system is consistent with observations. In their stationary observable class, removing density-matrix elements between distinct energies leaves expectations unchanged. This algebraic relation and their observability argument for a closed system including its observers correspond to Claims 4–12 and Results 1, 2 and 8. Their argument about observation records within a single closed system is Claim 46; embedding in a pure zero-energy state of a larger system is Claim 26 and Result 9.

Second Statement

Page and Wootters’ second statement gives a method for describing dynamical evolution through correlations conditioned on internal clock readings. The spin model in Section II corresponds to Claims 13–27 and Results 3–10; the noninteracting ideal clock in Section III corresponds to Claims 28–35 and Results 11–13. For the ideal clock, global stationarity, the additive Hamiltonian, the selected clock zero state, the projection tied to its orbit and positive conditioning weight imply that conditional rest-system expectations equal expectations of Heisenberg-evolved operators in clock time.

Third Statement

Page and Wootters’ third statement restricts observable temporal dependence to dependence on internal clock time. Claims 36–42 and Results 14–16 concern reading interactions, energy-limited precision and abstract coordinate time as an idealization of real clocks. The practical approximation applies when interaction effects on a good clock are negligible. Commutation with the Hamiltonian serves as a necessary condition in the source’s observability argument.

Common Audit with Specified Domain of Application

The stationary description of a closed system, internal-clock conditional evolution and real-clock readings and approximations have their respective state, operator and interaction conditions specified above.
