Formal objects

The audit uses 61 source claims, 70 time contents, 35 transfers, and 26 results. Claims 1–13 specify material and spatial initial components; Claims 14–61 specify phase laws, measurement rules, application conditions, arguments by Wolf et al., and prior work reported in their paper.

Assumptions

The three-pulse action cancellation uses the same at-most-quadratic Lagrangian, its classical paths, position continuity and closure, and equal and opposite recoils. Vanishing of the whole free phase also requires the internal-state term to vanish, as it does for the symmetric geometry T=T′T=T^{\prime}. Its intervals carry the proper-time interpretation in footnote 6 on p. 10, and each expression is used within its stated approximation. The two-pulse Ramsey geometry uses negligible recoil and wave-packet overlap within the coherence length.

Individual statements

Statement evidence 1

Original text (English):

clock operating at the Compton frequency

The source location is the abstract on p. 1 of Wolf et al., Does an atom interferometer test the gravitational redshift at the Compton frequency? The minimal context is the passage in which Wolf et al. report the claim by Müller, Peters, and Chu that an atom is a clock operating at the Compton frequency associated with its rest mass. Wolf et al. are the authors of the quoted passage; Müller, Peters, and Chu are the proponents of the reported claim.

The statement object is the interpretation that treats an atom used in an atom interferometer as a clock operating at the Compton frequency. The predicate is that the atom is a clock operating at the Compton frequency. The explicit domain of application is the existing interpretation, introduced in the abstract, of the atom interferometer as a gravitational-redshift test.

Statement evidence 2

Original text (English):

not a real clock

The source location is p. 6 of the same source. The minimal context compares a clock whose resonant signal is read by tuning a drive to an internal atomic transition with the reinterpretation of the whole atom as a clock ticking at the Compton frequency. The agents to whom the statement is attributed are P. Wolf et al.

The statement object is the reinterpretation that treats the atom as a whole as a clock ticking at the Compton frequency. The predicate is that the whole atom supplies no physical signal at the Compton frequency and thus does not constitute a clock with a readable resonant signal in the sense just described. The explicit domain of application is the signal-based criterion for clock operation on p. 6.

Individual audit

Clock criterion and phase relations

Müller, Peters, and Chu treat the whole atom as a clock operating at the Compton frequency. Wolf et al. use a readable resonant signal tuned to an internal transition as the criterion for clock operation and criticize applying the Compton-clock interpretation to a whole atom that supplies no signal at that frequency. The internal-transition clock on p. 11 uses Ramsey fringes from two-pulse interference to lock the drive frequency to the internal transition frequency. Interference between the two arms constitutes its clock operation.

For the three-pulse interferometer, classical paths obtained from the same at-most-quadratic Lagrangian, together with the position conditions in Eq. (2.12) and recoil conditions in Eq. (2.13), yield cancellation of the external action difference in Eq. (2.17). The free-propagation phase retains the internal-state term ωg⁢g′⁢(T−T′)\omega_{gg^{\prime}}(T-T^{\prime}) in Eq. (2.18); the full free-propagation phase vanishes for the symmetric case T=T′T=T^{\prime}. Footnote 6 on p. 10 specifies that the interval in this internal phase is, strictly, a proper-time interval. In the weak-field, nonrelativistic, linear-potential approximation to general relativity, the total phase is the light-interaction phase k⁢g⁢T2kgT^{2}.

Comparison of the atomic acceleration inferred from the interferometer with the measured acceleration of a separately falling corner cube constitutes the universality-of-free-fall test. The source’s model using the same modified Lagrangian for trajectory and phase also makes this comparison. A model assigning different Lagrangians to the trajectory and wave phase yields a redshift-like phase; Wolf et al. argue that this assignment conflicts with the Feynman path-integral and least-action basis used to calculate the phase.

For the neutron interferometer, the leading expression in Eq. (A1) is transformed through the de Broglie relation, T=L/vT=L/v, and the Bragg condition into the phase representation in Eq. (A4), using the lattice wave vector, gravitational acceleration, and flight time. The flight time carries temporal dependence through the neutron velocity. The source provides a theoretical analysis of these existing atom- and neutron-interferometer experiments.
