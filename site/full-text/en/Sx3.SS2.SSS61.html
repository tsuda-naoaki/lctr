Original-location content

The audit domain specified in the bibliographic identification and the original-location columns of the respective evidence tables.

Formal objects

The audit uses 19 time contents, 36 source claims, 10 transfers, and 26 results. Claims 1–12 specify material and spatial initial components; Claims 13–36 specify physical laws, formal operations, measurement rules, and historical accounts.

Assumptions

The Lorentz transformation uses inertial frames with a common origin, linearity, the principle of relativity, and invariance of the vacuum speed of light. The temporal structures of the physical spacetime interval and Minkowski metric belong to Time Contents 17 and 18. Historical calculations under the ether hypothesis constitute the domain of Time Contents 13 and 19 and Results 22–24 and 26.

Nontemporal physical inputs

The generation-initial set comprises the material degrees of freedom and initial spatial configurations in Claims 1–12. Temporal event order, clock-tick sequences, motion histories, reading intervals, and simultaneity belong to their respective time contents.

Evidential scope

The domain comprises Lorentz transformations, relative simultaneity, causal order, time dilation for clocks, signals, and general processes, worldline proper time, and the hypotheses and readings specified in each historical account.

Result-preservation domain

The 26 results hold under their respective conditions on correspondence of the same event pair or process, inertial frames, time orientation, unit calibration, physical metric, and historical hypotheses.

Placement of grounding obligations

The 19 time contents are used as already established inputs without prior generation from nontemporal physical inputs. The placement judgment is that the grounding obligation for physical time is backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.

Evidence of actual invalidation

Whether the input conditions specified here actually fail cannot be determined from the source evidence.

Repair scope

Repair concerns the grounding conditions for generating time contents before use. If an actual failure of a specific condition is located, dependent results are recalculated from repaired inputs or restricted to the domain in which their conditions hold.

Individual statements

Statement evidence 1

Original text (English):

the same argument holds for any process, be it clocks, elementary particles or human hearts

The source location is Tong, Dynamics and Relativity, §7.2.3 Time Dilation, p. 119. The minimal context is the extension of the argument for the clock-tick interval ratio to physical processes other than clocks. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is David Tong.

The statement objects are the argument given for the clock-tick interval ratio and the physical processes to which it is applied. The predicate is that the same argument holds for any process, including clocks, elementary particles, and human hearts. The explicit domain of application comprises inter-frame time dilation in §7.2.3 and the clock, particle, and biological processes enumerated by the source.

Statement evidence 2

Original text (English):

The correct interpretation is that time itself runs more slowly in moving frames.

The source location is Tong, Dynamics and Relativity, §7.2.3 Time Dilation, p. 119. The minimal context is the interpretation stated after the derivation of the time-dilation ratio. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is David Tong.

The statement object is the rate of passage of time itself in a moving inertial frame. The predicate is that, on the correct interpretation, time itself runs more slowly. The explicit domain of application is the clock-tick interval ratio between moving inertial frames derived in §7.2.3.

Statement evidence 3

Original text (English):

it is time itself which is running slower.

The source location is Tong, Dynamics and Relativity, §7.2.3 Time Dilation, under the subheading On Muons and Planes, p. 120. The immediate context is the paragraph attributing the increased muon lifetime to time itself running more slowly, on the grounds that it cannot be explained by the slowing of an internal mechanical clock. The agent to whom the statement is attributed is David Tong.

The statement object is that to which slower running is attributed in the explanation of the increased muon lifetime. The predicate is that time itself is what runs more slowly. The explicit domain of application is the explanation of muon lifetimes in §7.2.3.

Individual audit

Objects and claims of the statements

Statement 1 asserts that the same argument about clock-tick intervals holds for every physical process, explicitly including elementary particles and human hearts. Statement 2 presents time itself running more slowly in moving inertial frames as the “correct interpretation.” Statement 3 follows the explanation that elementary particles are not clocks with internal machinery and attributes the increased muon lifetime to time itself running more slowly.

For the same pair of tick events on the same clock, the interval T′T^{\prime} in its rest frame and the interval TT in another inertial frame are related by T=γ⁢T′T=\gamma T^{\prime}, with γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2\gamma=(1-v^{2}/c^{2})^{-1/2}. Time Contents 5 and 6, Transfer 5, and Result 4 give this relation. The emission and floor-arrival events in Figure 51 correspond to Time Content 7 and Result 5; the geometry of signal propagation yields the same factor γ\gamma. The generalization to every process corresponds to Transfer 10 and Result 6, relating the rest-frame elapsed content 15 to the moving-frame comparison content 16 for the same process. The physical velocities in these factors use the common temporal scale and unit calibration of Time Content 11.

Common audit with specified domain of application

Theoretical generalization and experimental evidence

The source describes tests in particle accelerators such as the LHC and LEP, muon lifetimes, and comparisons of readings from aircraft-borne atomic clocks. The muon and aircraft-clock examples correspond to Time Contents 8 and 9 and Results 7 and 8. Both special and general relativity contribute to the aircraft-clock difference. Human hearts occur in the theoretical generalization as physical processes to which the same argument applies.
