Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The 11 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment is grounded in the comparison between the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation and the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or establishment condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or invalidation condition.

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

The complete judgment and remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from the actual invalidation of a specific condition that determines a result’s value or domain of application. The original-source formal evidence and LCTR audit evidence for this reference do not establish the actual invalidation of such a target condition. The actual-invalidation judgment is “indeterminate from original-source evidence”; actual invalidation is not inferred from the backgrounding of the grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

The reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined for this reference by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because the actual invalidation of a specific condition has not been established, the judgment of actual reach from the invalidation position to a result is “indeterminate from original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

The repair targets based on structural reach are the corresponding time content and the generation or establishment conditions required before its use. The recalculation scope is limited to results for which actual invalidation is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that invalidation position. Because actual invalidation is indeterminate in this reference, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If the condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their establishment domain is restricted. Values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Audit Conclusion

Proposition S.59 (Complete Time-Use Audit of von Neumann 1932).

This reference has 70 semantic components, 11 time contents, eight time-content transfers, and 13 results. The generated time contents under the complete judgment and the reached time contents under the local-reach judgment both number zero, and results 3, 5–7, and 10–13, eight in total, depend on primitive time roots.

Proof.

Every transfer in Definition S.51 takes time content as an input. In Definition S.55, results 3, 5–7, and 10–13 have time-content presuppositions, whereas results 1, 2, 4, 8, and 9 do not. Every edge that outputs time content takes time content as an input. Thus no time content is reached even when the reference’s formal-evidence environment is added to the physical-input initial set. The time-content sets under both the complete judgment and the local-reach judgment are therefore empty, and all 11 time contents are primitive time roots. ∎

The boundary between the two is arbitrary to a considerable extent.

Statement Evidence 4

Original (German):

Daß diese Grenze beliebig tief ins Innere des Körpers des wirklichen Beobachters verschoben werden kann, ist der Inhalt des Prinzips vom psychophysikalischen Parallelismus — dies ändert aber nichts daran, daß sie bei jeder Beschreibungsweise irgendwo gezogen werden muß, wenn dieselbe nicht leer laufen, d. h. wenn ein Vergleich mit der Erfahrung möglich sein soll.

English Translation:

That this boundary can be displaced arbitrarily far into the interior of the actual observer’s body is the content of the principle of psychophysical parallelism. This does not alter the fact that in every mode of description the boundary must be drawn somewhere if the description is not to run empty, that is, if comparison with experience is to be possible.

The source location is von Neumann, Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik, Springer (1932), p. 224, §VI.1. The minimal context simultaneously states the mobility of the boundary and the necessity of placing a boundary for comparison with experience. The attributing source of the statement is J. von Neumann.

Statement Evidence 5

Original (German):

Denn die Erfahrung macht nur Aussagen von diesem Typus: ein Beobachter hat eine bestimmte (subjektive) Wahrnehmung gemacht, und nie eine solche: eine physikalische Größe hat einen bestimmten Wert.

English Translation:

For experience makes statements only of this type: an observer has had a particular subjective perception, and never of this type: a physical quantity has a particular value.

The source location is von Neumann, Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik, Springer (1932), p. 224, §VI.1. The minimal context is the distinction between the types of statements supplied by experience. The attributing source of the statement is J. von Neumann.

Statement Evidence 6

Original (German):

Die Quantenmechanik beschreibt nun die Ereignisse, die im beobachteten Teile der Welt stattfinden, solange sie mit dem beobachtenden Teile nicht in Wechselwirkung stehen, mit Hilfe des Prozesses 2. (V. 1.); sobald aber eine derartige Wechselwirkung stattfindet, d. h. eine Messung, schreibt sie das Verwenden des Prozesses 1. vor.

English Translation:

Quantum mechanics describes the events occurring in the observed part of the world by means of process 2 so long as that part does not interact with the observing part. As soon as such an interaction, that is, a measurement, occurs, it prescribes the use of process 1.

The source location is von Neumann, Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik, Springer (1932), p. 224, §VI.1. The minimal context contrasts the conditions of application of process 2 and process 1. The attributing source of the statement is J. von Neumann.

Statement Evidence 7

Original (German):

Indessen besteht die Gefahr, daß das Prinzip vom psychophysikalischen Parallelismus verletzt ist, solange nicht gezeigt wird, daß die Grenze zwischen dem beobachteten System und dem Beobachter im w. o. angegebenen Sinne beliebig verschiebbar ist.

English Translation:

There is, however, a danger that the principle of psychophysical parallelism is violated unless it is shown that the boundary between the observed system and the observer can be displaced arbitrarily in the sense stated above.

The source location is von Neumann, Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik, Springer (1932), p. 224, §VI.1. The minimal context connects the description using the two processes to the condition of boundary mobility. The attributing source of the statement is J. von Neumann.

Common Evidence with Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first statement explicitly states that the wave-function representation of a nonstationary state contains time tt. The second statement presents the claim that subjective apperception associated with measurement is a new entity irreducible to the physical environment. The third statement presents the arbitrariness of the placement of the boundary between the observed object and observer; the fourth, the mobility of that boundary and the necessity of placing it for empirical comparison. The fifth statement treats empirical statements as statements about an observer’s subjective perception and distinguishes them from statements that a physical quantity has a value. The sixth statement gives the conditions of application of process 2 in the absence of interaction with the observing part and process 1 when measurement interaction occurs. The seventh states that the principle of psychophysical parallelism is in danger of being violated unless boundary mobility is demonstrated.

Independent Grounds

The seven statements set out the formal and conceptual relations among state evolution by external numerical time, the measurement boundary, subjective apperception, empirical statements, and the two processes.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first statement directly supports that the wave function contains time tt, but does not support the physical generation, measurement procedure, or physical-time qualification of tt. The second through fifth statements directly support the distinctions adopted by the original source concerning measurement, subjective apperception, boundary placement, and empirical statements. The sixth statement directly supports the conditions of application of process 1 and process 2, but does not support the generation of the temporal order, interaction duration, or evolution time used by the two processes. The seventh statement directly supports boundary mobility as a condition on psychophysical parallelism.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The first statement corresponds to the time variable used in physical and formal claim component 16 of Definition S.48 and to time content 2, and supports the time input of result 5 in Definition S.53. The second and fifth statements correspond to the distinction between subjective perception and physical description treated in component 30. The third statement corresponds to the boundary placement in component 36; the fourth corresponds to psychophysical parallelism in component 31 and to boundary placement and mobility in components 36 and 37. The sixth statement corresponds to the conditions of application of the two processes treated in components 18 and 21, and uses mathematical object type 7. The seventh statement states that the principle of psychophysical parallelism in component 31 is in danger of being violated unless boundary mobility in components 36 and 37 is demonstrated. The boundaries in the third, fourth, and seventh statements correspond to the measurement boundary in mathematical object type 8.

Within-Reference Comparison

The first statement uses external time for state evolution; the second through fifth and seventh treat the boundary between the observed object and observer and the attribution of empirical statements; and the sixth distinguishes the two processes by the presence or absence of measurement interaction. Formal time, measurement interaction, boundary placement, and subjective perception have distinct object types within the same reference and cannot be integrated into a single evidence type.
