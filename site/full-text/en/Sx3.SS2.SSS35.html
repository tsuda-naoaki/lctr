Definition S.321 (Formal objects and explicit assumptions in Connes–Rovelli 1994).

Connes and Rovelli propose the thermal-time hypothesis, which uses the modular flow associated with a specified state and observables algebra as physical time. The GNS representation, Tomita–Takesaki construction and outer automorphism class supply mathematical constructions. Internal clocks, comparison with known Hamiltonian time, Rindler proper time, the bath rest frame and FRW time have their respective physical application conditions.

The modular construction uses a fixed von Neumann algebra and faithful normal state, or a cyclic separating vector. The Gibbs branch uses a finite partition function; the classical branch uses a positive density and Poisson structure; the Rindler branch uses Minkowski causal structure and the vacuum restricted to a wedge. Adoption as physical time is given by the thermal-time hypothesis. Scale comparisons in each branch use the known time identified by the source.

The formal objects comprise the following nine groups.

  1. 1.

    Observables, states, physical reference fields and classical phase space

  2. 2.

    Relational observables and internal clocks in generally covariant theories

  3. 3.

    GNS representation, modular group and outer automorphism class

  4. 4.

    Thermal-time hypothesis, KMS condition and correlators

  5. 5.

  6. 6.

  7. 7.

    Rindler wedge, boost flow, Unruh relation and Hawking extension

  8. 8.

    Selection of subalgebras and states, and the Planck-phase proposal

  9. 9.

Evidence Hierarchy

Connes and Rovelli propose the thermal-time hypothesis on the basis of operator-algebraic constructions and discuss internal clocks, Gibbs states, the classical limit, the Rindler branch and cosmological applications. The source reports zero directly measured objects and zero direct-measurement predicates from measurements of its own. Clocks, preparation apparatus and detectors appear as objects of physical interpretation.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count is zero; source-evidence-conditioned local reach contains 3 time contents, leaving 25 primitive-time roots. Thermal time from a state and algebra, thermal time from a classical density and Poisson structure, and application to the specified CMB state are reached conditionally on the source’s constructions and thermal-time hypothesis. Known Hamiltonian time, reference clocks, proper time, Lorentz time and the FRW comparison time enter their respective application domains as inputs.

Results reachable from the source formal-evidence environment without added time roots are 8–11, 13, 15, 18–19, 22–24, 30–32, 34, 37: 16 results, including mathematical constructions, conditions of state specification and local thermal-time constructions. There are 40 root–result dependence pairs, of which 22 are necessary.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Connes and Rovelli define thermal flow from a state and algebra and adopt it as physical time. Internal clocks and experimental interpretation use clock readings, order and intervals; special branches use correspondence with known times. All 28 time contents are primitive-time roots under the complete judgment. Under local reach, Time Contents 11, 19 and 27 are constructed and 25 remain. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Faithfulness, local clock invertibility, normalization of the Gibbs state, existence of the classical flow and conditions on the wedge algebra specify the domains of the respective results. The source discusses stopping, reversal and nonunitarity of internal clocks as general applicability problems. Actual failure of a specific audited physical-time generation condition and the resulting change in result values are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Repair Scope

Individual Statements

Absence of a Preferred Time Flow

Original text:

there is no preferred time flow

The passage occurs in arXiv:gr-qc/9406019v1, p. 2, §1. Connes and Rovelli state that a Hamiltonian function specifies evolution in non-generally-covariant theories, while generally covariant theories supply no preferred flow of the same kind. Their immediately following account of general relativity retains causal classification and worldline proper time on each spacetime solution and discusses their state dependence.

Postulate Identifying Thermal Time with Physical Time

Original text:

thermal time … defines the physical time

The passage occurs at the beginning of p. 4, §1, continuing a sentence from the end of p. 3. For the flow determined by a state in generally covariant classical statistical mechanics, Connes and Rovelli introduce the identification of thermal and physical time after the phrase “one can postulate”. On the same p. 4 they extend the idea to quantum theory by identifying physical time with the modular group associated with a state and algebra, and state the thermal-time hypothesis in §3.1, p. 14.

Individual Audit

Absence of a Preferred Time Flow

The first statement concerns selection of a preferred flow in generally covariant theories. Its ground in the source is the structural comparison with non-generally-covariant theories, where a Hamiltonian function specifies evolution. The immediately following account of general relativity retains causal classification and worldline proper time on each solution. Hamiltonian comparison time is Time Content 1; causal classification and proper time are Time Contents 2–3; the corresponding results are 1–2. The corresponding claims are 5–6.

Postulate Identifying Thermal Time with Physical Time

The second statement expresses Connes and Rovelli’s postulate that a state-determined flow is used as physical time. The classical account uses a positive density and Poisson structure; the quantum extension uses the modular construction for a specified algebra and faithful normal state. The outer class on a fixed algebra and the self-KMS relation are mathematical results of this construction. Scale comparisons in the Gibbs and Rindler branches use known times, while the CMB–FRW correspondence is a model result cited from Reference 11. The locally reached Time Contents 11, 19 and 27 and the comparison Time Contents 1, 3, 13, 15–18, 20–23 and 26 have the respective inputs and conditions shown in the tables. Corresponding evidence comprises Claims 12–15, 17–18, 21–34 and 40–41, Transfers 6–13, and Results 8–11, 13–14, 17–29 and 34–35.
