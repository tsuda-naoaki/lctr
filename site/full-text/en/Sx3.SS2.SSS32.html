Formal Objects

The audit uses 19 time contents, 45 claims, 11 transfers and 23 results. The tables specify information relations for joint pure states, random averages, physical applications to evaporation and the conditions of each model. Page discusses information release in radiation conditional on an S-matrix description of black-hole formation and evaporation and calculates subsystem information for random joint pure states. The source examines the initial information outflow rate during adiabatic evaporation in a box, and the large-N expansion and measurement count in a two-dimensional dilatonic model. The moving-mirror example of early–late radiation correlations is distinct from the dilatonic model.

Assumptions

The bipartite calculation uses a normalized joint pure state and a random-state ensemble. The box model uses semiclassical thermal equilibrium, slow expansion, fixed total entropy and the locally stable domain. The dilatonic coordinate transformation uses the exterior region and a positive model constant; its large-N information estimate uses the source’s validity domain and fixed radiation dimension. The claim and result tables specify the conditions for the equations and their physical applications.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Claims 1–4 are the generation-initial inputs, supplying state spaces and operators, state spectra and ensembles, state data for the box model, and nontemporal data for the dilatonic model. The corresponding claims and transfers specify their relations to physical evolution, expansion, coordinates and outflow rates.

Evidence Hierarchy

Page presents model calculations, examples from earlier work, conjectures about application to radiation information, and measurement-count estimates. The source reports zero directly measured objects and zero direct-measurement predicates from measurements of its own.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count and source-evidence-conditioned local reach count are both zero, leaving 19 primitive-time roots. The source discusses information release using given formation–evaporation order, state evolution, expansion operations, physical time, null coordinates and temporal intervals.

Result Dependence

Of the 23 results, 14 depend on primitive-time roots and 9 are reachable from the source formal-evidence environment. The latter are Results 4–9, 14, 16, 20, covering static entropy and mean-information relations, the dimension pairs in Fig. 1, the nonanalytic exponential factor, and mass–entropy conditions. No result is generated from nontemporal physical inputs alone. There are 40 root–result dependence pairs, of which 19 are necessary.

Evidentiary Effect

Page expects information to emerge in radiation conditional on an S-matrix account of formation and evaporation. Applying the subsystem average to actual radiation is Page’s first guess. The box model uses slow adiabatic expansion and the locally stable domain; the dilatonic model uses the exterior coordinate domain and the valid regime for the 1/N expansion. The large-N estimate of small information holds radiation dimension fixed. Exponential growth of measurement count is Page’s estimate, and the information-transport regions are his speculation.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Formation–evaporation order, quantum evolution, box operations and information outflow rates, temporal and null coordinates of the metric, fixed emission duration, measurement order and the causal structure of the conjectured transport regions are premises of the respective arguments. None of these 19 time contents belongs to the generation closure from nontemporal physical inputs or the local closure with source formal evidence. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Actual failure of the physical-time generation conditions audited by LCTR, and consequent changes in result values, are undetermined by the source evidence.

Individual Statements

Statement evidence 1: S-matrix description and the appearance of information in radiation

Original text (English):

If black hole formation and evaporation can be described by an S matrix, information would be expected to come out in black hole radiation.

Source location

The opening of the abstract, p. 1 of Page, Information in Black Hole Radiation, arXiv:hep-th/9306083v2.

Minimal context

The conditional expectation that information appears in the radiation if black-hole formation and evaporation admit an S-matrix description.

Attribution

A statement by Don N. Page.

Statement evidence 2: Bipartite-system model assuming a random pure state

Original text (English):

Therefore, here I shall here examine a more natural model, in which the black hole and its surrounding radiation are two subsystems of a combined system which is assumed to be in a random pure state.

Source location

The model introduction preceding the subsystem-entropy calculation, p. 3 of Page, Information in Black Hole Radiation, arXiv:hep-th/9306083v2.

Minimal context

The introduction of a model treating the black hole and its surrounding radiation as two subsystems and assuming that the combined system is in a random pure state.

Attribution

A statement by Don N. Page.

Statement evidence 3: First guess in the absence of a full quantum analysis

Original text (English):

Without a full quantum analysis, we cannot really answer this question definitely. However, a reasonable first guess would be that Ir is near the average information in a subsystem of dimension m when the total system, of dimension mn, is in a random pure state.

The introduction to the mean-information estimate immediately preceding Eq. (6), p. 5 of Page, Information in Black Hole Radiation, arXiv:hep-th/9306083v2.

Minimal context

The passage explicitly states the absence of a full quantum analysis and adopts the mean subsystem information in a random pure state as a first guess.

Attribution

Individual Audit

First Statement

Page expects the initial information to emerge in radiation conditional on an S-matrix description of formation and evaporation. This expectation uses the ordering of formation and evaporation, the evolution connecting initial and final states, and the information-release process. The corresponding formal evidence is Claim 5 and Result 1.

Second Statement

After the moving-mirror example correlating early and late radiation, Page introduces a model treating the black hole and surrounding radiation as subsystems of a random joint pure state. The model defines reduced states by partial traces and calculates entropies and mean information. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 11–13 and 15–18 and Results 4–8.

Third Statement

Page’s first guess is that the actual radiation information is close to the subsystem average for random pure states. The source applies this conjecture to changing subsystem dimensions during evaporation, the information outflow rate in the adiabatic box model, and the large-N estimate at fixed radiation dimension. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 14, 16–17, 21, 28, 39 and 41, Transfers 2 and 6, and Results 6–7, 11, 13, 18 and 20.
