Result Dependencies

There are three root–result dependencies. The only necessary dependency is from Root 1 to Result 2. Result 4 is reachable from either Root 1 or Root 2 and does not require one particular root. The spectral definition and comparison in Results 1 and 3 do not presuppose either of these temporal roles.

Evidential Force

The formal evidence supports the definition of energy width, conditioned lifetime, parameter differences, and the limit of applicability for broad resonances. Defining energy width and evaluating lifetime from an inverse-lifetime quantity are different operations. Successful evaluation in the latter operation does not imply physical generation of its temporal roles. Result 4 uses the lifetime meaning of Time Content 2 to state the limit of applicability of the relation.

Result-Preservation Domain

Each result is preserved within its specific conditions in Table 641. For Results 2 and 4, the physical grounding of Time Contents 1 and 2, respectively, is additionally supplied by the LCTR generation sequence.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the grounding obligation

The two time contents are not generated from nontemporal physical inputs before use. Transfer 1 gives the lifetime representation of Time Content 2 by presupposing the inverse-lifetime role of Time Content 1. The placement determination for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This determination follows by comparing the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation with the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation condition or formation condition as an input, a formation condition, an evaluation condition, or a failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete-generation and residual-root determinations concern physical-time admissibility and are distinct from the actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. The source’s formal evidence and the LCTR audit evidence do not establish the actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure determination is “indeterminate from the primary-source evidence”; actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of the grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream reach

The reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root–result dependency relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because the actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach determination from such a failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the primary-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair scope

On the basis of structural reach, the repair targets are the corresponding time contents and the generation conditions or formation conditions required before their use. The recalculation scope is limited to results to which an independently established actual failure reaches through dependency. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their domains of validity are restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Proposition S.672 (Complete Audit of Time Use in the PDG 2023 Source on Resonances).

Both the number of generated time contents satisfying the complete determination and the number of reached time contents satisfying the local-reach determination are zero. Both time contents remain as primitive time roots. Results 1 and 3 are reachable under the source-internal evidence conditions. Result 2 requires Time Content 1, and Result 4 requires Time Content 2.

Proof.

Every edge requires at least one of Claims 5–15, so no edge applies from initial Claims 1–4 alone. In the evidence environment, the inputs for Results 1 and 3 are available. Transfer 1 and Result 2 require Time Content 1, whereas Result 4 requires Time Content 2. Thus an empty time seed set reaches no time content and reaches only Results 1 and 3. Supplying Time Content 1 produces Time Content 2 through Transfer 1 and also reaches Results 2 and 4. Supplying only Time Content 2 reaches Result 4 but neither Time Content 1 nor Result 2. ∎

Common Evidence with a Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Independent grounds

The review text and the description adjacent to Eq. (50.20) separately state the complex pole position, pole mass, pole width, threshold-conditioned resonance lifetime, difference from Breit–Wigner parameters, and absence of a direct relation between pole position and total width or lifetime for broad resonances.

Interpretive possibility 1

The source refers to the energy width that, together with pole mass, represents the complex pole position.

Boundary of evidential force 1

The quotation directly supports energy width as a pole parameter.

Interpretive possibility 2

The source gives the inverse relation between pole width and resonance lifetime for states whose relevant thresholds lie well below the resonance position.

Boundary of evidential force 2

The quotation and adjacent equation support this conditioned relation.

Interpretive possibility 3

The source states the difference between conventional Breit–Wigner parameters and pole parameters due to finite-width effects, thresholds, and background terms.

Boundary of evidential force 3

The quotation supports this parameter difference. It does not rule out agreement in a special approximation or limit.

Interpretive possibility 4

For broad resonances, the source states that there is no direct relation between pole position and total width or lifetime and that pole residues are needed to quantify decay properties.

Boundary of evidential force 4

The absence of a direct relation between width and lifetime concerns broad resonances; its domain differs from that of the threshold-conditioned relation in the second statement.

Corresponding formal evidence

This audit corresponds to Definition S.668, Definition S.667, Results 1–4 of Definition S.670, and Proposition S.672.

Within-source collation

The relation between pole width and resonance lifetime is conditioned on relevant thresholds lying well below the resonance position. The difference between Breit–Wigner parameters and pole parameters depends on finite-width effects, thresholds, and background terms. The statement that there is no direct relation concerns broad resonances; it does not exclude the conditioned relation within its stated domain.

Common Audit with a Specified Domain of Application

The pole-width definition, threshold-conditioned lifetime evaluation, parameter difference, and broad-resonance applicability limit each retain their source context and conditions. The quantitative relation used to evaluate lifetime is distinct from physical generation of its temporal roles.
