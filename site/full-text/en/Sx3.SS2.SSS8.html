Evidence Effect

The formal evidence supports the relations among the exponential-decay law, differential decay rate, half-life, and mean life. Its empirical and technical validity does not imply that unit time, elapsed time, time differentiation, half-life, and mean life have been generated from non-temporal inputs.

Result-Preservation Domain

The four results are preserved insofar as their time-content presuppositions are generated in the LCTR generation sequence and the conditions in Table 88 are satisfied.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The seven time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment is grounded in the comparison between the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation and the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or establishment condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or invalidation condition.

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

Evidence of Actual Invalidation

The complete judgment and remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from the actual invalidation of a specific condition that determines a result’s value or domain of application. The original-source formal evidence and LCTR audit evidence for this reference do not establish the actual invalidation of such a target condition. The actual-invalidation judgment is “indeterminate from original-source evidence”; actual invalidation is not inferred from the backgrounding of the grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

The reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined for this reference by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because the actual invalidation of a specific condition has not been established, the judgment of actual reach from the invalidation position to a result is “indeterminate from original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

The repair targets based on structural reach are the corresponding time content and the generation or establishment conditions required before its use. The recalculation scope is limited to results for which actual invalidation is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that invalidation position. Because actual invalidation is indeterminate in this reference, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If the condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their establishment domain is restricted. Values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Common Evidence with Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The four quotations name the law of exponential decay, the decay constant, the half-life, and the mean life. The surrounding context links these names to the decay equation and its derivation, which use unit time and elapsed time. The four quotations supply no procedure for generating unit time or elapsed time.

Independent Grounds

The four statements directly give the names of the law and quantities; the surrounding equations and derivations give the relations among the quantities.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The four quotations directly support the names and correspondences of the exponential-decay law, decay constant, half-life, and mean life. The transition from those names and their technical use to the physical generation and physical-time qualification of unit time, elapsed time, time differentiation, half-life, and mean life lies beyond the evidence-effect boundary under Definition 7.115.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The first statement corresponds to the name of the exponential-decay law treated in claim component 10 of Definition S.63 and in result 1 of Definition S.66. The second statement corresponds to the name of the decay constant used in time content 3 of Definition S.64. The third statement corresponds to the name of the half-life treated in claim component 11, time content 5, and result 3; the fourth corresponds to the name of the mean life treated in claim component 12, time content 6, and result 4.

Within-Reference Comparison

In the source, the decay constant in the exponential-decay equation and the half-life and mean life derived from it are linked by the names in the four quotations. Consistency between these names and the surrounding equations does not supply the physical generation of the time inputs they use.

Common Audit with Domain of Application

The names in the four statements are distinguished from the relations among quantities given by the surrounding equations, and the evidence-effect boundary stated above applies.
