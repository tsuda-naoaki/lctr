Formal Objects

The audit uses 21 time contents, 54 claims, 11 transfers and 24 results. The claim table specifies the geometries, matter fields, actions, measures, contours, boundary conditions and relations. Hartle and Hawking start from the Euclidean remote-past construction of ground states in ordinary quantum systems and propose defining the closed-universe ground state by integrating over compact Euclidean geometries with the prescribed three-dimensional boundary as their only boundary, together with regular matter fields. Given their claim that a closed universe has no independent time standard or natural definition of total energy, they associate the ground state with minimal excitation and high symmetry (pp. 2960–2962, 2965–2966).

In the amplitude between two boundaries, specifying their three-geometries and field values leaves proper time dependent on the four-geometries being summed over. Hartle and Hawking state that an amplitude specifying proper time requires intermediate observations that determine its value (pp. 2961–2962).

Following the general prescription, Hartle and Hawking consider a spatially closed, homogeneous isotropic geometry with positive cosmological constant and one homogeneous massless conformal field. The model’s Euclidean boundary conditions yield a Gaussian matter factor; its geometric factor is studied through the semiclassical approximation and the Wheeler–DeWitt equation. They associate the two large-radius phases with contracting and expanding branches of Lorentzian de Sitter space, and discuss matter excitations, tunneling probabilities and correlations between multiple boundaries (pp. 2967–2974).

In their conclusion, Hartle and Hawking state that the zero-volume amplitude is finite and nonzero and suggest interpreting it as the possibility of continuation through a singularity to another expansion period. Under that interpretation, classical time would not permit saying that expansion occurred after contraction. They state that the massless conformal-field ground state does not represent the observed universe and discuss the possibility that a ground state including a massive field could do so (pp. 2974–2975).

Assumptions

The general functional integral uses specified boundary geometries, regular fields, an action, a measure and an integration contour. Hartle and Hawking condition the construction on convergence of the non-positive-definite conformal direction along a suitable complex contour. The canonical description uses lapse, shift, canonical momenta and operator ordering; Hermiticity uses a domain, measure and boundary conditions. The subsequent conformal model has positive cosmological constant. Semiclassical evaluation uses specified saddles and contours, while the resonance and tunneling approximations use a small positive cosmological constant and low excitation.

Common Evidence with Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audits

First Statement

Hartle and Hawking state that a closed universe has no independent time standard and characterize its ground state through minimal excitation and high symmetry. The wave-functional construction in Section I provides the context. Claim 12 and Results 2 and 6 correspond to this statement.

Second and Third Statements

Hartle and Hawking’s two-boundary amplitude specifies three-geometries and field values on the boundaries and sums over the intervening four-geometries. Proper time depends on each four-geometry. Equation (1.8) in the second statement and Eq. (2.2) in the third give the same relation; immediately after the third statement, the source identifies the need for intermediate observations that determine proper time. Time Contents 4–7, Claims 8–10 and Result 7 correspond to these statements.

Fourth Statement

Hartle and Hawking use ℓ2=16⁢π⁢G\ell^{2}=16\pi G and ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1 to normalize the gravitational action. The statement concerns the unit convention and action normalization, corresponding to Claim 4 and Results 3 and 11.

Fifth Statement

Hartle and Hawking insert an age estimate of about 106010^{60} Planck times into the radiation-dominated model to estimate high matter excitation and the tunneling scale for a small cosmological constant. Time Content 21, Claim 46 and Result 18 correspond to this statement.

Sixth Statement

The sixth statement concerns the gravitational action with cosmological constant in Eq. (1.11). Hartle and Hawking propose a ground state by integrating over compact geometries and regular fields. Claims 4 and 21 and Result 6 correspond to this statement.

Seventh Statement

Hartle and Hawking relate the sign of the cosmological constant to compactness of regular Euclidean solutions and give spherical, flat and hyperbolic symmetric examples. Claim 23 and Result 6 correspond to this statement. The radius of the spherical metric follows from Eq. (3.2).

Eighth Statement

The potential in Eq. (7.2) has Vmax=1/(8⁢λ)V_{\max}=1/(8\lambda), differing from the quoted 1/(4⁢λ)1/(4\lambda). The two large-radius asymptotic phases represent contracting and expanding branches through their correspondence with Lorentzian de Sitter space. Time Contents 14, 15 and 17, Claims 39 and 43 and Results 14 and 16 correspond to this statement.

Ninth Statement

Hartle and Hawking suggest interpreting the zero-volume amplitude as passage through a singularity and state that, under that interpretation, classical time would not order expansion as occurring after contraction. Hartle and Hawking support that interpretation by stating that the zero-volume amplitude in their minisuperspace model is finite and nonzero. That interpretation is the condition of their claim about temporal ordering. Time Contents 17 and 19, Claims 51–52 and Results 20–21 correspond to this statement.
