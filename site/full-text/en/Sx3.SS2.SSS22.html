Individual Statements

Verbatim Original (English):

the so-called time dilation, or slowing down of moving clocks

The source location is Bailey et al., Measurements of relativistic time dilatation for positive and negative muons in a circular orbit, Nature 268, p. 301.

Verbatim Original (English):

agrees with experiment

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

In the abstract on the same page, Bailey et al. report that, for positive muons, the Einstein time-dilation factor agrees with experiment within a fractional error of 2×10−32\times 10^{-3} at 95% confidence.

Verbatim Original (English):

Assuming special relativity

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

In the same abstract, Bailey et al. describe agreement between the negative-muon rest lifetime inferred assuming SR and the previously measured positive-muon rest lifetime as confirmation of CPT invariance. On page 304, they report the ratio (τ0−−τ0+)/τ0+(\tau_{0}^{-}-\tau_{0}^{+})/\tau_{0}^{+} as −0.00105⁢(46)-0.00105(46), with a 95% confidence interval of [−0.002,−0.00013][-0.002\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak-0.00013]. In the gain-effects section on page 303, the experimenters measure apparatus gain as a function of time.

Objects of the Statements

The first statement concerns time dilation and the rate of moving clocks; the second, agreement between the Einstein time-dilation factor for positive muons and experiment; and the third, the negative-muon lifetime at rest and the CPT comparison conditional on special relativity.

Original-Source Locations

The first statement appears on p. 301 of the original source; the second and third statements appear in the abstract. The description of measuring gain effects as a function of time appears under Gain effects on p. 303.

Minimal Context Required for Interpretation

The first statement belongs to the introductory context that treats moving unstable particles as relativistic clocks. The second belongs to the report of the relative error in the Einstein time-dilation factor for positive muons at the 95%95\% confidence level. The third belongs to the context in which the negative-muon lifetime at rest is estimated with SR as an antecedent, and its agreement with the positive-muon lifetime at rest is used in a CPT comparison.

Attributed Authors of the Statements

The three statements are attributable to J. Bailey et al.

Predicates Assigned by the Statements to Their Objects

The first statement explains time dilation as moving clocks running slowly. The second states that the specified positive-muon factor agrees with experiment. The third explicitly states the SR assumption used in estimating the negative-muon lifetime at rest.

Explicit Domains of Application of the Statements

The explicit domain of the first statement is moving clocks and the circular-orbit muons of this source; that of the second is the Einstein time-dilation factor for positive muons and the reported confidence interval; and that of the third is the negative-muon estimate and CPT comparison under the SR condition.

Individual Audit

First Statement

Bailey et al. explain time dilation as the slowing of moving clocks and use lifetime measurements of radioactive samples at known velocity to test it. The measurements in this experiment concern positive and negative muons stored in circular orbits at CERN and their decay records. The experimenters obtain lifetimes in motion from the decay-electron time spectra and relate them to the Lorentz factor determined from rotation and proton magnetic-resonance frequencies and to previously measured lifetimes at rest. The formal correspondence is to Claims 31–37, Time Contents 4–10, 12, 16 and 17, Transfers 3–5, and Results 1 and 9–14.

Second Statement

Bailey et al. state that, for the positive muons in this experiment, the Einstein time-dilation factor agrees with experiment at the reported 95% confidence level. The comparison uses the positive-muon lifetime in motion, the calibrated Lorentz factor, and an independently measured positive-muon lifetime at rest. The source gives the signed fractional difference (τ0+−τ+/γ¯)/τ0+(\tau_{0}^{+}-\tau^{+}/\bar{\gamma})/\tau_{0}^{+} as (2±9)×10−4(2\pm 9)\times 10^{-4}, with a 95% confidence interval of [−1.6,2.0]×10−3[-1.6\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak 2.0]\times 10^{-3}. The formal correspondence is to Claims 31 and 33, Time Contents 4, 5, 8, 12 and 16, and Result 10.

Third Statement

Bailey et al. infer the negative-muon lifetime at rest assuming special relativity and compare it with the previously measured positive-muon lifetime at rest. The inferred negative-muon value of 2.1948(10) microseconds follows from applying the SR lifetime relation to the lifetime in motion and the calibrated Lorentz factor. The abstract describes the agreement as confirmation of CPT invariance, while page 304 reports the ratio (τ0−−τ0+)/τ0+(\tau_{0}^{-}-\tau_{0}^{+})/\tau_{0}^{+} as −0.00105⁢(46)-0.00105(46), with a 95% confidence interval of [−0.002,−0.00013][-0.002\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak-0.00013]. The formal correspondence is to Claims 31, 34 and 35, Time Contents 4, 5, 8, 12, 16 and 17, Transfer 5, and Results 11 and 12.

Auxiliary Apparatus Description

In the gain-effects section on page 303, the experimenters measure apparatus gain as a function of time using LED pulses and discriminator count ratios. This calibration uses the selected detectors, pulse times, discriminator thresholds and corresponding measurement conditions. The mean gain curve G⁢(t)G(t) and sensitivity factor S give S⁢G⁢(t)SG(t), which enters the gain correction of the decay-electron spectrum. The formal correspondence is to Claims 5, 12, 21 and 24, Time Contents 1–3, Transfers 2 and 3, and Result 5.
