Formal Objects

The audit uses 47 time contents, 82 claims, 25 transfers and 38 results. The tables specify the constructions and conditions of ordinary quantum theory, relativistic spacetime, ADM constraints, internal clocks, reference fluids, Wheeler–DeWitt interpretations, WKB time, conditional probabilities, histories and evolving constants. Kuchař examines the problems of time arising in quantization, starting from hypersurfaces, constraints and embedding representations in canonical general relativity. After comparing the background structures, preferred foliations and degrees of freedom of eleven models, he classifies ten interpretations into three frameworks: internal time, Wheeler–DeWitt theory, and interpreting quantum theory without requiring time. He presents constructions, conditions and obstacles for internal clocks and reference fluids, four-volume time, Klein–Gordon inner products, WKB time, third quantization, conditional probabilities, histories and evolving constants.

Assumptions

Each approach uses its specified canonical variables, states, constraints, boundary conditions and operator domains. Reduced evolution in an internal clock requires the domain of the selected clock, a constraint root and inner-product conditions. Heat flow and energy conditions enter the Gaussian reference-fluid construction; inner-product sign and symmetry enter the Klein–Gordon interpretation; phase branch, transverse surface and approximation order enter the WKB construction. Conditional probabilities and histories use their respective conditions on states, projectors, detectors and normalization.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Claims 1–4 are the initial inputs for generation: spatial geometry and canonical data, phase-space functions and coefficients, states and operators, boundary values, and data for detectors and coarse graining of paths. The conditions under which each approach assigns temporal meaning and operations to these data are given in the corresponding claims and transfers.

Evidence Hierarchy

Kuchař reviews earlier quantum-gravity approaches and examines their definitions, derivations, conditions and unresolved problems. The source reports zero directly measured objects and zero direct-measurement predicates from measurements of its own.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count is zero; 9 time contents are locally reachable under source-evidence conditions, leaving 38 primitive-time roots. Local reach includes the constructions of intrinsic time from logarithmic volume, extrinsic time in the linearized theory, York time and WKB time, together with quantum evolution satisfying their respective conditions. The WKB construction determines the temporal correspondence from a phase flow and a transverse surface. The corresponding time contents are 10–11, 15–16, 20–21, 32–34.

Result Dependence

Of the 38 results, 28 depend on primitive-time roots and 10 are reachable from the source formal-evidence environment. The latter are Results 4, 8, 10, 12, 14, 21–22, 26, 28, 38. No result is generated from nontemporal physical inputs alone. There are 63 root–result dependence pairs, of which 48 are necessary.

Evidentiary Effect

Kuchař presents three frameworks and ten interpretations addressing the problems of time, together with their specific constructions, conditions and obstacles. Internal-clock constructions, reduced quantum evolution, reference fluids, Klein–Gordon inner products, WKB approximations, conditional probabilities, histories and evolving constants have the conditions stated in the result table. Classical evolving constants are constructed from the specified clock sections. For self-adjoint quantum operators, Rovelli proposes an inner-product conjecture and Kuchař presents a counterconjecture.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

External instants, hypersurfaces and embeddings, asymptotic time, material-clock worldlines, the temporal parameter of a clock-projector family, temporal labels for histories and global clock sections provide temporal premises for the respective approaches. None of the 47 time contents belongs to the generation closure from nontemporal physical inputs alone, and 38 roots remain even after the local constructions under source formal evidence. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Kuchař discusses global clock obstructions, failure of the energy condition for Gaussian reference fluids with heat flow, the obstruction to the specified positive-frequency construction in quantum geometrodynamics, and the failure to retain the leading-order Schrödinger evolution at the next WKB order. Actual failure of the physical-time generation conditions audited by LCTR, and consequent changes in result values, are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Structural reach is determined by 63 root–result dependence pairs. Actual reach from the failure location of a specific physical-time generation condition to a result is undetermined by the source evidence.

Common Evidence with Specified Domain of Application

The four statements above give the original text of Kuchař 1992, the original-source location, the minimal context, and the source attribution.

Individual Audit

First Statement

Kuchař uses closure of the classical canonical constraints to explain the foliation independence of Hamilton–Jacobi evolution connecting initial and final hypersurfaces. The first statement concerns the functional nature of the time variable in this classical evolution. The corresponding formal evidence consists of Claims 9–11, Transfers 1–2 and Result 2.

Second Statement

In Section 6, Kuchař compares intrinsic, extrinsic and matter time and introduces matter fields coupled to gravity as internal-clock candidates. The Gaussian reference fluid in Section 7 uses clock fields along material worldlines to give formal quantum evolution. The energy condition for a fluid with heat flow and the dust case in which that condition is preserved have distinct domains. The specific constructions and conditions of the matter-time approach introduced by the second statement correspond to Claims 36–40, Transfers 14–15 and Results 16–17.

Third Statement

Kuchař compares four field-theoretic models and seven models with finitely many degrees of freedom in terms of background spacetime, preferred foliations, degrees of freedom and constraints. In cases such as the Barbour–Bertotti model, the model structure permits separation of clock variables. The third statement concerns the inference from such a solution of a time problem in a model to full quantum gravity. Claims 20 and 22 and Result 7 provide the corresponding formal evidence; the structures retained by each model determine its domain of application.

Fourth Statement

Kuchař classifies ten interpretations into three frameworks: internal time, Wheeler–DeWitt theory, and interpreting quantum theory without requiring time. He compares how they address the problems of time and which conditions remain unresolved. The fourth statement is Kuchař’s assessment of their lack of success as of 1992, based on that comparison. The corresponding formal evidence consists of Claims 24 and 82 and Results 10 and 38.
