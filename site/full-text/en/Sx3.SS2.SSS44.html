Individual Audit

Decoherence and branches of the many-worlds interpretation

In the first statement, Zurek states that decoherence makes the many-worlds interpretation complete. Interference suppression and pointer selection through system–environment interaction provide records stably available to observers. In Zurek’s construction, unitary premeasurement, memory retention and environment records support this account. The open issues Zurek identifies in Section IX, including the definition of a system, concern the domain of this theoretical construction. Claims 7, 9, 15, 28, 37, 48 and 50 and Results 2, 8, 21, 30, 41 and 43 correspond to the statement.

Einselection and observer memory

In the second statement, Zurek attributes the collapse known to the observer to einselection and the one-to-one correspondence between memory state and information. When system–apparatus correlations undergo einselection, the records encoded in pointer states become available for consultation by the observer. Consultation uses the interval after decoherence and before relaxation erases the information. The correspondence between observer-memory states and redundant information in the environment provides the account of outcomes and branches in the source’s existential interpretation. Claims 7, 9, 15, 28, 29 and 50 and Results 2, 8, 21, 22 and 43 correspond to the statement.

Environment records and relative objectivity

In the third statement, Zurek describes the environment recording system information as a witness in determining what exists. Observers obtain information from environment fragments correlated with the system without acting directly on it. For a specified environment partition and basis, a redundancy ratio based on locally accessible information expresses independent accessibility of records, while consultation of stable records by multiple observers establishes relative objectivity. Record formation and retention, the rate of timed copying and past inference from present records use their respective evolution, order and dynamical conditions. Claims 33–39 and Results 26–32 correspond to the statement.
