Statement object, minimal context, and attribution

The statement object is the correspondence between the time coordinate tt of the Robertson–Walker metric and the proper time of clocks accompanying the galaxies. The quotation belongs to the context immediately after Eq. (1) in §II of the original source, which introduces the Robertson–Walker metric and explains the relation between comoving galaxies and the cosmic-time coordinate. The statement is attributed to R. J. Cook and M. S. Burns.

Predicate and explicit applicability domain

The quotation specifies clocks moving with the galaxies and identifies the proper time of those clocks with the time coordinate tt. Its explicit applicability domain is the congruence of comoving galaxy observers in the Robertson–Walker model.

Individual Audit

Comoving Clocks and Cosmic Time

The clocks specified by Cook and Burns in the quotation move with the galaxies of the Robertson–Walker model. Setting the spatial coordinates constant in equation (1) gives d⁢s2=−c2⁢d⁢t2=−c2⁢d⁢τ2ds^{2}=-c^{2}dt^{2}=-c^{2}d\tau^{2}. On a future-directed worldline, d⁢τ=d⁢td\tau=dt, so τ=t+b\tau=t+b for clock zero bb. Clock readings agree with tt when the common zero b=0b=0 is chosen.

Proper time for a noncomoving clock is obtained from the same metric along that clock’s worldline. Comparing a comoving clock and a rigid-frame clock uses the same meeting event, the relative velocity of the two congruences and the local Lorentz relation.

The statement corresponds to Time Contents 1,3,4, Claims 13–14, Transfer 1 and Result 11. The proper-time relation takes cosmic time, the comoving congruence and clock readings as inputs; there are zero paths to these time contents from the generation-initial set in Proposition S.510. Comparison with noncomoving clocks corresponds to Time Contents 16–17,21–25, Claims 35–39 and Results 18–19.
