Definition S.310 (Formal objects and explicit assumptions in Barbour 1994 II).

Barbour proposes using a static wavefunction on relative configurations of the whole universe to explain temporal experience and the appearance of dynamics through record-bearing configurations. Classical histories and ephemeris time from Part I, external time and measurement in ordinary quantum theory, the stationary Mott model, and WKB solutions and the semiclassical program play different roles in his argument.

The account of configuration probabilities uses a finite-dimensional interpretive model and assigns fixed weights to configurations. Association of experience with brain configurations uses psychophysical parallelism. The Mott model uses fixed nuclei, a special zeroth approximation, a distinguished Ra atom and consistent outgoing waves at every order. Spontaneous operation of the same mechanism for configurations of the whole universe is Barbour’s conjectural extension to quantum gravity. The appendix examines the conditions required to recover first-order TDSE evolution from a general WKB state.

Evidence Hierarchy

Barbour discusses an interpretation of static quantum theory, correlations in the Mott model, configuration-space structure, cosmological conjectures and criticism of the semiclassical approximation. The source reports zero directly measured objects and zero direct-measurement predicates from measurements of its own. Configurations of measuring instruments, memories, geological records and particle tracks are objects whose appearance of time Barbour seeks to explain.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count and source-evidence-conditioned local reach count are both zero, leaving 21 primitive-time roots. Classical ephemeris time presupposes succession along a history; temporal intervals in Mott tracks presuppose a dynamical interpretation of the track; semiclassical differential time presupposes a WKB history. Experience and records associated with static configurations are also interpreted using temporal content.

Result Dependence

Of the 26 results, 18 depend on primitive-time roots. The results reachable from the source formal-evidence environment without added time roots are 1, 6, 10, 13, 15, 17, 19, 22: 8 results concerning static constructions, configuration probabilities, Mott correlations, conjugation invariance, WKB form, model conditions and configuration-space structure. There are 30 root–result dependence pairs, of which 22 are necessary.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Barbour’s proposal interprets static configurations as experienced instants, records of a past or carriers of directed histories. The Mott model uses a dynamical reading of stationary correlations, while the classical and quantum theories used for comparison employ histories, law time and initial conditions. None of the 21 time contents belongs to the generation closure from nontemporal physical inputs or the local closure with source formal evidence. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Fixed nuclei, the zeroth-order state, the outgoing convention and semiclassical approximation conditions specify the domains of the respective models and claims. Actual failure of physical-time generation conditions and consequent changes in result values are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Structural reach is determined by 30 root–result dependence pairs. Actual reach from the failure location of a specific generation condition to a result is undetermined by the source evidence.

Repair Scope

The repair targets are the temporal uses and the physical-time generation conditions required before those uses. If actual failure of a specific condition is established, recalculation concerns the results dependent on that condition.

Evidentiary Effect

Definition S.318 (Evidentiary Domain of Barbour 1994 II).

The interpretation of static configuration probabilities distinguishes possibilities, actualities and actualization of one configuration. The Mott model exhibits track-like correlations under fixed-nucleus and specified wave and scattering conditions. Conjugate wavefunctions have the same configuration probabilities; evidence for a direction of motion belongs to the dynamical reading of the whole configuration. Configuration-space geometry and hypotheses explaining the cosmic arrow and finite past have their respective result conditions. Barbour presents concentration on record-bearing configurations in general WDE solutions as an open problem and, in the appendix, criticizes the conditions used to discard the second time derivative in the semiclassical program.

Individual Statements

The Nonexistence of Time in the Kinematic Foundations

Original text:

time is truly non-existent in the kinematic foundations of both theories

The passage occurs on pp. 2875–2876, §1. Immediately before the quoted clause, Barbour writes “if, as argued in [1]”, taking the result argued in Part I as a premise for examining what remains of the structures of classical and quantum theory. In the same introduction, he contrasts ephemeris time determined from two configurations in a classical history with time inferred from records within one static configuration.

Time Capsules

Original text:

Such configurations are called time capsules

The passage occurs in the abstract on p. 2875. Barbour proposes that the appearance of time, dynamics and history is encoded as apparently consistent records within experienced static configurations of the universe, and calls such configurations time capsules. In §4, p. 2884, he defines a time capsule as a static configuration containing structures that suggest mutually consistent records of past processes governed by laws.

Individual Audit

The Nonexistence of Time in the Kinematic Foundations

The first statement expresses a kinematic premise referring to Part I. Barbour uses ephemeris time along configuration histories in classical theory and proposes explaining the appearance of time from records within one configuration in quantum theory. The histories, ephemeris time and local proper time of the former correspond to Time Contents 3–5; the experience, past records and dates of the latter correspond to Time Contents 6–8. The static equation and configuration construction form Result 1, the classical references form Results 2–3, and the interpretation of experience and records forms Results 7–8. The corresponding claims are 5–6, 9–10, 13–17 and 43–44.

Time Capsules

The time capsule in the second statement follows Barbour’s definition: a static structure suggests records of lawful past processes. In the Mott model, fixed nuclei, a specified zeroth-order state and consistent outgoing waves at every order support track-like correlations. The course, direction and temporal intervals of a track belong to the dynamical interpretation of that configuration. Barbour shows that conjugating the entire wavefunction gives the same configuration probabilities and locates evidence for motion direction in the whole configuration. Analogous concentration in WDE solutions for the entire universe is the conjecture examined in §8. The corresponding evidence comprises Claims 16, 22–28, 33 and 35–39, Transfers 3–5, and Results 8, 10–14, 17 and 20–24.
