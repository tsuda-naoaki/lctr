Assumptions

Smolin’s temporal relationalism takes time, causation, energy and momentum as fundamental, while treating space, spacetime and locality as emergent. Event updating in causal sets, local geometric constructions from momentum space, coarse graining to macroscopic spacetime, stochastic hidden variables and quantum recovery from real ensembles each have distinct conditions.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Nontemporal candidate data in Claims 1–7—event carriers, energy and momentum labels, relations, records, states, configurations and geometric coefficients—form the generation-initial set. Causal order, temporal updating and clock interpretations belong to the time contents of the claims that use them.

Downstream Reach

Individual Statements

Statement 1

Original text (English):

I take the opposite view, that time is fundamental.

The source location is Lee Smolin, Temporal Relationalism, arXiv:1805.12468v1, p. 3, Section 1. The minimal context is the passage that states Smolin’s position after setting out the opposition between the fundamentality and emergence of time in relational theories.

The statement object is Smolin’s position on the fundamental status of time. The predicate is that time is fundamental. The explicit domain of application is the discussion in Section 1 that contrasts the fundamentality and emergence of time in relational theories.

Statement 2

Original text (English):

the aspect of time I assert is irreducible is its activity as the generator of novel events from present events

The source location is p. 3, Section 1 of the same source. The minimal context is the passage that identifies which aspect of time is taken to be fundamental and introduces the thick present and the addition of novel events.

The statement object is the event-generating activity treated as an irreducible aspect of time. The predicate is that the irreducible aspect of time is its activity of generating novel events from present events. The explicit domain of application is the construction in Section 1 that discusses the thick present and the addition of novel events.

Statement 3

Original text (English):

Instead, we propose that laws are only meaningful for limited ranges of space and time, and that on cosmological scales, laws evolve.

The source location is p. 17, Section 3.1 of the same source. The minimal context is the passage that rejects absolute, immutable, deterministic laws and states their ranges of application and cosmological evolution.

The statement object is the range within which physical laws are meaningful and their evolution on cosmological scales. The predicate is that Smolin proposes limited ranges of space and time in which laws are meaningful and evolution of the laws themselves on cosmological scales. The explicit domain of application is Section 3.1, which criticizes immutable laws and proposes limited domains of application and the evolution of laws.

Statement 4

Original text (English):

The state of the art concerning models in which classical spacetime emerges from an underlying dynamical quantum geometry is that those that succeed either have a fixed boundary, or assume a prior temporal or causal order.

The source location is p. 18, Section 3.2 of the same source. The minimal context is the passage that considers whether spacetime is fundamental or emergent and states the ordering between temporal or causal order and the emergence of spacetime.

The statement object is the condition under which models deriving classical spacetime from a dynamical quantum geometry have succeeded. The predicate is Smolin’s assessment that the successful models considered in the source either have a fixed boundary or assume a prior temporal or causal order. The explicit domain of application is Section 3.2, which discusses the state of models for the emergence of classical spacetime.

Common Evidence with a Specified Domain of Application

Source attribution

All four statements are attributed to Lee Smolin.

Individual Audit

Fundamental time and the generation of novel events

In Statement 1, Smolin expressly adopts fundamental time; in Statement 2, he specifies its irreducible aspect as the activity of generating novel events from present events. These statements correspond to Claims 11 and 13 and to Claims 19–20 and 39–40 describing event updates. Updating uses causal order, causal pasts and update stages, which form premises of Transfer 2 and Results 9–10 and 13.

Domains of physical laws and evolution of the laws themselves

In Statement 3, Smolin proposes limited spatiotemporal domains in which laws are meaningful and evolution of the laws themselves on cosmological scales. These correspond to Claims 29–30 and Result 14, using the validity domains and ordering of law changes in Time Content 8. Specific research constructions for evolving laws belong respectively to cosmological natural selection, the principle of precedence and universality of metalaws described in Section 6.

Conditions for emergent-spacetime models

Statement 4 gives Smolin’s assessment of the successful emergent-spacetime models considered in the source: they have a fixed boundary or assume a prior temporal or causal order. Fixed boundary and prior order are the two alternatives of the disjunction. The relation between temporal or causal order and macroscopic spacetime corresponds to Claims 16, 41 and 65 and Results 16–17 and 28.

Common Audit with a Specified Domain of Application

The temporal premises of each statement and their connections to results are given by the finite representations in Definitions S.618, S.622 and S.623. Smolin’s event generation extends causal histories; Proposition S.626 gives the generation judgment for the time contents used by that construction.
