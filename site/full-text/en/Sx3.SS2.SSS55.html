Conditions

Transfer 1 integrates the line element determined by the metric and timelike worldline. The choice of coordinate time and the value of proper time are distinct objects. Transfers 2 and 3 are separate clock-comparison and ranging examples; Transfers 5–7 are three separate routes illustrating relative evolution. The ranging paragraph does not specify a concrete conversion formula.

Transfer 9 specifies a regime in which the constraint has the form of Eq. (3) for a partial observable and its conjugate momentum. Writing the canonical coordinates as q0,p0q^{0}\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak p_{0}, with H=p0+H0⁢(qi,pi,q0)H=p_{0}+H_{0}(q^{i}\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak p_{i}\mathchar 44\relax% \allowbreak q^{0}), gives {q0,H}=1\{q^{0}\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak H\}=1 along the constraint-generated flow. This identifies q0q^{0} as nonrelativistic time in that regime. For an approximate form, the identification has the same approximation domain. Transfer 10 uses the pendulum’s clock reading and special Hamiltonian form.

Transfer 11 obtains −β−1⁢ln⁡ρ0=H0−β−1⁢ln⁡N-\beta^{-1}\ln\rho_{0}=H_{0}-\beta^{-1}\ln N from the normalized Gibbs distribution ρ0=N⁢e−β⁢H0\rho_{0}=Ne^{-\beta H_{0}}. The additive constant leaves the Hamiltonian flow unchanged; a multiplicative constant fixes its unit. Transfer 12 constructs the Hamiltonian vector field of Hρ=−ln⁡ρH_{\rho}=-\ln\rho from a statistical phase space Γ\Gamma with a symplectic structure and a positive smooth density on it. Where constraint reduction is required, the construction uses a regular region in which the reduced phase space has this structure. Its flow is defined on the domain of existence of its solutions; extension to all parameter values requires completeness of the vector field. The clock condition in Transfer 13 is that the device reading increase linearly along this flow. Transfer 14 belongs to the radiation-filled covariant cosmological model.

In Transfer 15, spectral calculus on the support space (ker⁡ρ)⟂(\ker\rho)^{\perp} of the positive density operator ρ\rho defines the self-adjoint operator Hρ=−ln⁡ρH_{\rho}=-\ln\rho and the unitary group ei⁢tρ⁢Hρe^{it_{\rho}H_{\rho}}. In infinite dimension, HρH_{\rho} may be unbounded but is self-adjoint on its dense domain, and its exponential is unitary on the entire support space. Unitary conjugation acts on bounded operators on the support space; restricting observables to a subalgebra requires that this action preserve the subalgebra. In Transfer 16, the von Neumann algebra is the weak operator closure of the observable algebra in the chosen representation, equipped with a faithful normal state. The cyclic separating vector in the GNS representation determines the Tomita modular group. In Transfer 17, state independence belongs to the outer equivalence class of modular automorphisms modulo inner automorphisms. For a finite matrix algebra, automorphisms are inner and this outer flow is trivial. An admissible constant classical density gives a zero Hamiltonian vector field, while the maximally mixed state ρ=I/d\rho=I/d in dimension dd gives αtρ⁢(A)=A\alpha_{t_{\rho}}(A)=A. Conditions for distinguishable change serving as a clock are therefore additional to conditions for the existence of a flow.

Grounds

Results 8 and 9 use the general formalism of partial observables and correlations in Section IV.B. Results 11–16 are formal constructions of constraints, orbits, and operators. Result 10 applies the correlation formalism to subsequent measurements. In the pendulum example, β\beta is the clock reading and α\alpha the elongation; the source writes the quantum-oscillator propagator with arguments t,t′t\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak t^{\prime}.

The orbit construction in Appendix A uses the kernel of the presymplectic form on a regular constraint surface. Where d⁢H≠0dH\neq 0 and a≠0a\neq 0, replacing HH by a⁢HaH preserves the characteristic direction on the constraint surface. For H2H^{2}, the differential vanishes on the same zero set, so equality of zero sets alone does not guarantee equality of characteristic directions. In the pendulum-state representation (A,ϕ)(A\mathchar 44\relax\allowbreak\phi), all phases at A=0A=0 describe the same motion.

The range of the group-averaging integral and operator domains depend on the system. The source’s P:S→S′P:S\to S^{\prime} includes distributional operators, and its discrete-event probabilities use the normalization ⟨s|P|s⟩=1\langle s|P|s\rangle=1. The quantum structure is presented as tentative, with repeated measurements listed as an unresolved issue.

The Gibbs–KMS branch uses the convention αt⁢(A)=ei⁢t⁢H0⁢A⁢e−i⁢t⁢H0\alpha_{t}(A)=e^{itH_{0}}Ae^{-itH_{0}}, for which Eq. (23) gives WA⁢B⁢(t)=WB⁢A⁢(−t−i⁢β)W_{AB}(t)=W_{BA}(-t-i\beta). Mathematical existence of the Tomita flow, state independence of the outer flow, physical-time identification under the thermal-time hypothesis, and the thermodynamic account of experienced temporal features each have their own conditions.

Evidential hierarchy

The source discusses relational mechanics and constructions recovering time. It reports no new quantitative measurements. Two clocks, planetary ranging, a binary pulsar, pendulum/pulse comparison, and clock readings serve as physical or operational examples. Time identification from a special Hamiltonian form or statistical state is a construction within the specified formal or approximation domain. The thermal-time hypothesis and thermodynamic origin of experienced temporal features are presented as the author’s physical interpretation. Agreement with Friedmann time is a correspondence in the radiation cosmological model cited by the source.

Individual statements

Statement 1

Original text (English):

I summarize here this point of view, explaining why I think that in a fundamental description of nature we must “forget time”, and how this can be done in the classical and in the quantum theory.

The original-source location is Carlo Rovelli, Forget Time, arXiv:0903.3832v3, p. 1, abstract. The minimal context is a sentence in the abstract that states a strategy for constructing, for quantum gravity, a physical description in which time has no role and then summarizes a formalism that treats dependent and independent variables on the same footing.

The statement objects are the role of time in a fundamental description of nature and the construction policy for classical and quantum descriptions that do not privilege time. Its predicate is that a fundamental description must “forget time” and that such a description can be constructed in both classical and quantum theory. Its explicit applicability domain is the relational formalism supplied by the original source for relations among partial observables, unparametrized classical mechanics, constrained systems, and quantum events.

Statement 2

Original text (English):

The notion of time familiar to us may then be reconstructed in special physical situations, or within an approximation, as is the case for a number of familiar physical quantities that disappear when moving to a deeper level of description (for instance: the “surface of a liquid” disappears when going to the atomic level, or “temperature” is a notion that makes sense only in certain physical situations and when there are enough degrees of freedom.)

The original-source location is p. 1, Section I of the same source. The minimal context is a sentence that follows the proposal to construct a theory of quantum gravity in which the concept of time does not appear and states the conditions for reconstructing a concept of time by using the surface of a liquid and temperature as analogies.

The statement object is the reconstructibility of the familiar concept of time after it loses its privileged role in the fundamental description. Its predicate is that the concept of time may be reconstructed in special physical situations or within an approximation. Its explicit applicability domain comprises the approximate nonrelativistic time treated by the original source, selected statistical states, macroscopic descriptions with sufficiently many degrees of freedom, and the validity conditions of the respective recovery branches.

Statement 3

Original text (English):

Time is, that is to say, the expression of our ignorance of the full microstate.

The original-source location is p. 8, Section VI of the same source, in the paragraph stating the thermal-time hypothesis. The minimal context is the sentence immediately following the thermal-time hypothesis, which calls the thermal time of a statistical state selected by macroscopic variables “time.”

The statement object is the relation, under the thermal-time hypothesis, between time and a coarse-grained informational state relative to the complete microstate. Its predicate is that time is an expression of ignorance of the full microstate. Its explicit applicability domain is the thermal-time construction using selected macroscopic variables, an observable algebra, a statistical state, Gibbs and KMS flows, and Tomita modular flow.

Individual audits

Forgetting time in the fundamental description

Statement 1 proposes constructing a fundamental classical and quantum description through relations among partial observables, without a privileged independent time. Its grounds are the constructions of event space, the correlation triple, the constraint surface, presymplectic orbits, quantum events, and transition amplitudes. General correlation, constraint, and operator constructions are reachable under the source formal-evidence environment; clock examples and applications to subsequent measurements use clock readings and measurement order. The corresponding formal evidence is Results 1–17.

Reconstruction in special situations or approximations

Statement 2 uses the surface of a liquid and temperature as analogies to state that the familiar concept of time may be reconstructed in special physical situations or within an approximation. Its grounds are the identification of nonrelativistic time by a special Hamiltonian form, reconstruction of the Hamiltonian from a Gibbs distribution, and classical and quantum thermal flows obtained from statistical states. Time identification belongs to each construction’s formal or approximation domain; a thermal-clock reading additionally requires linear increase along the flow. The corresponding formal evidence is Results 18–23 and Transfers 9 and 11–17.

Time as an expression of ignorance of the complete microscopic state

In the context of the thermal-time hypothesis, Statement 3 identifies physical time with the thermal time of the statistical state fixed by selected macroscopic variables. Its grounds are the source’s constructions of a logarithmic generator and thermal flow from a statistical state, and of modular flow from a state and observable algebra. The source explicitly identifies this as a statement about macroscopic descriptions under incomplete microscopic knowledge, rather than about the fundamental mechanical structure of the world (Section VI, p. 8). The formal flow constructions and the hypothesis identifying their flow as physical time correspond, respectively, to Results 19, 20, 22, and 23 and to Result 21.
