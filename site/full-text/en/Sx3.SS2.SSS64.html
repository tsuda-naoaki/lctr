Formal Objects

The audit uses 34 time contents, 52 physical and formal claims, 12 transfers and 32 results.

Assumptions

The BIPM defines the SI by fixing the numerical values of seven defining constants and realizes units through physical quantity relations and experiments. Exact defining values and realization uncertainty concern different objects. The second refers to the specified caesium-133 transition frequency and applies as a unit of proper time. Clock comparisons use signal propagation and delays, a synchronization convention and relativistic corrections appropriate to the domain. TAI computation uses a clock ensemble, long-term stability, calibration against standards, reference-surface corrections and frequency steering when necessary. UTC and legal time follow integral-second offsets and national time-offset conventions respectively.

Nontemporal Physical Inputs

Candidate values, atomic and event carriers, count records, apparatus configurations, spatial paths and reference-surface shapes in Claims 1–8 form the generation-initial set. Periods, clock readings, synchronization, temporal comparisons and correction values belong to the time contents of the claims that use them.

Downstream Reach

Repair Scope

Repair concerns the time contents used by each result and the physical-time generation conditions required before their use. If actual failure of a specific condition is established, recalculation concerns the results dependent on that condition.

Individual Audit

Unit convention for the second

Statement 1 gives the convention that the second is the SI unit of time. The immediately following definition fixes the numerical value of the unperturbed ground-state hyperfine transition frequency of caesium 133 at 9 192 631 770 Hz. The relation between this frequency and the duration of its periods corresponds to Claims 15–16, Transfer 1 and Results 6 and 8.

Proper-time application and local realization

Statement 2 applies the second as a unit of proper time in general relativity. The same section specifies relativistic frequency corrections when signals from clocks at different locations are combined. The second mise en pratique specifies a local comoving domain, shifts due to atomic motion and environment, and a reference point at accuracies where field nonuniformity within the apparatus matters. These correspond to Claims 18–19 and 30–31, Transfers 2 and 5 and Results 9 and 13.

Ensemble-based TAI and coordinate time scales

The 1980 supplement in Statement 3 gives TAI in a geocentric reference frame the SI second realized on the rotating geoid as its scale unit. The establishing body in the 1970 definition was the BIH; responsibility for TAI moved to the BIPM in 1988. TAI computation in the second mise en pratique uses clock comparisons, a weighted ensemble, calibration against standards, reference-surface corrections and steering when necessary. Claims 36–43, Transfers 8–10 and Results 16 and 19 express this operational scale and its relations to ideal TT, TCG and UTC under their respective conditions.

Common Audit with a Specified Domain of Application

The temporal premises of each statement and their connections to results are specified in Definitions S.630, S.633 and S.634. Proposition S.635 gives the generation judgment for time contents used in unit definitions, clock realizations and time-scale formation.
