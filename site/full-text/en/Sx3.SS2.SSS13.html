Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

The 41 time contents audited in this reference are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already-established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment follows by matching the use of each time content in a law operation or result derivation against the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or validity condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

Downstream Reach

Downstream Reach

For this reference, the reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from that failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Repair Scope

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the objects of repair are the corresponding time contents and the generation or validity conditions required before their use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual failure is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that failure location. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual Statements

Time and Space

Verbatim original (English):

time cannot be separated from space. Space and time are part of a single entity, spacetime.

The source location is Taylor and Wheeler, Spacetime Physics, 2nd ed., Chapter 1, §1.2, printed p. 7. The minimal context is the passage explaining that temporal and spatial separations differ across inertial frames while the value of the spacetime interval agrees. The statement is attributable to Taylor and Wheeler.

Biological Clock and Aging

Verbatim original (English):

our biological clock, by which we age, and all other good clocks carried along will tick away in concord with it

The source location is Chapter 4, §4.5, Flight Plan, printed p. 124, in the same book. The minimal context is the explanation of a flight plan that determines the elapsed time shown by the traveler’s wristwatch. The statement is attributable to Taylor and Wheeler.

Clocks in Another Inertial Frame and Time-Stretch Vocabulary

Verbatim original (English):

Each observes less elapsed time on clocks in the other frame than on his or her own clocks (time stretching or time dilation).

The source location is Chapter 4, §4.9, printed p. 131, in the same book. The minimal context is the explanation in which each observer compares clocks in the other of two inertial frames in relative motion. The statement is attributable to Taylor and Wheeler.

Common Evidence with Specified Domain of Application

Individual Audit

Interpretability

The first statement explains the relation between frame-dependent temporal and spatial separations and an invariant spacetime interval in terms of a single spacetime. The second states that the traveler’s wristwatch, biological clock and good clocks carried along advance in concord. The third describes each relatively moving inertial frame comparing clocks selected according to its own simultaneity with clocks in the other frame, calling the smaller elapsed time in the other frame time stretching or time dilation.

Independent Grounds

The grounds for the three statements are spacetime-interval invariance, the worldline and proper time of the Canopus round trip, and clock comparison between inertial frames. The correspondence between biological aging and wristwatch time is asserted in Sections 4.5 and 5.6; Section 5.7 discusses clock mechanisms’ sensitivity to acceleration and the conditions for good clocks. Section 4.10 reports aircraft atomic-clock comparisons, particle-lifetime measurements and thermal-motion effects on nuclear-clock frequency as experimental comparisons.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first statement uses temporal separation between events; the second uses proper time along a worldline and aging; the third uses each inertial frame’s simultaneity and clock readings. Their physical use requires the generation structure corresponding to each time content to hold. The evidence-effect boundary of Definition 7.115 lies at the passage from the interval, worldline and clock-comparison relations supporting each statement to this establishment.

Corresponding Formal Evidence

The first statement corresponds to time contents 1 and 16 in Table 122, claims 13–17 in Table 121, and results 1–2 in Table 128. The second corresponds to time contents 8 and 23, claims 48–56 and result 15. The third corresponds to time content 25, claims 57–58 and 69–71, and result 16. The result-dependence table and Proposition S.118 specify the premises and minimal-root supports of each result.

Within-Source Comparison

Chapter 1 integrates time and space into spacetime, while page 18 describes their different properties. Sections 4.5 and 5.6 relate wristwatch time to aging, and Section 5.7 states the conditions for the clocks used to measure it. Reciprocal time dilation in Section 4.9 is specified by each frame’s clock pairs and simultaneity. These accounts connect the coordinate description of events, proper time along paths and application to physical processes.
