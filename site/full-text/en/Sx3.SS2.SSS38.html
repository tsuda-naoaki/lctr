Evidence Hierarchy

Photometric, spectral and observation-time records form the observational material. Type Ia classification confidence, redshift, peak magnitudes and stretch factors are obtained by identification, calibration and fitting of that material. Cosmological parameters, present acceleration and cosmic age are inferred from the likelihood for corrected magnitudes and redshifts and the specified cosmological model. The appendix’s extinction-estimation bias is a mathematical calculation for assumed probability distributions.

Generation Judgment

The complete LCTR generation count and source-evidence-conditioned local-reach count are both zero, leaving 15 primitive-time roots. Transfers to light-curve phase, stretch and width and to model acceleration and age take observation epochs, template time, cosmic time or Hubble rate as inputs.

Result Dependence

Of the 25 results, 24 depend on primitive-time roots. Result 23, concerning extinction priors and bias in individual estimates, is reached without additional time roots as the statistical relation in Appendix Equations (A1)–(A4). There are 122 root–result dependence pairs, of which 102 are necessary.

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Perlmutter et al. use observation epochs, light-curve phases and widths, astronomical velocities, propagation, expansion history and ages in the analysis. Temporal-width conversions and model calculations take existing time contents as inputs, leaving all 15 as primitive-time roots. The LCTR placement judgment is “backgrounded by primitive-time inputs.”

Evidence of Actual Failure

Sample selection, calibration, corrections, covariance, errors, prior domain and model family specify the conditions of each result. The source tests changes in estimates and robustness under variations of these conditions. Actual failure of a specific audited physical-time generation condition and the resulting change in result values are undetermined by the source evidence.

Downstream Reach

Structural reach is determined by 122 root–result dependence pairs. Actual reach from failure of a specific generation condition to a result is undetermined by the source evidence.

Repair Scope

The repair targets are each temporal use and the physical-time generation conditions required before that use. If actual failure of a specific condition is established, recalculation concerns the results dependent on it.

Individual Statements

Temporal-width correction of the light-curve template

Original text:

For the supernovae discussed in this paper, the template must be time-dilated by a factor 1+z1+z before fitting to the observed lightcurves to account for the cosmological lengthening of the supernova timescale (Goldhaber et al. 1995; Leibundgut et al. 1996a; Riess et al. 1997a).

Source location: Section 2, between Equations (1) and (2). Perlmutter et al. distinguish intrinsic stretch from cosmological temporal dilation by 1+z1+z and apply the latter to the template before fitting the observed light curve.

Stretch factor common to two bands

Original text:

A similar relation corrects the V band lightcurve, with the same stretch factor in both bands.

Source location: Section 2, immediately after Equation (1). Perlmutter et al. also apply the width–luminosity correction to the V band and use the same intrinsic stretch factor in both B and V.

Inconsistency with a flat Λ=0\Lambda=0 cosmology

Original text:

The data are strongly inconsistent with a Λ=0\Lambda=0 flat cosmology, the simplest inflationary universe model.

Source location: Abstract. Perlmutter et al. correct the light curves of 42 high-redshift supernovae and a low-redshift sample and report cosmological parameter distributions and flat-conditional estimates from the specified FRW likelihood.

Common Evidence with Specified Scope

The table gives the objects, predicates and domains of the three statements.

Individual Audit

Inputs to temporal-width correction

The first statement gives Perlmutter et al.’s procedure of dilating the template width by 1+z1+z before fitting an observed light curve. Its grounds are the timed photometry, redshifts, template and stretch fitting in Section 2, using Time Contents 1–5. Correspondence between existing temporal widths in the specified sample and template conditions this procedure. Claims 6–8, Transfer 1 and Results 2–3 correspond to the statement.

Shared condition for two bands

The second statement gives Perlmutter et al.’s use of the same intrinsic stretch factor in the B- and V-band width–luminosity corrections. Its ground is the two-band template fit in Section 2, which uses the stretch factor determined from the B band also in V. Its domain is the source’s sample, template and width–luminosity relation. Claim 8, Time Contents 3–5 and Result 3 correspond to the statement.

Statistical inconsistency with the flat model

The third statement is Perlmutter et al.’s statistical conclusion that their corrected supernova data are strongly inconsistent with a flat Λ=0\Lambda=0 cosmology. Its ground is the model comparison using the samples, corrections, errors and FRW likelihood in Sections 3 and 5. The specified model family, prior domain and error conditions define the domain of this inconsistency and the support for a positive cosmological constant. Claims 19–22 and 34 and Results 7–9 and 24 correspond to the statement.
