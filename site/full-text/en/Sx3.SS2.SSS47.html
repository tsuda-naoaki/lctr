Assumptions

Kiefer adopts quantum universality and canonical quantum gravity for a closed universe. The continuous model uses a hyperbolic Wheeler–DeWitt equation; the loop model uses the specified difference equation and operator representation. Semiclassical time uses slowly evolving gravity and a Born–Oppenheimer-type approximation. The arrow construction uses perturbation potentials, a subsystem split and a small-scale product boundary. Recollapse and black-hole consequences use symmetric cosmological boundaries and the validity domains of the respective branches.

Evidence Hierarchy

Kiefer discusses quantum-cosmological constructions and model and approximation analyses from the cited literature. This source reports zero newly acquired direct measurements. The black-hole consequences in Section 5 depend on a recollapsing universe and symmetric boundary conditions. The source calls for a full theory of quantum gravity and experimental tests for a final answer.

Generation Judgment

Judgment: complete LCTR generation 0; source-evidence-conditioned local reach 4; remaining primitive-time roots 18. The locally reached contents are continuous intrinsic time, discrete intrinsic time, entanglement and reduced-entropy growth along the intrinsic scale, and the time direction defined by that growth.

Individual statements

Object of statements

The external time parameter at the most fundamental level, intrinsic time given by the scale factor, discrete intrinsic time given by an integer label, and the failure boundary of a semiclassical approximation along a classical trajectory.

Original (English)

Quantum gravity does not contain an external time parameter at the most fundamental level.

intrinsic time, given in quantum cosmology by the scale factor

The number n can be interpreted as playing the role of ‘discrete intrinsic time’.

one cannot find a narrow wave packet all along the classical trajectory – the semiclassical approximation must breakdown somewhere

Source location

The first and second quotations are in arXiv:gr-qc/0502016v1, p. 6, §4; the third and fourth are on p. 3, §2.

Minimal context

The first and second quotations belong to the context in which an intrinsic-time role is assigned to the scale factor in quantum cosmology starting from quantum gravity without external time. The third belongs to the discussion of the difference equation of loop quantum cosmology, the integer label and operator eigenvalues, and the large-integer limit. The fourth belongs to the discussion of the turning point and domain of validity of the semiclassical approximation when a wave packet represents a recollapsing classical trajectory.

Attribution

All four quotations are attributed to the text by Claus Kiefer.

Content stated directly by the quotations

The first quotation states the absence of an external time parameter at the most fundamental level, and the second states that the scale factor gives intrinsic time. The third states that the integer label can be interpreted as discrete intrinsic time; the fourth states that the semiclassical approximation cannot be maintained along the entire classical trajectory.

Explicit domain of application

The first quotation is limited to quantum gravity at the most fundamental level; the second and third to the adopted quantum-cosmological models; and the fourth to the semiclassical wave-packet description of a recollapsing model.

Evidence provenance

All four quotations belong to Kiefer, Quantum Cosmology and the Arrow of Time, arXiv:gr-qc/0502016v1, pp. 3,6.

Individual Audit

Absence of External Time at the Most Fundamental Level

The first quotation rests on the closed-universe constraint H^⁢Ψ=0\hat{H}\Psi=0 and the theory’s state dependence on three-dimensional quantities. It corresponds to Claim 11 and Result 4. Time-reparametrization invariance corresponds to Claim 12 and Time Content 5; the construction using a scale variable as intrinsic time corresponds to Claim 13.

Intrinsic Time through the Scale Factor

The second quotation rests on the hyperbolic principal part of quantum geometrodynamics and the scale variable of the specified minisuperspace model. With Claim 13 as evidence, Transfer 2 yields Time Content 6. Applications to recollapsing wave packets or semiclassical time additionally use the boundary, branch and approximation conditions of Results 11 and 26.

Discrete Intrinsic Time through an Integer Label

The third quotation rests on the eigenvalue representation of p^\hat{p} in source Equation (3) and the specified loop-cosmological difference equation. With Claim 15 as evidence, Transfer 3 yields Time Content 7. Recovery of a continuous equation at large integer values, the factor-ordering-dependent dynamical initial condition, and continuation through the zero label follow the respective conditions of Claims 16–18.

Breakdown Boundary of the Semiclassical Approximation

The fourth quotation states that, in a recollapsing model whose small-scale initial data contain both incident and returning packets, a narrow-packet approximation cannot hold along the entire classical trajectory. It corresponds to Time Contents 6 and 8, Claim 20 and Result 11. Source Section 4 also discusses breakdown in the turning region, interference between quasiclassical components, vanishing of the wave function at large scale and the limit of classical observer continuity. These follow the conditions of Claims 38–40 and Result 27.

Common Audit with Specified Domain

The results of the four statements concern the closed-universe constraint, constructions of continuous and discrete intrinsic time, and the semiclassical validity domain of the recollapsing model. Local reach of intrinsic time and complete generation from physical inputs are given in Proposition S.468.
