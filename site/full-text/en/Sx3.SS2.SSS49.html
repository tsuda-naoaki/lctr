Definition S.481 (Formal Objects and Explicit Assumptions of Ryu–Takayanagi 2006).

The source uses the AdS/CFT correspondence and dimension-dependent Newton constants to obtain entropy from the area of a static minimal surface anchored on ∂A\partial A. It uses eρ0≫1e^{\rho_{0}}\gg 1 in the global branch, β/L≪1\beta/L\ll 1 in the finite-temperature CFT2 branch, and a large interval with a correlation-length IR cap in the massive branch. The higher-dimensional branches use the specified strip and disk regions, UV cutoffs and a gravitational description of the strongly coupled theory. The coefficient comparison with free Yang–Mills theory includes the estimate that a gauge field contributes as two real scalars. Complementary-entropy equality assumes a globally pure state; the horizon thermal contribution uses a large strip width and the finite-temperature metric.

Grounds of establishment

Results 1, 4, 5 and 12 give the reduced state and entropy, central charge, cutoff dictionary, complementary equality for a globally pure state, and subadditivity. Results 2, 3, 6–11 and 13–16 use the static area prescription and the metric, boundary conditions, cutoff and temperature scale of the respective branch. The signed multiple-interval combination is Equation (2.7), which the source obtains from a cited CFT result. The free-theory coefficient ratio is an estimate with a gauge-field approximation; the thermal contribution is the near-horizon calculation at large strip width.

Evidence hierarchy

The source supplies geometric calculations, agreement with known CFT results, scaling with degrees of freedom, and comparisons of strongly coupled and free theories. It has zero new direct-measurement objects and predicates. The higher-dimensional comparison concerns functional forms and coefficients supported by the specified gravitational description and free-theory estimate.

Result-preservation domain

Definition S.490 (Result-Preservation Conditions for Ryu–Takayanagi 2006).

Each result holds within the corresponding AdS/CFT dictionary, state conditions, boundary anchoring, metric, cutoff, dimensional and coupling conditions, and approximations. Complementary entropies are equal for a globally pure state; the equality does not hold generically at finite temperature. A result with temporal prerequisites is preserved as a conditional result when an LCTR generation sequence establishes the required coordinate, order, scale, period and slicing correspondences before use and its nontemporal conditions are also retained.

Allocation of the grounding obligation

Placement of grounding obligations

The nine time contents audited in this source are not generated from nontemporal physical inputs before use but are accepted as already established time inputs. The placement judgment for their physical-time grounding obligations is ”backgrounded by primitive time input.”

Evidence of actual failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete-generation judgment and remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification and are distinct from actual failure of a specific condition governing a result value or domain of application. Neither the formal evidence of the original source nor the LCTR audit evidence establishes actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is ”indeterminate from the original-source evidence”; actual failure is not inferred from backgrounding alone.

Downstream reach

Downstream reach

The extent of reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root–result dependency relation and minimal primitive-time-root support reconstructed in this division. This is structural reach. Because actual failure of a specific condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from a failure location to a result is ”indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair scope

Repair scope

The repair targets given by structural reach are the corresponding time contents and the generation or establishment conditions required before their use. Recalculation is limited to results reached by dependence from a failure location after actual failure has been established independently. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If the condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their domain of establishment is restricted. Values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual statements

Statement 1

Original (English):

A holographic derivation of the entanglement entropy in quantum (conformal) field theories is proposed from AdS/CFT correspondence.

The source location is Shinsei Ryu and Tadashi Takayanagi, Holographic Derivation of Entanglement Entropy from AdS/CFT, arXiv:hep-th/0603001v2, p. 1, abstract. Its minimal context is the opening sentence of the abstract, which summarizes the AdS/CFT correspondence, the area of a minimal surface, and the analogy with the Bekenstein–Hawking formula.

The object of the statement is a proposal for a holographic derivation of entanglement entropy in quantum (conformal) field theories based on the AdS/CFT correspondence. Its predicate is that a prescription is proposed for deriving entanglement entropy from the area of a bulk minimal surface. Its explicit domain of application is the static AdS/CFT setting treated by the original source, the boundary subsystem and its boundary, the corresponding static minimal surface, and the cases compared within the original source.

Statement 2

Original (English):

Here, we present a simple proposal on this issue in the light of AdS/CFT duality.

The source location is p. 1, Section I of the same source. Its minimal context is the sentence positioning the source’s proposal immediately after the authors state that a comprehensive gravitational interpretation of entanglement entropy had been lacking.

The object of the statement is a research proposal based on AdS/CFT duality for the problem of a gravitational interpretation of entanglement entropy. Its predicate is that a simple proposal is presented on the issue. Its explicit domain of application is limited to the problem defined in Section I, the static prescription presented by the source, and the tests conducted within the source.

Statement 4

Original (English):

In higher dimensions (d≥2d\geq 2), a quantitative comparison is not easy because the gravity description corresponds to the strongly coupled gauge theory whose entanglement entropy is not known at present.

The source location is p. 5, Section V of the same source. Its minimal context is the sentence stating the limit of the higher-dimensional comparison after noting that, for d=1d=1, the formula in the original source agrees exactly with the entropy calculated directly from the CFT.

The object of the statement is the availability of a quantitative comparison for holographic entanglement entropy in strongly coupled gauge theories with d≥2d\geq 2. Its predicate is that such a quantitative comparison is not easy because the entanglement entropy of the corresponding strongly coupled gauge theory was unknown at the time of publication. Its explicit domain of application is the results known at publication for higher-dimensional strongly coupled gauge theories, distinguished from the quantitative comparison performed for d=1d=1.

Individual audit

Proposal for a holographic derivation

Statement S49-Q001 proposes a holographic derivation of quantum-field-theory entanglement entropy through the AdS/CFT correspondence. Its independent grounds are the analogy with the Bekenstein–Hawking area formula, area calculations for a static minimal surface anchored on the boundary, and comparisons with known CFT results. The source treats static AdS/CFT settings: it demonstrates agreement with two-dimensional CFT formulas and compares higher-dimensional gravitational calculations with the functional forms and coefficients of the free theory. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 7, 8, 13–18, 32, 33 and 40 in Table 458 and Results 2, 3, 9 and 13–15 in Table 461.

A simple proposal for the gravitational interpretation

Statement S49-Q002 concerns entanglement entropy, for which a comprehensive gravitational interpretation had been lacking. The proposal is a single prescription that uses AdS/CFT duality to normalize the area of the static bulk minimal surface corresponding to a boundary subsystem by the Newton constant. Its grounds within the source are the definitions of the reduced state and entropy, the boundary-anchoring condition, and calculated agreement with known CFT formulas. The specified dimensions, geometries and states determine the scope of these comparisons. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 8, 9 and 13 and Results 1–3 in Table 461.

Area law from a static minimal surface

Statement S49-Q003 proposes SA=Area⁡(γA)/(4⁢GN(d+2))S_{A}=\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_{A})/(4G_{N}^{(d+2)}) for a dd-dimensional static minimal surface in A⁢d⁢Sd+2AdS_{d+2} satisfying ∂γA=∂A\partial\gamma_{A}=\partial A. Its independent grounds are the area calculation for a surface anchored on the specified boundary and normalization by the Newton constant. The source’s A⁢d⁢S3/C⁢F⁢T2AdS_{3}/CFT_{2} calculations agree with known entropy formulas under the respective conditions of the global single-interval, finite-temperature Euclidean-BTZ and Poincaré branches. Each surface belongs to the fixed slice specified in its branch. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 13–18 and 20–27, Results 2, 3 and 13–15 in Table 461, and the fixed-slice conditions in Definition S.482.

Limits of quantitative comparison in higher dimensions

Statement S49-Q004 says that quantitative comparison at d≥2d\geq 2 is difficult because the entanglement entropy of the strongly coupled gauge theory used for comparison was then unknown. Its independent grounds are agreement with the known CFT formula at d=1d=1 and the comparisons between the strongly coupled gravitational calculation and free theory at d≥2d\geq 2. The source’s 𝒩=4\mathcal{N}=4 comparison gives agreement in functional form and an estimated finite-term coefficient ratio (free theory/gravitational calculation) of about 3/23/2, assuming that the gauge field contributes as two real scalars. The corresponding formal evidence comprises Claims 32, 33 and 40 and Result 9 in Table 461.
