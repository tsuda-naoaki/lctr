Definition S.379 (Formal Objects and Explicit Assumptions of Adler 2003).

The source assumes an initial product state of system, apparatus, and environment and deterministic unitary evolution generated by a self-adjoint Hamiltonian. Evidential Force

Definition S.388 (Evidential Scope and Boundaries of Adler 2003).

The scope comprises apparatus–environment overlap decay and suppression of system interference, the logical constraint on a common initial state and unitary action, recording and later readout, additional stochastic differential dynamics, and the causal region R=c⁢TR=cT. Adler assesses the stochastic approach as a viable phenomenological solution while allowing its possible falsification by future experiments.

Even when the apparatus–environment overlap decays, the overlap of two vectors obtained from the same normalized state by the same unitary action is 1. Distinct orthogonal single outcomes require changing these antecedents or adding structure. Evolution time, measurement duration, light speed, decay rate, the ordering of later readout, stochastic differentials, and the Hamiltonian’s temporal scale are temporal inputs preceding the source’s calculations and constructions.

Result-Preservation Domain

Definition S.389 (Conditions for Preserving the Results of Adler 2003).

Recording and later readout use the measurement duration, apparatus configuration, and readout ordering; the stochastic alternative uses the linear and nonlinear stochastic terms and d⁢Wt2=d⁢tdW_{t}^{2}=dt; the causal region uses cc and TT. If an LCTR generation sequence supplies the same order, scale, and correspondence for each temporal input, and these assumptions and construction rules are retained, the corresponding conditional results are preserved.

Placement of grounding responsibilities

Placement of grounding responsibilities

The nine time contents audited in this source are not generated from nontemporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already established temporal inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding responsibility is ”backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment is based on comparing, for each time content, its use in a law operation or result derivation against the absence, before that use, of a corresponding generation condition or condition of validity stated as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition.

Evidence of actual failure

Evidence of actual failure

The complete-generation judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern qualification as physical time and are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing the value or domain of application of a result. Neither the source’s formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence establishes actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is ”indeterminate from the original source evidence”; actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding responsibility alone.

Downstream reach

Downstream reach

The scope of reach to results dependent on primitive time roots is determined source by source by the primitive-time-root/result dependency relation reconstructed in this division and by minimal primitive-time-root support. This is structural reach. Because no actual failure of a concrete condition has been established, the judgment of actual reach from a failure location to results is ”indeterminate from the original source evidence.” Structural reach does not substitute for actual reach.

Repair scope

Repair scope

On the basis of structural reach, the repair targets are the corresponding time contents and the generation conditions or conditions of validity required before their use. The recalculation scope is restricted to results reached by dependency from a failure location whose actual failure has been established independently. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this source, this repair and recalculation specification is conditional. If the condition is met, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from repaired inputs, or their domain of validity is restricted. Values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual Statements

Temporal decay of the apparatus–environment branch overlap

The statement concerns decay of the apparatus–environment branch overlap in the unitary measurement model. Original (English):

the rapid decay with time of the inner product

The source location is p. 7, the paragraph introducing Equation (4) of arXiv:quant-ph/0112095v3.

The minimal context is as follows. The overlap in Equation (4) is between apparatus–environment branch states and decays from its initial value 1. Equations (5a) and (5b) factorize it under the scattering approximations; p. 8 describes its asymptotic exp⁡(−Λ⁢t)\exp(-\Lambda t) decay using the time dependence of the factor count. The attributed author is Stephen L. Adler. The predicate is “the rapid decay with time”; the explicit domain is pages 6–8, Equations (3)–(5b), the initial state, unitary evolution, scattering approximations, and time-dependent factor count.

Qualification of decoherence as a solution

The statement concerns qualification of decoherence as a solution to the quantum measurement problem. Original (English):

decoherence has not solved the quantum measurement problem.

The source location is p. 2, the abstract of arXiv:quant-ph/0112095v3.

The minimal context is as follows. The source distinguishes suppression of interference through decay of the apparatus–environment overlap from selection of a single outcome. The argument on p. 8 uses the fact that two states obtained from the same normalized initial state by the same unitary action have overlap 1, which is incompatible with the orthogonality of Equations (6a) and (6b). Page 9 explains that only enough repeated trials to obtain each distinct outcome at least once are needed. The attributed author is Stephen L. Adler. The predicate is “has not solved the quantum measurement problem”; the explicit domain is the abstract on p. 2, pp. 8–9, Equations (6a) and (6b), and the requirements of a common normalized state, a common unitary action, and orthogonal outcomes.

Status as a phenomenological solution

The statement concerns the status of decoherence as a phenomenological solution to the measurement problem. Original (English):

Decoherence, in the absence of a detailed theory showing that it leads to stochastic outcomes with the correct properties, has yet to achieve this status.

The source location is p. 11, the concluding paragraph of arXiv:quant-ph/0112095v3.

The minimal context is as follows. The preceding sentence states that the stochastic approach constitutes a viable phenomenological solution to the measurement problem, although it may ultimately be falsified experimentally. The subject of the quoted sentence is decoherence. The absence of a detailed theory leading to stochastic outcomes with the correct properties is the condition under which it has yet to attain that status. The attributed author is Stephen L. Adler. The predicate is “has yet to achieve this status”; the explicit domain is the stochastic differential evolution on pp. 10–11 and the assessment of decoherence following the phenomenological assessment of the stochastic approach on p. 11.

Individual Audits

Temporal decay of the apparatus–environment branch overlap

Statement S40-Q001 concerns the apparatus–environment branch overlap. Its independent basis is the scattering model in Equations (5a) and (5b) and the asymptotic calculation on p. 8, supporting overlap decay and interference suppression. The calculation uses the initial state, unitary evolution, scattering approximations, evolution time tt, and rate Λ\Lambda. The decaying overlap and the overlap of the total states, including the system, in Equations (6a) and (6b) have different objects. The corresponding formal evidence is Results 1 and 2 in Table 371 and Definition S.388.

Qualification of decoherence as a solution

The independent basis of Statement S40-Q002 is the argument that, for |A⟩=U⁢|0⟩|A\rangle=U|0\rangle and |B⟩=U⁢|0⟩|B\rangle=U|0\rangle obtained from the same normalized state |0⟩|0\rangle by the same unitary action UU, one has ⟨A|B⟩=⟨0|U†⁢U|0⟩=1\langle A|B\rangle=\langle 0|U^{\dagger}U|0\rangle=1. This is incompatible with orthogonality of the two outcomes. Adding decay of the apparatus–environment branch overlap leaves this implication unchanged. Its domain is the common-state, common-action antecedent, corresponding to the simplified measurement problem identified in footnote 1 on p. 9. The corresponding formal evidence is Results 3 and 4.

Status as a phenomenological solution

Statement S40-Q003 compares the status attained by decoherence with the status of a phenomenological solution just accorded to the stochastic approach. The source grounds that assessment of the stochastic approach in discrete outcomes with Born-rule probabilities, maintenance of coherence under conditions where it is observed, and state reduction under measurement-apparatus conditions. It attributes detailed analyses of these properties to the cited literature; the paragraph supplies the stochastic differential construction and phenomenological assessment. Results 3 and 4 correspond to the nonattainment judgment for decoherence, while Results 5 and 7 correspond to the construction and assessment of the stochastic alternative.

Common Audit with Specified Domain of Application

The three statements connect three objects: decay of the apparatus–environment overlap, orthogonal single outcomes of the total state, and additional stochastic dynamics. The decoherence calculation evaluates a time-dependent overlap. The single-outcome incompatibility is a formal consequence of the common-state, common-action antecedent. The stochastic alternative uses time and stochastic differentials. In Table 372, Results 3 and 4 have no temporal prerequisites, while the other six results depend on primitive time roots.

Boundary of Evidential Force

The source’s negative logical judgment assumes a common initial state and common deterministic action. Its assessment of the stochastic approach carries the stated phenomenological properties and possibility of falsification. The generation judgment for the nine time contents and the four-class partition of the eight results are given by Proposition S.390.
