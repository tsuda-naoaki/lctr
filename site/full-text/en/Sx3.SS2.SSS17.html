Assumptions

The reference assumptions are the low-velocity approximation, weak gravitational field, the selected nonrotating coordinate system, the rotating Earth, the clock rate of a reference clock on Earth’s surface, route data for latitude, altitude, and ground speed, the resolution boundary for higher-order relativistic terms, the smallness of external-field residuals, and coordinate invariance of the total display difference at the reunion event.

The components with positive grounding grounds as non-temporal physical inputs are the portable cesium-clock apparatus, aircraft, Earth’s spatial radius, route geometry, geographical position, altitude, joining of the departure and reunion events, and identity of the check points. Result-Preservation Domain

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Placement of the Grounding Obligation

Time Contents 1–77, which belong to law-time uses, are not generated from non-temporal physical inputs before use; they are accepted as already-established time inputs. The placement judgment for the physical-time grounding obligation is “backgrounded by primitive time input.” This judgment follows by matching the use of these time contents in law operations or result derivations against the absence, before that use, of the corresponding generation or validity condition as an input, formation condition, evaluation condition, or failure condition. Time Content 78 is the temporal order from the preliminary report to the present report; it is not a premise of a law operation or physical-result derivation.

Evidence of Actual Failure

Evidence of Actual Failure

The complete judgment and the remaining-root judgment concern physical-time qualification; they are distinct from actual failure of a concrete condition governing a result’s value or domain of application. Neither the original formal evidence nor the LCTR audit evidence in this reference establishes an actual failure of such a target condition. The actual-failure judgment is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence,” and actual failure is not inferred from the backgrounding of a grounding obligation alone.

For this reference, the reach to primitive-time-root-dependent results is determined by the primitive-time-root–result dependence relations and minimal primitive-time-root supports reconstructed in this division. Because actual failure of a concrete condition has not been established, the actual-reach judgment from that failure location to a result is “indeterminate from the original-source evidence.” Structural reach does not replace actual reach.

Repair Scope

On the basis of structural reach, the objects of repair are the corresponding time contents and the generation or validity conditions required before their use. The scope of recalculation is limited to results for which actual failure is independently established and which are dependency-reachable from that failure location. Because actual failure is indeterminate in this reference, this repair-and-recalculation specification is conditional. If that condition is satisfied, the target results are regenerated or recalculated from the repaired inputs, or their validity domain is restricted. The values after recalculation are determined as audit results.

Individual Statements

Objects of the Statements

Detectability of the predicted effect with portable cesium clocks; evidential force of the east–west directional asymmetry; the physical relation between inertial systems and the universe; and observability and invariance of the difference between the readings of two clocks at the same place.

Verbatim Original (English)

The theory predicts a detectable effect with cesium beam clocks if they are flown around the world at typical jet aircraft speeds (4).

Furthermore, the mere existence of a definite east-west directional asymmetry in the observed time differences would give strong evidence for the validity of the kinetic term in Eq. 2.

Although inertial systems are highly specialized, they have an objective physical relationship with the universe because they have no acceleration or rotation relative to the universe.

The difference in the times indicated by two clocks located at the same place is a physically observable quantity that is invariant.

Original-Source Locations

The first quotation is from the introduction of Hafele–Keating, Around-the-World Atomic Clocks: Predicted Relativistic Time Gains, Science 177, p. 166. The second is from the paragraph following Figure 1 on p. 167; the third and fourth are from note 6 on the same page.

Minimal Context

The first quotation belongs to the introduction that separates the proposed global-flight test using portable atomic clocks into a prediction report and a subsequent observation report. Following the detection threshold shown in Figure 1, the second describes east–west directional asymmetry as evidence supporting the validity of the kinetic term. The third and fourth belong to a note explaining description by an inertial reference system and the frame independence of the difference between the readings of two reunited clocks.

Attribution

All four statements are attributable to the main text or a note by J. C. Hafele and Richard E. Keating.

Contents Directly Stated by the Quotations

The first states that the predicted effect is detectable with portable cesium clocks. The second states that an east–west directional asymmetry in the observed time differences would constitute strong evidence for the validity of the kinetic term. The third states that inertial systems have an objective physical relationship with the universe. The fourth describes the difference between the readings of two clocks at the same place as an invariant physically observable quantity.

Explicit Domain of Application

The first and second quotations concern portable cesium clocks, typical jet-aircraft speeds, global flight, a detection threshold, and east–west directional comparison. The third concerns inertial systems that are neither accelerated nor rotating relative to the universe; the fourth concerns two clocks compared at the same place.

Evidence Provenance

The quotations are from the published article in Science 177, pp. 166–168.

Individual Audit

Interpretive Possibilities

The first statement is a conditional description predicting a detectable reading difference in cesium clocks flown around the world at typical jet-aircraft speeds. The second is a conditional evaluation of evidence that treats east–west directional asymmetry as evidence supporting the validity of the kinetic term. The third is a theoretical explanation that attributes an objective physical relationship to inertial systems that are neither accelerated nor rotating relative to the universe. The fourth describes the reading difference between two clocks compared at the same place as an invariant physically observable quantity.

Independent Grounds

The four statements theoretically describe a detectable effect in globally flown cesium clocks, east–west directional asymmetry, the relation between inertial systems and the universe, and invariance of the reading difference between reunited clocks. This reference is a prediction report; the quoted passages contain no direct measurement result obtained from clock-display differences after global flight.

Evidence-Effect Boundary

The first statement predicts detectability. The second states the evidential force supporting the kinetic term if the specified east–west directional asymmetry is observed. The third supports the authors’ explanation of the relation between inertial systems and the universe, but supplies no result independently measuring that relation. The invariance in the fourth statement concerns the reading difference between two clocks reunited at the same place; it supports neither invariance nor dilation of a time attribute independent of physical carriers.

Within-Reference Comparison

The first statement predicts detectability, and the second conditionally states the evidential force of an observed east–west directional asymmetry. The third describes the relation between inertial systems and the universe; the fourth describes the reading difference between reunited clocks as an invariant observable quantity. The prediction, conditional evaluation of evidence, reference-system explanation, and clock-comparison invariant are mutually related, but they constitute neither a measurement result nor measurement of an attribute of a time entity.
