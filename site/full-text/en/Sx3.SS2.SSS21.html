Individual Statements

Objects of the Statements

The relation between the known laws of physics and a classical spacetime background; the use of Fourier components with respect to time; the unit convention G=c=ℏ=k=1G=c=\hbar=k=1; and Hawking’s statement that a surface near the initial big-bang singularity could be regarded as a hidden surface. The Planck time is given as an example of where to place that surface.

Verbatim Original (English)

all the known laws of physics … are all formulated on a classical space-time background

using Fourier components with respect to time

Throughout the paper units will be used in which G=c=ℏ=k=1G=c=\hbar=k=1.

One could regard a surface very close to the initial big-bang singularity (say, at the Planck time 10−43⁢sec10^{-43}\,\text{sec}) as being a “hidden surface” in the sense that we have no a priori information about it.

The third statement gives the unit convention G=c=ℏ=k=1G=c=\hbar=k=1. The fourth gives the Planck time 10−43⁢sec10^{-43}\,\text{sec} as an example of where a surface could be treated as hidden.

Original-Source Locations

The first quotation appears in the abstract of Hawking, Breakdown of Predictability in Gravitational Collapse, Physical Review D 14, p. 2460. The second appears in the introduction of the Fourier components on p. 2466, §2. The third appears in the unit convention at the beginning of §2 on p. 2464. The fourth appears in the application to the early universe at the end of §1 on p. 2463.

Minimal Context

The first quotation belongs to the abstract’s statement that the concepts of classical spacetime and the known laws break down at a singularity. The second belongs to the use of Fourier components with respect to time to calculate coefficients under an approximation in which the black-hole mass changes slowly. The third is the paper-wide unit convention following the formal setting of a massless Hermitian scalar field and an uncharged, nonrotating black hole. The fourth belongs to the discussion of the possibility that the principle of ignorance accounts for the homogeneity, isotropy, and thermal equilibrium of the early universe.

Attributed Author

The four quotations are attributable to S. W. Hawking’s exposition in the article.

Content Stated Directly by the Quotations

The first quotation states that all the known laws are formulated on a classical spacetime background; the second states the use of Fourier components with respect to time. The third states the unit convention in which four constants equal one. In the fourth quotation, Hawking states that a surface near the initial singularity could be regarded as hidden in the sense that we have no a priori information about it, and gives the Planck time as an example of when to place that surface.

Explicit Domain of Application

The first quotation is limited to the known laws and the classical spacetime background discussed by the source; the second, to the mode decomposition under the approximation that the black-hole mass changes slowly; the third, to the unit notation used throughout the source; and the fourth, to the discussion that applies the principle of ignorance to the early universe.

Individual Audit

First Statement

The first statement expresses the dependence of the known laws of physics on a classical spacetime background. Hawking argues that the classical spacetime concept and the known laws formulated on it break down at a singularity. In Section I, Hawking relates this claim to the singularity conditions and argues for a predictive limitation additional to ordinary quantum uncertainty. The formal correspondence is to Claims 11 and 24, Time Contents 1–3, 5–6, 8–9, 12, and Result 1.

Second Statement

The second statement gives the procedure for calculating Bogoliubov coefficients using Fourier components with respect to time under the slow-mass-variation approximation. In Sections II and III, Hawking obtains the outgoing particle number using the incoming vacuum, positive frequency relative to asymptotic time, backward-propagated modes and finitely normalized packets. The formal correspondence is to Claims 25, 27, 29 and 30, Time Contents 33, 36, 38, 43–44, 47, 51–52, and Results 6 and 12.

Third Statement

The third statement is the unit convention G=c=ℏ=k=1G=c=\hbar=k=1 used throughout the source. Numerical representations of mass, length, time and temperature are converted according to this convention. The formal correspondence is to Claim 25 and Auxiliary Object 68; Claims 31 and 39 use the relations among mass, surface gravity and temperature.

Fourth Statement

In the fourth statement, Hawking proposes heuristically treating a surface near the initial big-bang singularity as hidden, giving the Planck time as an example of when to place that surface. Using the principle of ignorance and nearly zero total energy, Hawking further argues that equal-weight counting of microscopic configurations on a later hypersurface makes a thermal, homogeneous and isotropic macrostate the most probable. The formal correspondence is to Claims 19, 53 and 54, Time Contents 23–25, 30–31, and Result 30.

Common Audit with Specified Domain of Application

Evolution on classical spacetime, frequency decomposition relative to asymptotic time, temporal quantities expressed through unit conversion, and early and later cosmological surfaces enter the respective arguments under their specified conditions.
